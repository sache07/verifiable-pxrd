# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is modeled as two crystalline precursor phases:

| Phase | Structural model | Crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| Monoclinic WO3 | P 21/c (No. 14) | 0.75397 (75.40 wt%) |
| Orthorhombic Mn2O3 | Pbca (No. 61) | 0.24603 (24.60 wt%) |

Fractions are normalized over the modeled crystalline material. They do not quantify amorphous material. A realistic uncertainty is about 0.02–0.03 in absolute fraction because scale, surface roughness, polarization, and microstructural broadening are correlated; sub-percent precision is not supported.

## Evidence

The diagnostic monoclinic-WO3 group at approximately 23.43, 23.91, and 24.68 degrees 2theta, together with the groups near 33.6–34.5 and 50.2 degrees, accounts for the dominant pattern. The remaining distinct reflections, including those near 33.27, 38.54, 49.63, 55.47, and 66.03 degrees, are accounted for by orthorhombic alpha-Mn2O3. The refined fraction ratio is also close to that expected for an unreacted 1:1 Mn:W precursor charge. Thus the 500 degrees C specimen is best interpreted as largely unreacted WO3 and Mn2O3, with no reliably detected crystalline tungstate product.

Candidate comparisons favored the monoclinic WO3 framework (standardized from COD 2311041) over orthorhombic and triclinic WO3 alternatives, and Pbca Mn2O3 (COD 1514103) over the tested cubic Mn2O3 models. A trial MnWO4 component refined below about 1 wt%, required boundary-limited broadening and an implausibly large cell change relative to the available reference, and did not have stable phase-specific evidence. It is therefore not reported as a determined phase.

The submitted atomic coordinates were kept within the small reference-mismatch range supported by the data and pass the geometric/occupancy checks. Heavy-atom frameworks and unit cells are well supported; individual oxygen coordinates are substantially less precise and should not be interpreted as a high-precision structure determination.

## Refinement and fit

The GSAS-II Rietveld model used the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, a Bragg-Brentano specimen model, a smooth positive background, specimen displacement and roughness, isotropic crystallite size, and isotropic/uniaxial microstrain as supported by the data. Fresh atomic replay of `final.gpx` over all 8301 observations gives Rwp = 6.073%, Rp = 4.404%, chi-square = 43185.69, and goodness of fit = 2.286. The replay produced no physical-profile review flags.

Minor crystalline material below roughly 1 wt%, especially if strongly broadened or overlapping, cannot be excluded. The data do not constrain amorphous content.
