# Powder XRD phase analysis

The crystalline model contains **wolframite NiWO4, 76.16 wt%**, and **monoclinic WO3, 23.84 wt%**, normalized over these modeled crystalline phases. No amorphous fraction can be obtained from this scan without an internal standard.

## Evidence

The NiO + WO3 precursor context and 900 °C firing make NiWO4 the expected reaction product. The wolframite model accounts for the independent peak series at approximately 19.21, 23.91, 24.85, 30.87, 36.52, 54.57 and 65–66 degrees 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 is required by the separate 23.06/23.53/24.29 degree triplet and matching lines near 28.84, 33.19, 33.52 and 34.09 degrees. Related P21/n, P-1 and orthorhombic WO3 models were screened; the retained P21/c model (COD 2311041 parent setting) gave the best stable whole-pattern fit.

The NiWO4 framework was adapted from available wolframite analogues and cross-checked against published COD 5910277. That Ni record has a duplicated/incomplete atom loop and cannot be imported directly, but its Ni and W coordinates agree closely with the refined heavy-atom sites. The refined cells are NiWO4 a=4.60439, b=5.66785, c=4.91445 A, beta=89.9883 degrees; and WO3 a=7.69573, b=7.53987, c=10.53767 A, beta=136.0886 degrees (the retained nonstandard P21/c setting).

The final fresh atomic replay gives Rwp=4.237%, Rp=3.272%, Rexp=3.479% and goodness-of-fit=1.218; chi-square per point is 1.483 over 8282 points. A nonnegative NiO addition refined to only 0.014 wt% and did not materially change Rwp; its required strong line near 43.4 degrees is absent, so NiO is not included. There are no other coherent unmodeled peak series.

## Remaining uncertainty

The fractions are estimated to about +/-2 wt% absolute because phase scale, preferred orientation, displacement, line broadening and background are correlated. Trace crystalline phases below roughly 0.5–1 wt% cannot be excluded. Heavy-atom positions and cells are robust, but oxygen coordinates are less well constrained by Cu X-ray powder data; the submitted oxygen sublattices remain chemically plausible and should not be interpreted at single-crystal precision. Closely related very small WO3 symmetry distortions are not uniquely distinguishable at this resolution.
