# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is modeled as a two-phase, largely unreacted precursor mixture:

| Phase | Refined structure | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| WO₃ | monoclinic *P*2₁/*c*, a = 7.69773 Å, b = 7.54628 Å, c = 10.5527 Å, β = 136.163° | 0.7420 |
| Mn₂O₃ | cubic *Ia*-3, a = 9.42114 Å | 0.2580 |

The monoclinic WO₃ model accounts for the diagnostic 23.43/23.90/24.68° triplet and the dense higher-angle reflection set. Cubic Mn₂O₃ accounts for distinct peaks near 33.27, 38.53, 49.60, 55.49, and 66.03° 2θ. Alternative WO₃ polymorphs, other Mn-oxide models, and MnWO₄ were tested; they gave poorer whole-pattern agreement. In particular, the expected strong MnWO₄ features around 30° are absent. No additional crystalline phase is required by the residual.

The native GSAS-II replay gives Rwp = 6.223% and Rp = 4.554% over all 8,301 points, using 1/max(observed,1) weights. The refined fractions are close to the 74.6:25.4 mass ratio expected for unreacted WO₃ and Mn₂O₃ dosed at a 1:1 Mn:W ratio, independently supporting the phase assignment.

The phase identities and approximate fractions are reliable, but the fractions carry an estimated systematic uncertainty of about ±0.03 absolute because surface roughness, displacement parameters, overlapping WO₃ intensities, and scale are correlated. The heavy-atom frameworks and unit cells are better determined than the oxygen coordinates; the latter should not be treated as a high-precision structure determination from these laboratory X-ray data. Amorphous material is not quantified, and a trace crystalline component below the pattern's detection limit cannot be excluded, so no structure or fraction is assigned to such material.
