# PXRD phase analysis

The crystalline pattern is modeled by two phases: monoclinic WO3 (70.79 wt%) and wolframite-type CoWO4 (29.21 wt%). Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases.

Evidence for WO3 is the characteristic 23.18/23.68/24.42 degrees 2theta triplet and its resolved higher-angle multiplets near 33-34, 41-42, 50, and 56 degrees. The complementary reflections at about 19.05, 30.71, 36.44, 54.14, and 61.86 degrees require the P2/c wolframite phase. A two-phase Le Bail check reached Rwp about 4.12%, showing that these cells account for the observed crystalline peak inventory; residuals did not require CoO or Co3O4.

The final atomic Rietveld model gives Rwp = 4.709%, Rp = 3.593%, and goodness of fit = 1.438, versus an expected counting-statistics Rwp minimum of 3.280%. The fit includes the reported Cu Kalpha doublet, refined broadening, specimen displacement, surface roughness, and weak preferred orientation. Native replay of `final.gpx` reproduced the complete-grid profile without physical-review flags.

The WO3 model is based on monoclinic COD 2311041 and was refined in its supplied nonstandard P21/c setting. Because the available CoWO4 database record was malformed, the CoWO4 model was constructed by substituting Co into the isostructural MgWO4 wolframite framework (COD 9013902), followed by cell and coordinate refinement. The phase identity and heavy-atom framework are reliable, but individual oxygen coordinates are less well constrained by this broad, overlapping laboratory pattern and should not be treated as a unique high-precision structure determination.

Scale/profile/texture sensitivity suggests roughly 1-2 wt% absolute systematic uncertainty in each normalized fraction; the strong correlation between the two surface-roughness parameters is not captured by a simple counting-statistical error. Any amorphous content or crystalline phase below the residual detection level cannot be quantified from this refinement, so absolute whole-sample fractions are not determined reliably.
