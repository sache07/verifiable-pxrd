# Powder XRD phase analysis

The modeled crystalline inventory is 72.85 wt% tetragonal scheelite SrWO4, 13.52 wt% monoclinic WO3, and 13.63 wt% monoclinic fergusonite-like SrWO4. Fractions are normalized only over these crystalline models; the scan does not determine amorphous content.

## Evidence

Scheelite SrWO4 (COD 1546036) accounts for the dominant coherent families at approximately 18.32, 28.00, 33.39, 45.45, 47.75, 51.98, 56.08, and 72.98 degrees 2theta and is a high-confidence identification. Monoclinic WO3 (COD 1528915) explains the characteristic split 22–25 degree group and recurring higher-angle lines that neither SrWO4 model supplies. A second, monoclinically split SrWO4 fingerprint is required by the 29.75–32.63 degree region and multiple repeated families through high angle. The exact-composition fergusonite record (COD 1546037) gave the best stable atomic-model fit among the SrWO4, CaWO4, CaMoO4, NdTaO4, and YNbO4 framework variants examined.

The common baseline uses one zero correction, a positive smooth eight-term Chebyshev background, nonnegative W/X peak-width terms, and phase-dependent isotropic coherent-size broadening. The final sizes (0.150, 0.073, and 0.101 micrometres) are conditional on fixed X because isotropic size and X are strongly correlated. Isotropic/anisotropic strain, surface roughness, free polarization, and free asymmetry were rejected when they became negative, bound-limited, highly correlated, or replay-unstable. March-Dollase [111] terms were retained for WO3 and the fergusonite-like phase after symmetry-distinct axis tests. Four successive save/reopen refinements gave Rwp = 13.031 +/- 0.001%. A fresh no-optimization replay of `final.gpx` gives Rwp 13.031%, Rp 9.246%, a positive 346–1996 count background, and no profile-validity flags.

## Remaining uncertainty

The scheelite assignment is robust. The WO3 phase identity is supported, but its published low-precision oxygen model contains a 1.58 A O–O contact; bounded coordinate refinements were singular or hit displacement limits, and alternate WO3 records fit less well. Its oxygen geometry is therefore provisional.

The minor monoclinic SrWO4 assignment is also provisional: the source structure was measured at high pressure, its refined cell is substantially contracted relative to that record, and the strong preferred-orientation correction may partly absorb framework error. It should be read as a fergusonite-like SrWO4 model, not proof that the high-pressure polymorph is thermodynamically stable after the reported furnace treatment. Candidate Sr3W2O9, SrCO3, Sr oxides, and reduced tungsten oxides did not survive the common-profile challenge without implausible cell changes, collapsed size, or unstable scale. No fourth phase can be quantified reliably from this pattern. The two minor fractions are consequently more model-dependent than the scheelite fraction.
