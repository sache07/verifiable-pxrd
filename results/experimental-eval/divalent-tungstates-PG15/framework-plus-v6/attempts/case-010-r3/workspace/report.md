# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is best described by unreacted monoclinic WO3 and bixbyite Mn2O3, plus a provisional trace of wolframite-type MnWO4. Fractions below are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases; no amorphous fraction was measured.

| Phase | Structural model | Refined cell | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| WO3 | P 21/c (No. 14) | a = 7.69970, b = 7.54331, c = 10.55474 A, beta = 136.1626 deg | 74.435 wt% |
| Mn2O3 | I a -3 (No. 206) | a = 9.42122 A | 24.641 wt% |
| MnWO4 | P 2/c (No. 13), provisional | a = 4.85001, b = 5.79188, c = 4.94393 A, beta = 90.9637 deg | 0.924 wt% |

The formal scale-covariance standard uncertainties are 0.241, 0.233, and 0.075 wt%, respectively. They do not include phase/profile-model uncertainty.

## Evidence and model selection

The dominant WO3 model accounts for the characteristic 23.43/23.90/24.68 deg cluster and the many split high-angle families. Among physically screened WO3 records, the P 21/c model (COD 2311041) gave the best stable baseline. A triclinic WO3 alternative approached a similar profile residual only after drifting away from its published lattice and driving roughness to a bound; other monoclinic records fit materially worse.

Mn2O3 is supported by coherent families near 33.27, 38.53, 49.60, 55.49, and 66.03 deg. The cubic I a -3 bixbyite model is the simplest model consistent with the absence of resolved orthorhombic splitting. A Pbca bixbyite record was only marginally worse, so the presence of Mn2O3 is reliable but its very small possible orthorhombic distortion is not resolved by this pattern.

Weak excess intensity at partly unique and partly overlapped wolframite reflections, including the families near 18.61, 30.19/30.57, and 36.24 deg, supports MnWO4. Its scale is positive at about twelve formal standard uncertainties. Mn3WO6 refined to approximately 0.01 wt% while its cell drifted strongly, and Mn3O4 required an approximately 15% cell-volume expansion; both were rejected. FeWO4-, ZnWO4-, and MgWO4-derived substituted prototypes were also inferior to the exact MnWO4 topology. The available exact MnWO4 record is from a high-pressure study, so it was used only as the wolframite topology and its cell was refined.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The final full-range GSAS-II refinement used the supplied Cu K-alpha doublet, an eight-term smooth Chebyshev background, one fitted zero correction, a nonnegative common U/W/X profile, and supported phase broadening. Texture trials collapsed to random and were removed. WO3 required isotropic size broadening plus [010] uniaxial microstrain; Mn2O3 retained isotropic microstrain only. Heavy-atom coordinates were tested and the accepted values were frozen for the final nuisance reconvergence. Oxygen-coordinate refinement produced implausible shifts and was rejected, so the detailed oxygen geometry is inherited from the reference structures and cannot be determined reliably from these broad laboratory data.

The final fit has Rwp = 6.1633%, Rp = 4.5439%, and goodness of fit = 2.3186 (counting-statistical expected Rwp = 2.6624%). Remaining residuals cluster around the strongest WO3 peaks and reflect profile/intensity mismatch rather than a coherent missing phase. The MnWO4 fraction is particularly model-dependent: reasonable restrained trace-profile trials span about 0.28-1.43 wt%; 0.924 wt% is the submitted common-profile value, not a high-precision assay.

The saved project was reopened and replayed over all 8301 points (9.9034-100.0949 deg) using freshly generated atomic structure factors. Replay reproduced the submitted fractions, reported no negative-width/background/ADP flags, and all three exported CIFs passed the geometry/occupancy contact screen.
