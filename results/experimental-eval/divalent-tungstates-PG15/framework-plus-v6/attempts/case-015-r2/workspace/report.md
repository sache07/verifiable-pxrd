# Powder XRD phase analysis

The crystalline inventory is **ZnWO4 (wolframite), monoclinic WO3, and wurtzite ZnO**. Quantitative results, normalized over these modeled crystalline phases, are:

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| ZnWO4 | P 2/c | a = 4.69190, b = 5.71976, c = 4.92723 Å; beta = 90.6365° | 0.74010 |
| WO3 | P 21/c | a = 7.30551, b = 7.53821, c = 10.53431 Å; beta = 133.1004° | 0.19175 |
| ZnO | P 63mc | a = 3.25102, c = 5.20919 Å | 0.06815 |

## Evidence

The dominant split families at 30.57/30.83/31.37° and 36.41/36.56° match wolframite ZnWO4. The distinct WO3 group at 23.23, 23.71 and 24.47°, together with its 33.4–34.3° family, cannot be represented by ZnWO4. Wurtzite ZnO is independently supported by the coherent 31.88, 34.53, 36.36, 47.63, 56.68, 62.93, 68.02 and 69.16° families. Removing either minor phase leaves its unique families in the residual and drives its scale back positive when reintroduced.

Published room-temperature parents were standardized before refinement: COD 1520641 (ZnWO4), COD 2311041 (WO3), and COD 9004178 (ZnO). Two physical monoclinic WO3 records were compared under the same background/profile model. The neutron-derived COD 2311041 parent gave Rwp 5.41% before coordinate refinement, versus 5.70% for COD 1528915, and retained sensible octahedral geometry; it was therefore selected. Orthorhombic and triclinic alternatives were inferior or relaxed toward the monoclinic metric.

The final model uses one Bragg–Brentano specimen-displacement correction, a positive common W/X profile, a smooth ten-term Chebyshev background, [100] uniaxial microstrain for WO3, isotropic size broadening for ZnO, and a small [001] March–Dollase correction for ZnWO4. Unsupported major-phase size/strain, minor-phase texture, extra asymmetry, and minor-phase oxygen-coordinate terms were pruned. Reopening and replaying the full range converged with maximum shift/esd 0.012. Independent native GSAS-II replay reported no negative-background, negative-width, or negative-ADP flags. The final fit is Rwp = 5.2578%, Rp = 4.0445%, and goodness of fit = 1.389.

The exported structures pass broad geometry checks: ZnWO4 has six-coordinate W–O = 1.797–2.153 Å and Zn–O = 1.982–2.208 Å; WO3 has six-coordinate W–O = 1.713–2.156 Å; ZnO has tetrahedral Zn–O = 1.975–1.990 Å.

## Remaining uncertainty

Phase identities are well determined, but the fractions carry model-dependent systematic uncertainty of roughly 1–2 absolute wt% (larger than covariance-only errors), mainly from roughness, polarization, overlap, and the WO3 profile. The detailed oxygen coordinates of the 19 wt% WO3 phase and the internal coordinate of the 7 wt% ZnO phase could not be refined reliably without excessive or unstable shifts, so defensible parent values were retained; those fine structural details should not be treated as independently determined by this pattern. Residual extrema lie chiefly on flanks of already indexed peaks rather than forming a coherent additional reflection family. Any amorphous material is invisible to this normalization and was not quantified.
