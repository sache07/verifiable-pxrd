# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is best described by three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over these phases only.

| Phase | Structure | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| PbWO4 | scheelite/stolzite, *I* 41/*a* | 72.42(45) wt% |
| PbO | massicot, *Pbcm* | 16.04(36) wt% |
| WO3 | gamma-monoclinic, *P* 21/*n* (No. 14) | 11.54(37) wt% |

## Evidence

PbWO4 accounts for the dominant series at 27.70, 29.91, 33.05, 45.02, 47.28, 51.48, 55.54, 56.82, and 72.27 degrees 2theta. Orthorhombic PbO is required by the independent series including 29.34, 30.61, 38.12, 49.50, 53.38, 60.53, and 63.28 degrees. Monoclinic WO3 is identified by its characteristic 23.38/23.88/24.64-degree triplet and supporting peaks at 26.78, 33.52, 34.44, 41.92, 50.18, and 61.72 degrees. These assignments are chemically consistent with incomplete reaction of the PbO and WO3 precursors at 500 degrees C.

Model-selection refinements support all three phases: under the same constrained profile model, Rwp was 14.68% for the selected model, 15.98% when WO3 was omitted, and 17.17% when PbO was omitted. Substituting tetragonal litharge for massicot drove its scale essentially to zero. A vacancy-ordered Pb7W8O28.8 model gave a worse Rwp of 15.31% and introduced unsupported superstructure reflections, so ordinary PbWO4 is preferred.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The final GSAS-II Rietveld refinement used the full 10.009-99.994-degree range, the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, an 18-term Chebyshev background, a refined common zero offset, axial-divergence and Gaussian profile terms, refined unit cells and phase scales, and isotropic crystallite-size broadening. Literature atomic coordinates from COD 9009811, 2310433, and 1528915 were retained because this overlapping multiphase laboratory pattern does not determine independent atomic coordinates reliably. The final statistics are Rwp = 14.680%, Rp = 10.206%, Rexp = 3.502%, and goodness of fit = 4.192.

The parenthetical fraction uncertainties are covariance-based statistical e.s.d.s and are lower bounds. Pointwise uncertainties and a calibrated instrument profile were not supplied, while preferred orientation, microabsorption, and amorphous content cannot be quantified from this scan alone. Practical fraction uncertainty is therefore a few absolute wt% (approximately 2-3 wt% for PbWO4/PbO and 1-2 wt% for WO3). Residuals are dominated by imperfect broad-peak line shapes and relative intensities; no coherent unmatched peak family justifies a fourth crystalline phase. Any amorphous fraction, if present, remains undetermined.
