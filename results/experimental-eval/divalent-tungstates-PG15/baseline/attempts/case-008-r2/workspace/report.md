# Powder diffraction phase analysis

## Result

The modeled crystalline material contains two phases:

| Phase | Structure | Refined cell (Å, °) | Crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| MnWO4 | wolframite, monoclinic P 2/c | a = 4.83470(8), b = 5.76112(8), c = 4.99998(8), beta = 91.2178(10) | 97.878(70)% |
| WO3 | gamma-WO3, monoclinic P 21/n | a = 7.3062(15), b = 7.5469(22), c = 7.6880(17), beta = 90.830(29) | 2.122(70)% |

Fractions are normalized over the modeled crystalline phases. The quoted parentheses are GSAS-II least-squares standard uncertainties; model-dependent uncertainty is larger (see below).

The final fit statistics over 10.001-100.008 degrees 2theta are Rp = 4.938%, Rwp = 7.063%, GOF = 2.565, and chi-square = 54257.99 for 8284 observations and 37 varied parameters.

## Evidence and model

The dominant reflections at 15.71, 18.68, 23.89/24.36, 30.12/30.58, 31.36, and 36.27 degrees, together with the higher-angle line set, index consistently as wolframite MnWO4. This is also the chemically expected reaction product from MnO and WO3 at 900 degrees C. A P 2/c MnWO4 reference model (COD 1520200) was used to start the refinement; its cell and symmetry-allowed coordinates were refined.

A MnWO4-only model gave Rwp = 7.43%. Weak satellites near 23.45 and 24.68 degrees and supporting weak lines are explained by room-temperature monoclinic WO3. Adding P 21/n WO3 lowered Rwp to 7.06% and gave a positive, well-determined scale. Among tested WO3 models, P 21/n fitted best; triclinic, orthorhombic, tetragonal, and hexagonal alternatives fitted less well or refined to negligible scale. The WO3 cell was refined, while its atomic coordinates were fixed to the reference model (COD 1528915) because a 2.1 wt% phase does not constrain them reliably.

The refinement used the supplied Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and fixed 0.5 intensity ratio, Poisson counting weights because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied, a 15-term Chebyshev background, and refined zero, effective polarization, and physically positive effective W/X profile terms. Atomic displacement parameters were not refined. No preferred-orientation correction was retained.

## Remaining uncertainty

The formal fraction uncertainty is about 0.07 wt%, but it does not include profile, background, preferred-orientation, or microabsorption systematics. The uncalibrated instrument profile and serially correlated residuals (Durbin-Watson = 0.371) indicate that a practical uncertainty of a few tenths of a weight percent is more realistic. The two W-rich phases have similar absorption, which limits the expected microabsorption bias.

Trial additions of MnO, Mn2O3, Mn3O4, and Mn3WO6 did not provide a unique third-phase assignment. Several unrelated manganese oxides could each absorb the same small systematic residual and return roughly 0.3-0.4 wt%, while Mn3WO6 refined to zero. Consequently, no additional crystalline phase or sub-0.5 wt% fraction can be determined reliably from this pattern. Amorphous content, if present, is not quantified because the reported fractions are normalized only over modeled crystalline phases.
