# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The modeled crystalline product is single-phase manganese tungstate, MnWO4, in the monoclinic wolframite structure (space group P 2/c, No. 13). Its weight fraction is **1.000 (100.0 wt%) when normalized over the modeled crystalline phases**. This normalization does not constrain an amorphous fraction or material below the reliable diffraction detection limit.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (Å, °) | Normalized crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| MnWO4 | P 2/c | a = 4.83044, b = 5.75668, c = 4.99593, β = 91.2174 | 1.000 |

The final Rietveld fit has Rwp = 5.068%, Rp = 3.813%, Rexp = 3.461%, and goodness of fit = 1.467. The phase residuals are RF = 1.855% and RF² = 3.664%.

## Evidence

The observed reflections—including the diagnostic low-angle peaks near 15.66° and 18.64° 2θ and the split groups near 24° and 30°—are indexed by the P 2/c wolframite cell. A wolframite MnWO4 reference topology was refined against all 8,301 points using the supplied Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and intensity ratio. The cell, atomic coordinates, phase scale, specimen displacement, surface roughness, background, and instrumental profile terms were refined; displacement parameters were retained from the starting structural model because freeing them produced unstable, nonphysical values in this laboratory pattern.

Mn3O4, Mn3WO6, and several ambient WO3 structure models were tested as additional phases. Mn3O4 refined to 0.16(18) wt% and Mn3WO6 to zero. WO3 gave model-dependent apparent values of roughly 0.3–0.7 wt%, with most leverage coming from overlap with MnWO4 rather than a coherent set of independent WO3 reflections. Therefore none is included as a supported crystalline phase. A sub-percent residual precursor, especially WO3, cannot be ruled out or quantified reliably from these data.

The refined profile coefficients are correlated (notably U and V), so their numerical values and the very small formal cell uncertainties should not be interpreted as an instrument calibration. This does not materially affect the phase assignment, but it limits claims about microstructure and trace-phase abundance.
