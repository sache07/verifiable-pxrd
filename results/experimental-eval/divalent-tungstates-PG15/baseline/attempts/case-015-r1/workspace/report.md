# Quantitative phase analysis

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases; they are not absolute bulk fractions.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (Å, °) | Crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| ZnWO4 (zinc tungstate) | P 2/c | a = 4.69242, b = 5.72040, c = 4.92814, β = 90.6293 | 75.60 wt% |
| WO3 (monoclinic) | P 21/n | a = 7.30533, b = 7.54158, c = 7.69294, β = 90.8089 | 16.85 wt% |
| ZnO (zincite) | P 63 m c | a = 3.25036, c = 5.21053 | 7.55 wt% |

The formal GSAS-II one-sigma uncertainties on the three fractions are 0.11, 0.08, and 0.09 wt%, respectively. These are covariance errors conditional on the selected model and are substantially smaller than the realistic systematic uncertainty. Given the absent instrument/profile calibration and strong peak overlap, about 1–2 wt% absolute is a more appropriate uncertainty scale for the reported crystalline fractions.

## Evidence and refinement

The result is chemically consistent with formation of ZnWO4 from the stated ZnO and WO3 precursors at 900 °C, with both precursors remaining. ZnWO4 accounts for the dominant reflections, including those near 19.02, 23.87/24.59, 30.70, 36.49, and 41.41° 2θ. Monoclinic WO3 is required by the 23–25° multiplet and distinct intensity near 26.69, 28.89, 33.41, and 34.31°. Residual intensity at approximately 31.88°, together with contributions near 34.3, 36.5, 47.8, 56.7, and 62.9°, identifies residual zincite ZnO. Under a common preliminary profile treatment, adding WO3 to a ZnWO4-only model reduced Rwp from about 18% to 12.7%, and adding ZnO reduced it to about 12.1%. Tetragonal and orthorhombic WO3 models and tested reduced tungsten oxides gave poorer peak-position/intensity agreement.

The final full-pattern Rietveld refinement used all 8,301 points from 9.9034 to 100.0949° 2θ. The Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio from the metadata were retained. Refined terms included phase scales and cells, a common pseudo-Voigt profile, zero offset, background, ZnWO4 coordinates, and the W coordinates in WO3. The weaker-scattering O coordinates of the minor WO3 phase and the ZnO coordinates were retained from their source structures because the laboratory pattern does not determine them robustly. A fixed Suortti surface-roughness correction was used for the pronounced reflection-geometry intensity curvature. The final statistics are Rwp = 5.480%, Rp = 4.146%, Rexp = 3.792%, and goodness of fit = 1.449; the refinement converged with maximum shift/e.s.d. = 0.089.

## Remaining uncertainty

The room-temperature monoclinic P 21/n WO3 model is favored, but its peak splitting is only marginally distinguishable from a closely related triclinic P -1 description at the available resolution. Thus the WO3 family and fraction are reliable, while the exact low-symmetry polymorph assignment remains moderately uncertain. The refined common profile parameters are correlated (especially U and V), and the surface correction is not independently calibrated; both limit the accuracy of the formal phase-fraction errors. No internal standard was supplied, so amorphous content and absolute whole-sample phase fractions cannot be measured. No additional crystalline phase is required by the residual, but a strongly overlapping or poorly crystalline component at roughly the 1–2 wt% level could escape detection.
