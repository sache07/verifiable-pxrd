# Quantitative phase analysis

Three crystalline phases were modeled: triclinic WO3, orthorhombic BaCO3 (witherite), and trace tetragonal BaWO4 (scheelite). Fractions are normalized over these crystalline phases only.

## Evidence and refinement

The chemical search was restricted by the BaCO3/WO3 precursor context. The observed 23.43, 23.90, and 24.66 degree peak group and its higher-angle sequence match triclinic WO3; the distinct 24.21, 28.04, 34–35, 42–47, and higher-angle lines require witherite BaCO3. A common refined zero offset of +0.31325 degrees accounts for the displacement of both patterns. The triclinic WO3 model (COD 2106293) fit better than the tested monoclinic and higher-symmetry WO3 alternatives.

Weak intensity at the expected shifted positions of scheelite BaWO4, including approximately 26.76, 32.17, 43.26, 48.97, and 54.76 degrees, supports a trace reaction product. Removing BaWO4 and reoptimizing the other parameters raised weighted chi-squared from 35546.8 to 36269.0 and Rwp from 7.203% to 7.276%. Other tested Ba-W-O candidates refined only at sub-0.2 wt% levels comparable to systematic residuals and were not retained.

The final GSAS-II refinement used the full 9.903–100.095 degree range, the reported Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, Poisson weights, a 12-term Chebyshev background, and an empirical global peak profile. Phase scales and the WO3/BaCO3 cells were refined. Atomic coordinates were retained from COD 2106293, 9010928, and 1527204. The BaWO4 cell and coordinates were fixed because its trace intensity cannot determine a reliable independent structure. Final Rwp = 7.203%, Rp = 5.462%, and goodness-of-fit = 2.073.

## Remaining uncertainty

The broad, overlapping peaks and lack of a calibrated instrumental profile limit separation of instrumental and sample broadening; two profile coefficients remain strongly correlated. The main-phase formal fraction errors therefore understate systematic uncertainty from profile choice, preferred orientation, and microabsorption. The approximately 0.6 wt% BaWO4 result is credible as a trace-phase indication, but its exact fraction and structure cannot be determined reliably from this pattern alone. Triclinic WO3 is the best tested model, although monoclinic pseudosymmetry remains a secondary structural uncertainty. No amorphous fraction can be measured without an internal standard.
