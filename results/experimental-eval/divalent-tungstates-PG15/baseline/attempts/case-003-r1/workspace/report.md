# Quantitative phase analysis

The pattern is modeled with three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over those phases only.

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction | Formal s.u. |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Monoclinic WO3 | P 21/c | 0.6011 (60.11 wt%) | 0.0027 |
| Scheelite CaWO4 | I 41/a | 0.1219 (12.19 wt%) | 0.0011 |
| Calcite CaCO3 | R -3 c | 0.2770 (27.70 wt%) | 0.0029 |

The final GSAS-II fit gives Rwp = 7.778%, Rp = 5.752%, Rexp = 2.394%, and goodness-of-fit = 3.255 over 8,293 points. The elevated goodness-of-fit reflects systematic peak-shape residuals and the absence of supplied pointwise uncertainties; the formal fraction s.u. values therefore understate total uncertainty.

## Evidence

Monoclinic WO3 is identified by the resolved 23.42/23.90/24.67° triplet and supporting peaks near 33.56, 33.89, 34.47, and 50.21°. Scheelite CaWO4 accounts for its diagnostic peaks near 18.89, 29.02, 31.72, 47.5, and 58.16°. Calcite accounts for the 29.71° maximum and the family near 36.30, 39.72, 43.47, 47.8, and 48.8°. No coherent unmatched peak family remains to justify a fourth crystalline phase.

Starting structures were COD 2311041 (WO3), COD 7219229 (CaWO4), and COD 1547347 (calcite). The refinement used the reported Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, a 12-term Chebyshev background, a reflection-geometry specimen-displacement correction, refined unit cells and phase scales, and phase-dependent isotropic size broadening for WO3 and CaWO4. A common isotropic displacement parameter was refined for WO3. Calcite required a March-Dollase correction along its natural (104) cleavage direction; CaWO4 and WO3 texture trials were negligible.

## Remaining uncertainty

The phase identities and the presence of all three phases are reliable. The fractions are semi-quantitative because no calibrated instrumental profile or external/internal standard was supplied and calcite is textured. Omitting the calcite texture correction changes the result to approximately 56.0 wt% WO3, 12.1 wt% CaWO4, and 32.0 wt% calcite, so a realistic systematic uncertainty is several percentage points for WO3 and calcite; the CaWO4 fraction is more stable at about 12 wt%. Any amorphous content cannot be determined and is excluded from the normalization.

The refined cells are supported by peak positions, but the individual light-atom coordinates—especially oxygen in WO3—cannot be determined reliably from this multiphase laboratory pattern. Those coordinates were retained from the reference structures rather than over-refined.
