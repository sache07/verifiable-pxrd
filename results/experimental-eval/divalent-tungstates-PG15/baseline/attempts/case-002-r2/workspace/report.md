# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is described by two crystalline phases, normalized over the modeled crystalline material:

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| Monoclinic WO3 | P 21/n (No. 14), COD 2311041 transformed from the published P 21/c setting | 64.58 wt% |
| Tetragonal BaO2 | I 4/mmm (No. 139), COD 1010113 | 35.42 wt% |

The refined cells are WO3: a = 7.27557, b = 7.49010, c = 7.64354 A, beta = 90.5629 degrees; and BaO2: a = 3.79474, c = 6.81848 A. Atomic coordinates were retained from the published structures; lattice parameters, phase scales, zero offset, background, sample broadening and mild preferred orientation were refined.

## Evidence

The 23.42/23.92/24.66 degree triplet and the associated 29.2, 34.4, 41-51 and higher-angle groups identify room-temperature monoclinic WO3. Tetragonal BaO2 accounts for the independent lines near 26.3, 27.1, 33.5, 43.0 and 46.6 degrees. Together the two phases index all prominent peak groups. The strongest expected scheelite BaWO4 line near 31.9 degrees is absent, so BaWO4 was not included. Alternative WO3 settings/published coordinate sets and a BaWO4 trial were inferior to the submitted model.

The final GSAS-II fit over 10.0-100.0 degrees gives Rwp = 12.644%, Rp = 9.251%, Rexp = 3.644%, and GOF = 3.474. Phase-specific RF values are 5.82% for WO3 and 4.77% for BaO2. The Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 doublet and reported 0.5 intensity ratio were used; raw counts were weighted as 1/I.

## Uncertainty

The conditional GSAS-II scale-factor esd is about 0.21 wt% for each fraction, but this understates model uncertainty. The instrument profile was not calibrated, the peaks show strong anisotropic broadening, and scale, broadening and preferred orientation are correlated. Comparisons among defensible profile and WO3 models changed the WO3 fraction by about one percentage point; a conservative uncertainty is therefore approximately +/-2 wt percentage points (absolute) on each reported fraction. The lattice parameters likewise carry unquantified systematic uncertainty because no internal angular standard was supplied.

Fractions are normalized only over the modeled crystalline phases. Amorphous material cannot be quantified from these data, and a trace or strongly overlapping crystalline impurity cannot be excluded. No additional crystalline phase can be assigned reliably from the residuals. The GOF above unity and structured residual around several intense/overlapped WO3 groups reflect these line-shape and intensity limitations.
