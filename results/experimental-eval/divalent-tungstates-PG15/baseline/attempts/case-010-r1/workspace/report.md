# Quantitative phase analysis

The pattern is best described by two crystalline phases: triclinic, pseudo-monoclinic WO3 (P -1) at **74.6 wt%** and cubic bixbyite Mn2O3 (I a -3) at **25.4 wt%**. Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases; amorphous material cannot be quantified without an internal standard.

|---|---|---|---:|
| WO3 | P -1 | a=7.30264, b=7.53468, c=7.69103; alpha=89.940, beta=90.673, gamma=89.961 | 74.6 |

## Evidence

The split WO3 triplet at 23.432, 23.910, and 24.682 degrees and its complex groups at 33–35, 47–50, and 54–56 degrees account for the dominant pattern. Mn2O3 is required by coherent residual peaks near 33.27, 38.54, 49.65, 55.49, and 66.05 degrees; adding it reduced Rwp from about 11% for WO3 alone to 7.44%. MnWO4 is not supported because its intense approximately 30.0/30.5-degree doublet is absent, and Mn3WO6 is excluded by the missing strong approximately 20.5-degree line. No additional crystalline phase produced a distinct unmatched fingerprint.

The final GSAS-II Rietveld fit used the supplied Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio over the entire supplied range. With no calibrated profile or pointwise uncertainties, the profile was refined empirically and Poisson counting uncertainties were used. A Bragg-Brentano specimen-displacement correction was refined instead of a constant zero offset. Unit cells, phase scales, isotropic size broadening, and heavy-cation coordinates were refined; oxygen coordinates and occupancies were held to reference values because unconstrained oxygen refinement was not reliable for this W-dominated laboratory X-ray pattern. Final statistics are Rp=5.47%, Rwp=7.44%, Rexp=2.66%, and GOF=2.80.

## Remaining uncertainty

The formal GSAS-II fraction standard uncertainty is about 0.33 wt%, but it omits model uncertainty. WO3 is metrically very close to monoclinic P21/n; that alternative gives a poorer fit (Rwp about 8.35%) but shifts the partition toward roughly 78/22 wt%. Weak orthorhombic distortion of Mn2O3 also cannot be resolved at the observed peak breadth. A realistic systematic uncertainty is therefore about **plus or minus 4 wt% per phase**, larger than the formal error. The submitted P -1/I a -3 model is the best-supported refinement, but the subtle symmetry choices and oxygen substructures should not be treated as uniquely determined.
