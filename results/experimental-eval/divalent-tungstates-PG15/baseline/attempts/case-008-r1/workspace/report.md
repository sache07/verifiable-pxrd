# PXRD phase analysis

The crystalline product is predominantly wolframite-type MnWO₄ with a small amount of unreacted monoclinic WO₃. Quantitative phase analysis, normalized over the modeled crystalline material, gives **97.98 wt% MnWO₄ and 2.02 wt% WO₃**. A practical uncertainty of about ±0.5 wt% is more realistic than the formal least-squares uncertainty (±0.085 wt%) because pointwise uncertainties and a calibrated instrument profile were not supplied.

The dominant P2/c MnWO₄ model accounts for the diagnostic sequence at 15.71, 18.68, 23.89, 24.36, 30.12/30.59, and 36.27° 2θ and the dense high-angle multiplets. Its refined cell is a = 4.83076, b = 5.75676, c = 4.99617 Å, β = 91.2165°. Residual peaks at about 23.45, 24.68, 33.58, and 34.47° form the characteristic room-temperature WO₃ series and are jointly explained by a P2₁/n model; its refined cell is a = 7.30063, b = 7.53850, c = 7.68483 Å, β = 90.7748°. Adding WO₃ lowered Rwp by about 0.30 percentage point in the comparable screening model and removed these independent residuals.

GSAS-II refinement used the reported Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths, an empirical Bragg–Brentano profile/specimen-height correction, a 15-term Chebyshev background, unit cells, and independent phase scale factors. Atomic coordinates and displacement parameters were held to the reference structures: the uncalibrated instrumental broadening and the 2 wt% abundance of WO₃ do not support reliable free refinement of fine structural details. The final fit has Rwp = 8.426%, Rp = 5.696%, Rexp = 2.759%, and goodness of fit = 3.059. The elevated goodness of fit and visible residual asymmetry around the strongest MnWO₄ peaks indicate remaining profile-model/systematic error.

MnO, Mn₃O₄, Mn₂O₃, and Mn₃WO₆ were tested as additional phases. They did not produce a coherent set of diagnostic peaks; trial scale factors merely absorbed small main-peak/profile residuals and changed Rwp by no more than 0.005 percentage point. They are therefore not included. Their absence should be interpreted as an approximate 0.5–1 wt% detection limit, not proof of zero abundance. The minor WO₃ identification is secure at the phase-family level, but this pattern alone cannot reliably distinguish very closely related monoclinic/triclinic WO₃ distortions or refine its atomic coordinates.
