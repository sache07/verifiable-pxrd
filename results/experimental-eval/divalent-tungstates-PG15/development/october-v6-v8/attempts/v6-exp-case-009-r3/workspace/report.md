# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is dominated by wolframite MnWO4 (P2/c), refined to 99.569(97) wt% of the modeled crystalline material. Its atomistic model accounts for the full sequence of strong and weak reflections from 10–100° 2θ, and the refined cell is a = 4.830535(82), b = 5.756807(92), c = 4.996050(85) Å, β = 91.2181(11)°. The refined CIF has full occupancies, plausible Mn–O/W–O contacts, and no CIF-audit flags.

A weak residual family near 23.37°, 33.18°, 38.46°, 55.39°, and 65.98° is consistent with bixbyite Mn2O3. Adding an audited Ia-3 model gives 0.431(97) wt% and lowers matched leave-one-out Rwp from 5.3614% to 5.3549%. This phase is tentative: Ia-3, Pbca, and I213 models are nearly tied at this intensity level, and its cell, coordinates, size, strain, and texture cannot be refined reliably. The submitted Mn2O3 CIF is therefore the fixed, conventional Ia-3 reference model, and its fraction should be treated as approximate rather than a robust trace assay.

Mn3O4, Mn3WO6, and orthorhombic WO3 refined to zero when retested against the mature model. Monoclinic WO3 gave no significant full-pattern benefit, while a triclinic WO3 trial obtained a small overlap-driven scale but predicted unsupported strong peaks at approximately 24.58°, 33.85–34.35°, 50.08°, and 56.42°; it was rejected. No remaining coherent residual family justified another crystalline phase.

The final refinement modeled the reported Cu Kα1/Kα2 doublet, a 20-point linear-interpolated background, sample displacement, minimal V/W Gaussian width terms, simple surface roughness, and isotropic MnWO4 crystallite size. Competing zero/shift, polarization, axial-divergence, Lorentzian-width, microstrain, and preferred-orientation branches were rejected for correlation, nonphysical values, instability, or negligible complete-pattern benefit. Final statistics are Rwp = 5.3549%, Rp = 4.0538%, Rexp = 3.4635%, and GOF = 1.5498 (8284 observations, 39 variables; maximum shift/esd = 0.009; no dropped variables).

Fractions are normalized only over the modeled crystalline phases and do not measure amorphous content. Since pointwise uncertainties and a calibrated instrument profile were not supplied, Poisson count uncertainties were assumed; the quoted fraction esds are covariance estimates and do not include model or instrument systematics. The approximately −0.508 mm refined specimen displacement also limits absolute lattice-parameter accuracy.
