# Quantitative crystalline-phase analysis

The pattern is best described by two crystalline phases: spinel Co3O4 and triclinic WO3. The refined fractions, normalized over those modeled crystalline phases, are:

| Phase | Structural model | Crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| Co3O4 | cubic Fd-3m (No. 227), a = 8.08076 A | 0.25997 (26.00 wt%) |
| WO3 | triclinic P-1 (No. 2), a = 7.30300 A, b = 7.53388 A, c = 7.69118 A, alpha = 89.9015 deg, beta = 90.6848 deg, gamma = 89.9473 deg | 0.74003 (74.00 wt%) |

The formal one-standard-deviation uncertainty from the scale-factor covariance is 0.00185 in fraction (0.19 absolute wt%) for each phase. This is conditional on the two-phase crystalline model. Because no internal standard, calibrated instrumental profile, or effective polarization was supplied, amorphous content cannot be quantified and practical systematic uncertainty in the crystalline ratio is likely larger than the formal value (potentially a few absolute wt%).

## Evidence and model selection

The Co3O4 model (starting structure COD 9005896) accounts for the coherent spinel reflection family, while triclinic WO3 (COD 2106293) accounts for the split low-angle WO3 peaks and the remaining WO3 families across the full 10-100 deg 2theta refinement range. Both submitted CIFs pass the supplied composition, occupancy, symmetry-expansion, duplicate-site, and contact audits without flags. The final Rietveld fit has Rwp = 3.6443%, Rp = 2.6025%, and GOF = 2.1189.

Matched complete-pattern replacements favored the submitted structures. Replacing triclinic WO3 with the clean monoclinic alternative increased Rwp to 3.7695% and introduced a 0.94 cell-parameter correlation. Two cobalt-vacancy spinel alternatives increased Rwp to 3.6986% and 3.9553%. Added CoWO4, CoO, W10O29, W18O49, and W3O8 models all refined to the numerical scale floor from the mature baseline, so there is no reliable multi-peak evidence for any of them. The largest remaining residuals are paired positive/negative shape or intensity lobes at strong reflections already assigned to Co3O4 or WO3, rather than a set of independent unmodeled peaks. Trace amounts below the diffraction detection limit cannot be excluded.

Only specimen displacement was retained for angular correction; a joint zero/displacement model was rejected at 99.97% correlation. The compact W-X peak profile and positive isotropic WO3 microstrain were retained. Removing WO3 microstrain worsened Rwp from 3.6443% to 3.6847%; size-only was poorer and correlated with the global Lorentzian term, and Co3O4 size/strain branches were unstable or nonphysical. Polarization, surface roughness, axial divergence, extra profile terms, and preferred orientation were rejected for instability, strong correlation, or no material full-range benefit.

Atomic coordinates were therefore kept at the audited literature-model values; coordinate and ADP refinements were not reliably determined by this powder pattern. Conservative positive isotropic displacement parameters were fixed in the submitted models (Co/O: 0.010 A^2; W: 0.005 A^2). The final project is converged (maximum shift/esd 0.0029, no SVD-dropped variables or covariance warnings), its regenerated profile is identical to the saved profile, and all generated total peak widths are positive.
