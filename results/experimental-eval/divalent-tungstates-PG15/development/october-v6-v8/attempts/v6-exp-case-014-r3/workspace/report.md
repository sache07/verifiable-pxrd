# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is modeled with four crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over those phases only:

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction |
|---|---:|---:|
| SrWO4 | I 41/a | 80.30(31)% |
| WO3 | P 21/c | 10.73(22)% |
| Sr3WO6 | P 21/n | 3.54(18)% |
| Sr3W2O9 | C 2/c | 5.43(22)% |

The final whole-pattern fit has Rwp = 15.246%, Rp = 9.919%, and GOF = 4.322. The relatively high GOF and visible systematic residual intensity mean that the covariance-derived uncertainties in parentheses are lower bounds; pointwise experimental uncertainties were not supplied.

## Evidence

Tetragonal scheelite SrWO4 (COD 1546036) accounts for the dominant reflection set. The residual pattern required three positive-scale atomistic components. In matched leave-one-phase-out tests, removing WO3, Sr3WO6, or Sr3W2O9 raised Rwp from 15.261% to 17.061%, 15.565%, and 15.785%, respectively. Each retained minor phase therefore has support from the complete pattern rather than a single overlap.

For WO3, the P 21/c model (COD 2311041) outperformed P-1 and Pnma alternatives under the same nuisance model. Monoclinic C 2/c Sr3W2O9 (COD 2311977) outperformed a hexagonal analogue; the latter refined to zero scale in the complete phase inventory. SrO, metallic Sr, carbonate, and other screened tungsten-oxide candidates gave only weak/overlap-driven scales or poorer complete-pattern behavior.

All submitted CIFs pass the supplied composition, occupancy, duplicate-site, symmetry, and contact audit. Atomic coordinates were kept at source-model values while phase scales and unit cells were refined. Profile testing retained one global Gaussian-angle term and a two-parameter surface-roughness envelope. Total Gaussian and Lorentzian widths remain positive for every regenerated reflection. Phase size/strain and preferred-orientation branches were rejected when they were unstable, boundary-limited, highly correlated, or improved only one angular region. A displacement-only correction of -0.580 mm was selected; the competing joint zero/displacement branch had 99.8% correlation.

## Remaining uncertainty

The exact Sr3WO6 structure cannot be determined reliably from this laboratory pattern. Its submitted P 21/n model was made by the charge-balanced Ca-to-Sr substitution in the Sr2CaWO6 topology (COD 2104688), then its cell and scale were refined. It is chemically and geometrically valid and improves several residual peaks, but it should be regarded as a provisional structural surrogate; its 3.54% fraction is correspondingly model-dependent.

The WO3 and Sr3W2O9 polymorph assignments are better supported but remain less secure than the dominant SrWO4 assignment because of peak overlap and fixed atomic coordinates. No additional atomistic phase passed the full-pattern tests. Without an internal standard, amorphous material cannot be quantified, so all reported fractions are conditional crystalline fractions.
