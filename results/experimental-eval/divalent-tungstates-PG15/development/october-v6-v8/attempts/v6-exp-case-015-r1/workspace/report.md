# Quantitative phase analysis

The modeled crystalline material is 73.97 wt% wolframite ZnWO4 (P 2/c), 19.21 wt% triclinic WO3 (P -1), and 6.82 wt% wurtzite ZnO (P 63 m c). Fractions are normalized over these crystalline phases. The final whole-pattern fit is Rwp = 4.720%, Rp = 3.602%, Rexp = 3.792%, and GOF = 1.248 (8,301 points, 46 refined variables).

## Evidence

The phase assemblage is chemically consistent with incomplete reaction of the stated ZnO and WO3 precursors to ZnWO4. Each phase explains a distinct multi-peak family: matched leave-one-phase-out fits raised Rwp to 40.58% without ZnWO4, 14.45% without WO3, and 6.42% without ZnO. Mature additions of rocksalt and zincblende ZnO refined to approximately 0.002 wt% and zero, respectively; the rocksalt cell also collapsed, so neither was accepted.

For WO3, clean atomistic P -1 and P21/c (Z = 8) models were carried through the same scale, cell, strain, envelope, and shared-positive-ADP treatment. P -1 converged at Rwp = 4.720%, versus 4.736% for P21/c, improved four of five angle bands, and remained preferred after penalizing its two extra cell variables (Delta BIC about 68). Pnma and P21/c (Z = 4) alternatives were substantially worse. The P -1 coordinates were retained from the audited reference model; the cell and one shared isotropic Uiso = 0.00854 A^2 were refined. ZnWO4 coordinates and all phase cells were refined; ZnO coordinates were kept at their reference values because coordinate refinement gave no material benefit.

The selected nuisance model uses specimen displacement, positive V/W Gaussian profile terms, and two surface-roughness envelope coefficients. Removing roughness increased Rwp to 8.92%; a polarization replacement refined to an impossible value. ZnWO4 isotropic size broadening (about 0.382 micrometres) and WO3 isotropic microstrain (about 3,665 microstrain) passed matched removal tests. Added Lorentzian terms, axial divergence, preferred orientation, and ZnO broadening were rejected as unstable, correlated, nonphysical, or immaterial.

## Remaining uncertainty

The triclinic-versus-monoclinic WO3 distinction is the main structural uncertainty: P -1 is the better complete-pattern model, but the small absolute Rwp separation and broad overlapping laboratory peaks do not make the symmetry assignment unequivocal. Phase fractions are nevertheless stable across those two WO3 models (about 0.2 wt% variation or less). Formal fraction ESDs are 0.13 wt% ZnWO4, 0.12 wt% WO3, and 0.08 wt% ZnO, but systematic uncertainty from profile and structural choices is larger. With no external standard, amorphous content cannot be measured, and trace crystalline phases below the pattern sensitivity cannot be excluded.

All submitted CIFs passed composition, occupancy, symmetry-expansion, duplicate-site, and contact screening. The final GSAS-II project also passed convergence, profile-regeneration, and total-peak-width audits without flags.
