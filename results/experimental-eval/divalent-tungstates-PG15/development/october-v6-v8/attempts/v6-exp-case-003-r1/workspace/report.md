# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The crystalline pattern is modeled with three phases. Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction | Refined cell |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Scheelite CaWO4 | I 41/a (No. 88) | 12.30 ± 0.11 wt% | a = 5.24168 Å, c = 11.37648 Å |
| Monoclinic WO3 (gamma-WO3) | P 21/c (No. 14) | 56.81 ± 0.16 wt% | a = 7.69116 Å, b = 7.53597 Å, c = 10.54051 Å, beta = 136.14288° |
| Calcite CaCO3 | R -3 c (No. 167) | 30.88 ± 0.20 wt% | a = 4.98754 Å, c = 17.06240 Å |

The quoted uncertainties are covariance-derived statistical scale uncertainties. They do not include phase-model, profile-model, preferred-orientation, absorption, sampling, or amorphous-content uncertainty and therefore understate the total uncertainty.

The final GSAS-II fit has Rwp = 7.4512%, Rp = 5.4263%, Rexp = 2.3944%, and goodness of fit = 3.1185 over 10.00–100.09° 2theta (8292 observations, 35 refined variables). The refinement converged with maximum shift/esd = 0.0267 and no dropped variables.

## Evidence and model selection

- Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the diagnostic 23.4–24.7° multiplet and many independent medium/high-angle lines. The accepted P 21/c model uses a transformed, doubled conventional gamma-WO3 cell. A triclinic WO3 replacement remained nonconvergent and worse (Rwp about 7.59%); Pnma and the chemically corrected small-cell P 21/c alternative were much worse. A separate malformed WO3 candidate with short O–O contacts was rejected.
- Scheelite CaWO4 is required by its independent 18.91°, 29.01/29.32°, 31.71°, and 34.46° families. Omitting it in screening increased Rwp from 12.80% to 16.77% even before the mature profile model. Monoclinic CaWO4 and Ca3WO6 replacements failed full-pattern and convergence tests.
- Calcite is required by the strong 29.69° line and supporting lines near 39.7°, 43.4°, 47.8°, and 48.8°. Omitting it increased the screening Rwp to 18.00%; an aragonite replacement was unstable and grossly worse.
- A four-phase aragonite addition refined to about 0.7 wt% and changed Rwp by only 0.006 percentage point. Its strongest calculated peaks all overlap accepted WO3/calcite reflections, with no multiple unique residual peaks. Its presence and fraction therefore cannot be determined reliably, and it is not included in the reported phase inventory.

The structural refinements were staged. The CaWO4 oxygen position and an antisymmetric pair of WO3 tungsten displacements were retained only after geometry and complete-pattern tests; oxygen-coordinate refinement in WO3 was unstable and was rolled back. Missing WO3 displacement factors were fixed at small positive values (Uiso = 0.005 Å² for W and 0.010 Å² for O) after a grouped refinement drove oxygen Uiso negative. All final CIFs pass the supplied composition, occupancy, symmetry, duplicate-site, and contact audit.

The selected profile contains positive global W, X, and Y terms, sample displacement, simple surface roughness, CaWO4 isotropic size and strain broadening, WO3 isotropic strain broadening, and a mild calcite [001] March–Dollase term. Leave-one-out copies degraded when each retained optional term was removed. Zero/displacement and joint angular branches were compared twice; the displacement-only model was retained, while the nominally lower-Rwp joint branch was rejected at 99.97% Zero/Shift correlation. The absolute microstrain values are not unique because global Y competes with a common strain offset, so they should not be interpreted as independently calibrated material properties.

## Remaining uncertainty

The three major phase identities are well supported, but the fit still contains systematic intensity/profile residuals at some dominant overlapping peaks, consistent with the goodness of fit above one. Trace aragonite remains unresolved rather than identified. No internal standard was provided, so amorphous content and absolute crystalline yield cannot be measured; all fractions are conditional crystalline fractions. The final GPX profile-regeneration and positive-total-width audits pass for every phase.
