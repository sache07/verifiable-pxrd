# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is described by two crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| Cobalt tungstate (wolframite), CoWO4 | P 2/c (No. 13), a = 4.66891, b = 5.68247, c = 4.94911 Å, beta = 90.0433° | 28.08(18) wt% |
| Monoclinic gamma-WO3 | P 21/n setting of No. 14 (equivalent to P 21/c), a = 7.30564, b = 7.53503, c = 7.69189 Å, beta = 89.2044° | 71.92(18) wt% |

The final full-pattern fit has Rwp = 4.7953%, Rp = 3.6532%, Rexp = 3.2739%, and goodness of fit = 1.4647 for 8,301 observations and 32 refined variables. Refinement converged with maximum shift/esd = 0.0129, no SVD-dropped or frozen variables, and positive calculated peak widths throughout the measured range.

## Evidence and model selection

The identification is consistent with the reported CoO + WO3 precursor mixture heated at 900 °C. More importantly, each model explains several independent reflections across the complete Cu K-alpha pattern. CoWO4 accounts for the resolved approximately 30.68/30.70° doublet, for which the nearest WO3 reflection is over 0.96° away. WO3 accounts for its characteristic 23.16–24.40° and 33.31–34.21° multiplets, which cannot be supplied by CoWO4. The two atomistic structures jointly reproduce the full angular range without a coherent family of residual peaks requiring another phase.

Candidates were audited before use. Both submitted CIFs have full occupancies, the expected reduced formulas, correct symmetry expansion, no duplicate sites, and no contact-screen flags. A freer CoWO4 oxygen-coordinate branch gave a slightly lower residual but an implausibly short W–O contact (about 1.64 Å), so it was rejected. The submitted chemistry-safe branch has W–O contacts of 1.78–2.13 Å and Co–O contacts of 2.00–2.29 Å. Alternative small-cell and triclinic WO3 descriptions either failed the complete-pattern test or became unstable; a malformed candidate with an O–O contact near 1.58 Å was rejected.

After the final structural rollback, CoO and Co3O4 were retested individually against the mature two-phase model. CoO refined to 0.035(96) wt% and improved Rwp by only 0.00005 percentage point, without independent multi-peak support. Co3O4 collapsed to the zero-scale bound. They are therefore not reported as phases. A CoO amount below roughly 0.3 wt% cannot be excluded by this dataset.

The retained nuisance model includes background, scales and cells, minimal global profile terms, specimen displacement, simple surface roughness, CoWO4 isotropic size broadening, WO3 isotropic microstrain, and a one-axis March–Dollase correction for WO3. Each optional term was checked by matched removal or competing branches; unsupported size/strain/texture terms were discarded. The angular correction helper selected the displacement-only baseline; a joint zero/displacement branch was rejected because of near-perfect correlation and an implausible displacement.

## Uncertainty and scope

The parenthesized fraction uncertainties are formal covariance esds (0.18 wt% absolute). They do not include model choice, preferred orientation, microabsorption, or uncalibrated instrumental-profile uncertainty; a more realistic practical uncertainty is approximately ±1–2 wt% absolute. No internal standard was supplied, so amorphous content cannot be quantified and is excluded from the normalization.

The phase identities and cells are well supported, but this laboratory pattern does not uniquely determine every internal coordinate. CoWO4 internal coordinates were fixed at the audited chemistry-safe branch, and WO3 internal coordinates were retained from the audited atomistic model because coordinate refinements were strongly correlated; only fit-supported cell/profile and a shared W displacement parameter were refined. The CIFs should therefore be treated as phase models consistent with this PXRD dataset, not as high-precision structure determinations.
