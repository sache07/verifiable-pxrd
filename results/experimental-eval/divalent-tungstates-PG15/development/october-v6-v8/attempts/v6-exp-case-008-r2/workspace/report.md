# PXRD phase analysis

The crystalline model contains two phases:

- **MnWO4 (wolframite, P2/c): 98.3065 wt%** of the modeled crystalline material.
- **Monoclinic WO3 (P21/c): 1.6935 wt%** of the modeled crystalline material.

The final whole-pattern fit has **Rwp = 6.0921%**, **Rp = 4.7667%**, and **GOF = 2.2103** over 9.9034–100.0949° 2θ (8301 points). The fractions are normalized only over the modeled crystalline phases; no amorphous fraction can be obtained without an internal standard.

## Evidence

The dominant peak positions, systematic absences, and intensity distribution are explained by wolframite MnWO4. Its refined cell is a = 4.83219, b = 5.75839, c = 4.99737 Å, β = 91.2157° (P2/c). Refining the MnWO4 coordinates materially improved the full-pattern fit, and the exported structure passed occupancy, symmetry-expansion, duplicate-site, and contact checks. A small [100] March–Dollase correction remained supported (ratio 1.02697).

Several coherent residual peaks, including the family near 23.45, 24.68, 33.58, and 34.45° 2θ after displacement correction, are jointly explained by monoclinic WO3. Removing WO3 from the mature model raises Rwp from 6.092% to 6.383%. Under the same nuisance model, audited triclinic and orthorhombic WO3 replacements both give about 6.179%, so the submitted monoclinic model is preferred. MnO, Mn3O4, and Mn3WO6 retests collapsed to the scale floor and were rejected; no third crystalline phase had stable multi-peak support.

The final model uses the supplied Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and ratio, a six-term fixed Chebyshev background, a specimen-displacement correction, positive global U/X width terms, axial-divergence asymmetry, and a refined polarization factor. Independent phase-size and microstrain terms were rejected because they were negative, limit-seeking, dropped, or nearly singular with the global profile terms. A surface-roughness model lowered Rwp numerically but was rejected because its two parameters were not identifiable: the constrained forms were boundary/arbitrary solutions strongly correlated with scale, while freeing both drove one parameter negative.

## Remaining uncertainty

The formal GSAS-II fraction uncertainty is about 0.00058 in weight fraction (0.058 percentage point) for each phase, but model and overlap uncertainty—especially for the 1.7 wt% WO3 component—is larger and is not reliably quantified by that covariance estimate. WO3 is structurally supported but less certain than MnWO4 because of its low abundance and extensive overlap. The large fitted specimen displacement is preferred over a zero-offset model; refining both together was rejected at approximately 99.7% correlation. No unmodeled component can be assigned reliably from the remaining residuals.
