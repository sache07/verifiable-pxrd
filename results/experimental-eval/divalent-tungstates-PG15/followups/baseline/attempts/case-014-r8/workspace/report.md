# Phase analysis and quantitative refinement

## Result

The pattern is multiphase. The modeled crystalline material consists primarily of scheelite SrWO4, with monoclinic WO3 and a less certain Sr3W2O9-type phase. Fractions are normalized over these three crystalline models only.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (A, degrees) | Weight fraction | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| SrWO4 | I 41/a (No. 88) | a = 5.42384, c = 11.97242 | 76.18 wt% | High confidence |
| WO3 | P 21/n (No. 14) | a = 7.31119, b = 7.54652, c = 7.69420, beta = 90.7291 | 10.24 wt% | High confidence |
| Sr3W2O9-type | C 2/c (No. 15) | a = 9.63762, b = 5.53864, c = 13.44175, beta = 103.5034 | 13.58 wt% | Provisional |

The final fit has Rwp = 16.689%, Rp = 12.482%, Rexp = 3.535%, and goodness of fit = 4.730 for 8,280 observations and 32 refined parameters.

## Evidence and refinement

SrWO4 is identified unambiguously by the scheelite series at approximately 18.32, 28.00, 30.22, 33.39, 45.45, 47.75, 51.98, and 56.08 degrees 2theta. The characteristic monoclinic WO3 triplet at 23.45, 23.97, and 24.73 degrees, together with its higher-angle lines, establishes residual WO3.

After fitting those two phases, independent peaks near 29.75, 32.63, 44.67, 50.6, and 61.2 degrees were indexed by the C 2/c Sr3W2O9 cell. In a fixed-profile comparison, adding this phase reduced Rwp from 21.78% to 19.93%, whereas SrO and a monoclinic SrWO4 polymorph gave only marginal improvements and unstable or negligible fractions. Both ambient and high-temperature SrCO3 models refined to the scale lower bound.

The refinement used the reported Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. Because no calibrated instrument profile or pointwise uncertainties were supplied, raw counts were weighted with the Poisson approximation w = 1/Iobs. Refined quantities were a 15-term background, angular zero, positive common W and X profile terms, phase scales and cells, and one moderate March-Dollase [001] correction for the Sr3W2O9-type model (ratio 0.815). Atomic coordinates and displacement factors were held at their reference values; exploratory free displacement factors became negative and were rejected.

## Remaining uncertainty

The Sr3W2O9 assignment is not definitive. Its available reference structure was reported from high-pressure synthesis, the phase has a relatively poor final Rf of 28.2%, and a residual line near 22.09 degrees is not reproduced adequately. The model is the best available indexing of the independent peak set, but this powder pattern alone cannot reliably distinguish it from a related Sr-rich Sr-W-O polymorph. Consequently, its reported 13.58 wt% fraction is provisional and should not be treated as a reliable absolute value without complementary data.

The high goodness of fit and structured residuals also show that the lack of an instrument calibration limits quantitative accuracy. Plausible profile/texture alternatives moved the modeled fractions over about 75.0-76.7 wt% SrWO4, 10.1-11.3 wt% WO3, and 12.8-14.9 wt% Sr3W2O9-type. These ranges are a more realistic indication of model sensitivity than the formal least-squares errors (about 0.2-0.3 wt%). No defensible fourth crystalline structure could be assigned. Amorphous content, if present, is not quantified.
