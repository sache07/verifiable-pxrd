# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

Four crystalline phases are supported by the pattern. Weight fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction | Formal e.s.d. |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PbWO4, scheelite | I 41/a (No. 88) | 72.45 wt% | 0.29 wt% |
| WO3, gamma monoclinic | P 21/c (No. 14) | 11.99 wt% | 0.26 wt% |
| PbO, massicot | Pbcm (No. 57) | 10.19 wt% | 0.16 wt% |
| PbO, litharge | P 4/nmm (No. 129) | 5.37 wt% | 0.14 wt% |

The final GSAS-II refinement has Rwp = 13.577%, Rp = 9.165%, Rexp = 3.502%, and goodness of fit = 3.886 for 8,282 observations and 37 refined variables.

## Evidence and model

The scheelite assignment accounts for the dominant line at about 27.71 degrees 2theta and the sequence near 33.06, 45.03, 47.28, 51.49, 55.54, and 56.82 degrees. Monoclinic WO3 is identified by its split low-angle triplet near 23.39, 23.89, and 24.64 degrees and the 33.5--34.8 degree group. Massicot and litharge are required by distinct PbO lines, including the massicot pair near 29.35/30.62 degrees and litharge contributions near 32.11, 45.92, 48.86, and 59.68 degrees. This is chemically consistent with incomplete reaction of PbO and WO3 at 500 degrees C and retention of both PbO polymorphs.

Leave-one-phase-out refinements raise Rwp from 13.58% to 37.51% without PbWO4, 14.88% without WO3, 16.10% without massicot, and 14.65% without litharge. Thus all four reported phases have independent profile support.

The Rietveld model used the supplied Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths (1.540598/1.544426 angstrom, intensity ratio 0.5), an 18-term Chebyshev-1 background, and a Poisson proxy weight of 1/I because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied. Phase scales and unit cells, zero offset, Gaussian/Lorentzian profile terms, polarization, and background were refined. Atomic coordinates, occupancies, and displacement parameters were held at the reference values; the submitted CIFs therefore represent refined lattice metrics rather than independently refined atomic geometries. Initial structures came from COD 9009811, 1528915, 2310433, and 9012700, respectively.

## Remaining uncertainty

A fifth crystalline phase cannot be assigned reliably. The largest narrow positive residual is near 25.91 degrees 2theta, with other maxima near 30.15 and 31.05 degrees. Fixed-cell tests of raspite PbWO4, WO2, Pb7W8O28.8, and corundum refined to zero. Pb3O4, Pb2O3, and W3O8 produced only small Rwp changes and poor phase-specific fits (Rf-squared about 64--71%); W3O8 and other tungsten suboxides are also inconsistent with the expected oxidation chemistry. None explains a unique, consistent residual-peak family, so no fraction is reported for them.

The fine monoclinic-versus-triclinic distortion of room-temperature WO3 is close to the resolution of this pattern; the chemically expected, lower-parameter gamma-monoclinic model is reported. The tabulated e.s.d.s are covariance estimates and understate the true uncertainty because the goodness of fit exceeds one, some systematic profile mismatch remains, and Pb/W mixtures can exhibit microabsorption. No amorphous fraction or absolute crystalline content can be obtained without an internal standard, so the reported fractions are conditional on the four-phase crystalline model.
