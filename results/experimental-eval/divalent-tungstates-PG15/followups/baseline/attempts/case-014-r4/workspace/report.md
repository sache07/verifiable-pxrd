# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is best represented by four crystalline components. Fractions below are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases; they do not quantify any amorphous or otherwise unmodeled material.

| Phase model | Space group | Refined cell (Å, °) | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| SrWO4, scheelite | I 41/a | a = 5.41852, c = 11.96048 | 75.35(27)% |
| Sr3W2O9-like | C 2/c | a = 9.63538, b = 5.53519, c = 13.41777, beta = 103.4847 | 12.43(21)% |
| WO3, monoclinic | P 21/n | a = 7.30264, b = 7.54183, c = 7.69185, beta = 90.8217 | 11.51(20)% |
| SrO, rocksalt | F m -3 m | a = 5.14595 | 0.718(144)% |

Parenthesized uncertainties are GSAS-II covariance e.s.d.s. and are smaller than the practical uncertainty from phase/model choice.

The final Rietveld fit has Rwp = 13.984%, Rp = 9.697%, Rexp = 3.536%, and goodness of fit = 3.962 for 8,270 points and 28 refined parameters. The refinement converged with maximum shift/e.s.d. = 0.17.

## Evidence and refinement

The precursor context (SrCO3 + WO3, 900 °C) and the dominant line sequence identify scheelite SrWO4. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the characteristic split group near 23.45, 23.97, and 24.73° 2theta and its higher-angle line groups. The C2/c model accounts coherently for additional peaks near 29.75, 32.63, 44.67, 50.55, and 55–58°; adding it and refining its cell lowered Rwp from about 16.4% to 14.0%. Weak lines consistent with rocksalt SrO give a positive trace scale; omitting SrO raises Rwp from 13.984% to about 14.03%. Tested orthorhombic and rhombohedral SrCO3 models refined to zero and were not retained.

Starting structures were COD 1546036 (SrWO4), 1528915 (WO3), 2311977 (Sr3W2O9), and 7200689 (SrO). GSAS-II used the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, a 12-term Chebyshev background, and Poisson weights of 1/I because pointwise uncertainties were absent. Phase scales and unit cells, background, and effective polarization were refined. The empirical peak profile and specimen displacement were established before the final scale/cell cycles; atomic coordinates were retained from the reference structures because unconstrained coordinate/ADP refinements were unstable or chemically implausible.

## Remaining uncertainty

The Sr3W2O9 assignment is **provisional**. Its reference structure was reported for a high-pressure product, while this sample metadata reports only 900 °C heating. The fitted c axis (13.418 Å) is about 2.5% shorter than the reference value (13.763 Å), and its phase Bragg RF is 27.4%. The diffraction evidence supports a related C2/c Sr-rich tungstate lattice, but this powder pattern alone does not determine its exact stoichiometry or atomic structure reliably. Consequently, its 12.43% fraction is conditional on the Sr3W2O9 formula/model; reasonable profile variants moved it to roughly 12–15 wt% and SrWO4 to roughly 72–76 wt%.

WO3 itself is secure, but monoclinic P 21/n and a triclinic WO3 candidate gave nearly equivalent fits, so the exact low-symmetry polymorph is less certain than the chemical phase identification. SrO is near the practical detection limit and should be interpreted as approximately 0–1 wt%, not as a precise 0.718 wt%. The absence of supplied counting uncertainties and a calibrated instrument profile makes all formal fraction e.s.d.s optimistic; the structured residual around the strongest overlapping lines is reflected in the goodness of fit above 1.
