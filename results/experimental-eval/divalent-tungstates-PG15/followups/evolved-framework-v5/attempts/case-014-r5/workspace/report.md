# Powder XRD phase analysis

The accepted model contains three crystalline phases. Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases:

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| SrWO4 | Scheelite, `I 41/a` | 0.7706 (77.06 wt%) |
| WO3 | Monoclinic, `P 21/c` (alternative cell setting) | 0.1017 (10.17 wt%) |
| Sr3WO6 | Ordered double-perovskite type, `P 21/n` | 0.1277 (12.77 wt%) |

## Evidence

The dominant pattern is indexed by scheelite SrWO4, including its strong 112, 004/200, 024/220, and higher-angle families. A COD SrWO4 reference topology (1546036) refined to a = 5.41795 Å and c = 11.95888 Å. The refined oxygen position gives a physical W–O distance of about 1.905 Å.

The characteristic monoclinic WO3 group near 23.46, 23.92, and 24.70° 2θ, together with its higher-angle family, requires residual WO3. The submitted physical model is based on COD 2311041 and is expressed in a valid `P 21/c` alternative setting. Its shortest W–O distance is about 1.715 Å, and no short O–O contact is present.

After those two phases were fitted, a broad residual family at approximately 28.11, 29.72–29.78, 30.87, 42.1–42.7, 51.77, 53.38–53.94, 61.2–61.4, and 63.84° was consistently indexed by a monoclinic ordered Sr2(SrW)O6 model. The Sr3WO6 model was built from the isostructural Ca3WO6 reference (COD 1563081), followed by cell and profile refinement. It has physical nearest W–O and Sr–O distances of about 2.045 and 2.451 Å. Its positive scale remained stable in the complete three-phase refinement. The resulting bulk Sr:W molar ratio is 1.011:1, consistent with the SrCO3 + WO3 chemical context. SrCO3, SrO, and Sr3W2O9 alternatives either refined to zero/near-zero scale or lacked broad reflection-family support.

The saved fit gives Rwp = 14.374%, Rp = 9.799%, Rexp = 3.536%, and goodness-of-fit 4.065. A fresh structure-factor replay from `final.gpx` over all 8,282 measured points gives Rwp = 14.370%. The replay produced no negative-profile, displacement-tensor, or peak-width review flags.

## Remaining uncertainty

SrWO4 and WO3 are well supported. Sr3WO6 is less certain: no exact-composition reference was available in the frozen index, its oxygen coordinates were retained from the physical analogue because bounded coordinate refinements were unstable, and preferred orientation/profile broadening is correlated with its scale. Its topology is plausible and well indexed, but its detailed coordinates and 12.77 wt% fraction should not be treated as high-precision determinations; systematic uncertainty of several weight-percent is more realistic than a formal counting-statistical error.

A broad residual near 22.1° and intensity mismatches near 29.75, 32.60, 44.66, and 48.62° remain. They do not establish a chemically credible fourth phase with a stable positive scale, so any trace additional structure or its fraction cannot be determined reliably from this pattern. The reported fractions do not quantify amorphous material or absolute crystallinity.
