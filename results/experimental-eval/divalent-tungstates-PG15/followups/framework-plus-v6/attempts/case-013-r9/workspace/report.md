# Powder XRD phase analysis

The refined crystalline inventory contains seven phases. Fractions are normalized over the modeled crystalline phases; no amorphous/internal-standard correction is possible from this scan alone.

| Phase | wt% | Structural basis |
|---|---:|---|
| PbWO4 (I 41/a) | 58.28 | COD 9009811 (published scheelite) |
| PbO (P b c m) | 7.21 | COD 2310433 (published massicot) |
| Pb2WO5 (C 2/m) | 13.16 | analogue-derived from Pb2MoO5, COD 7220560, by Mo6+ -> W6+ substitution |
| WO3 (P 21/c) | 10.80 | COD 2311041 (published monoclinic WO3) |
| PbO (P 4/n m m) | 4.44 | COD 9012700 chemistry, represented in the correct P4/nmm origin choice |
| Pb3WO6 (P n m a) | 5.21 | analogue-derived from Pb3UO6, COD 2310493, by isovalent U6+ -> W6+ substitution |
| Pb3O4 (P 42/m b c) | 0.91 | COD 9012124 (published ambient tetragonal minium) |

## Evidence and model selection

Scheelite PbWO4 is the dominant phase and accounts for the strong families throughout the scan. Residual precursor/decomposition peaks require both PbO polymorphs and monoclinic WO3. The selected WO3 record (COD 2311041) has physical W–O geometry and outperformed the alternate monoclinic/high-pressure records.

No exact Pb2WO5 entry was available. The C2/m Pb2MoO5 parent (COD 7220560) was adapted by the isovalent Mo6+→W6+ substitution; its coherent families near 25.9, 29.2, 31.0 and 34.9° survive scale/cell replay, while C2/c, Cc and the alternative C2/m parents fit substantially worse as replacements.

The large residual at 30.22° and additional families near 26.59, 27.45, 29.0–29.4, 51.46, 54.43 and 57.1° require a second lead-rich tungstate. A Pnma Pb3WO6 model was constructed from published Pb3UO6 (COD 2310493) by U6+→W6+ substitution. Its adapted cell and 0.05 Å-capped heavy-atom shifts remain physical (approximately W–O 1.91–2.22 Å and shortest Pb–O about 2.17 Å). Removing this phase raises Rwp from 10.587% to about 11.00%.

Ambient tetragonal Pb3O4 is weak but replay-stable at about 0.91 wt%, with support from partly independent families around 26.7, 31.0, 45–46.5, 50.2 and 75.6°. Its cell could not be refined reliably without exceeding the reference-mismatch range, so the published cell was retained. Pb2O3 was rejected because its two strongest lines required a runaway monoclinic distortion; cubic PbO2 gave a numerical gain but is a 160 kbar fluorite record and has no defensible 500 °C ambient analogue.

The accepted nuisance model uses a smooth eight-coefficient background, one specimen displacement (-450.9 µm), polarization 0.823, U/W/X = 5.960/21.629/6.000, and fixed SH/L = 0.00666. Scheelite has an independently supported size of 0.171 µm. Supported March–Dollase terms are 0.827 along [100] for massicot and 0.885 along [010] for Pb2WO5. Roughness, extra SH/L freedom, and bound PbO/Pb3WO6 size/strain or texture terms were rejected.

## Fit and uncertainty

The final full-range fit has Rwp = 10.587%, Rp = 7.374% and GOF = 3.031 over 8282 points. The model remains above the Poisson-limit diagnostic (about 3.5% Rwp), so minor residual profile/intensity mismatch remains.

PbWO4, both PbO polymorphs, WO3 and Pb2WO5 are well supported as phases, but oxygen coordinates and fixed displacement parameters are only weakly constrained by Cu X-ray powder data. Pb2WO5 and Pb3WO6 are analogue-derived rather than uniquely solved ab initio; their topology and fractions are defensible, but detailed light-atom coordinates are source-conditioned. Pb3O4 is near the practical detection limit, and its reported fraction should be treated as approximate. Alternative weak-phase partitions can improve Rwp only by using chemically/structurally inadmissible cells, so they were not reported as determined phases.
