# Powder XRD phase analysis

The modeled crystalline product contains three phases. Weight fractions are normalized over these phases only.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (Å, °) | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| SrWO4, scheelite | I41/a (No. 88) | a = 5.41922, c = 11.96026 | 0.62383 |
| WO3 | P21/c (No. 14) | a = 7.69335, b = 7.53986, c = 10.53003, β = 136.06864 | 0.09719 |
| Sr2WO5 | Pnma (No. 62) | a = 7.25332, b = 5.54863, c = 10.89597 | 0.27898 |

The final full-range fit has Rwp = 6.8634%, Rp = 5.2541%, and goodness of fit = 1.9445. The formal GSAS-II fraction e.s.d.'s are about 0.001–0.002, but they do not include structural-model or nuisance-model uncertainty.

## Evidence

SrWO4 (COD 1546036) accounts for the dominant scheelite reflection families and is chemically expected from SrCO3 + WO3 at 900 °C. Monoclinic WO3 (COD 2311041) accounts for the remaining characteristic split tungsten-oxide peaks; alternate monoclinic/triclinic WO3 records gave very similar fits, but this P21/c model gave the best physically bounded comparison with only small cell changes.

A two-phase SrWO4 + WO3 model left coherent unmatched families and gave Rwp near 17% before profile correction. The strongest residual families at approximately 14.99, 16.61, 20.65, 22.09, 29.75, 35.32, 44.67, and 56.97° 2θ are jointly indexed by orthorhombic Sr2WO5. No exact database entry was available, so the Pnma Ba2ReO5 prototype (COD 1529756) was adapted by the isovalent substitutions Ba→Sr and Re→W. Its scale remained substantial under the common profile, and its refined geometry is physical (shortest W–O 1.76 Å, Sr–O 2.57 Å, and O–O 2.52 Å).

SrCO3 and SrO trials refined to zero scale. Sr3W2O9 remained below about 0.2 wt% without unique-peak support. An orthorhombic SrCO3 trial improved the residual only by collapsing to an unphysical crystallite size and unstable cell, so it was rejected.

The accepted nuisance model uses one specimen-displacement correction, a smooth positive six-term background, a common physically bounded profile, surface roughness, and isotropic phase size broadening. Isotropic strain refinements became negative or unsupported, and March–Dollase tests on symmetry-distinct axes stayed close to unity and changed Rwp by less than 0.015 percentage point; strain and texture were therefore omitted. The serialized project was reopened and replayed over all 8,282 measured points without frozen variables or physical-review flags.

## Remaining uncertainty

The Sr2WO5 phase assignment, cell, and abundance are well supported by multiple partly isolated families, but its exact oxygen coordinates cannot be determined reliably from this pattern: the oxygen sublattice remains that of the chemically screened prototype, while only the heavy-atom coordinates were accepted after restrained trials. The WO3 polymorph is also less certain than its composition because closely related low-symmetry records fit similarly.

Instrumental Lorentzian width and crystallite-size broadening are not independently identifiable without a standard, so the apparent sizes (about 0.09–0.13 µm) should not be treated as reliable absolute domain sizes. Polarization, roughness, displacement factors, and scale are correlated; plausible accepted-model trials moved the SrWO4 and Sr2WO5 fractions by roughly 0.02 absolute and WO3 by roughly 0.01. The tabulated fractions are the submitted best-fit values, while those larger ranges are a more realistic measure of systematic uncertainty.
