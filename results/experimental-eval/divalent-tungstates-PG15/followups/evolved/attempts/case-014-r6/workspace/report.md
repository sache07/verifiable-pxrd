# Phase analysis and refinement report

## Result

The submitted five-phase model gives the following fractions, normalized to 100% over the modeled crystalline material:

| Phase | Space group | Weight % | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| SrWO4, scheelite | I 41/a | 77.61 | Established |
| WO3, monoclinic | P 21/n | 9.63 | Established |
| Sr3WO6, prototype-derived model | P 21/n | 6.40 | Tentative structure and fraction |
| Sr3W2O9 | C 2/c | 5.25 | Diffraction-supported but tentative assignment |
| SrO | F m -3 m | 1.11 | Probable trace phase |

The final full-range GSAS-II replay uses all 8,282 points from 10.0094 to 99.9944 degrees 2theta and converged with Rwp = 14.678%, Rp = 10.386%, GOF = 4.156, and maximum shift/esd = 0.00024. Poisson counting uncertainties, sigma = sqrt(max(intensity, 1)), were used. The GOF above one and visible residual structure show that counting statistics do not describe all model error.

## Evidence and model selection

The chemical context (SrCO3 + WO3 fired at 900 degrees C) and the dominant peak positions identify scheelite SrWO4. COD 1546036 gives the coherent dominant families over the complete angular range. Monoclinic WO3 from COD 1528915 accounts for the principal remaining W-oxide families; alternative ambient WO3 records were slightly worse or refined to zero scale. A modest, replay-stable [110] March-Dollase correction was retained for WO3.

Two independent Sr-rich residual families remain after those phases. A database search found no exact Sr3WO6 record, so stoichiometric analogues were screened. The submitted model uses the P21/n Sr3UO6 topology (COD 2001752), isovalent U-to-W substitution, and a regularized WO6 shell guided by P21/n Ca3WO6 (COD 1563081). Its final W-O distances are 1.901-1.985 angstrom and it accounts chiefly for the approximately 18, 20.8, 29.8, 41-42, 51-53, and 61 degree families. A numerically better P21 analogue was rejected because it produced an implausible 1.86-2.43 angstrom W-O range. Because the submitted atomic coordinates were not independently solved from these powder data, both the Sr3WO6 structure and its 6.40% fraction are tentative.

Exact-record C2/c Sr3W2O9 (COD 2311977) independently improves sharp families near 32.6 and 44.7 degrees and associated higher-angle lines while retaining a positive Sr3WO6 scale. Adding it to the physical three-phase model lowered Rwp from about 15.65% to 14.79%. The database structure was reported from high-pressure synthesis, however, which is surprising for the stated ambient-pressure firing. The diffraction supports this topology or a closely related Sr-rich oxide, but does not make the exact Sr3W2O9 identity secure.

Rock-salt SrO is supported at trace level by its nine accessible reflections, including families near 30.5, 35.3, and 50.5 degrees; adding it lowered Rwp from about 14.79% to 14.68%. Its 1.11% fraction is consequently less secure than either major-phase fraction.

The common baseline has a smooth six-coefficient Chebyshev background, one zero correction, nonnegative W/X peak-width terms, and axial asymmetry. U repeatedly attempted to become negative and was pruned. Phase size, strain, roughness, other texture axes, and other extinction terms were unsupported and left neutral. A scheelite extinction correction improved multiple strong/weak families; after refinement it was fixed at its accepted value to remove an 88% scale correlation. Refined cells were likewise locked for the final common-profile replay after simultaneous weak-phase cell refinement proved less stable.

## Remaining uncertainty

Fergusonite SrWO4 refined to zero scale once Sr3W2O9 was present. High-temperature hexagonal SrCO3 also refined to zero. Orthorhombic SrCO3 can absorb about 2% through overlapped lines and lower Rwp slightly, but its strongest diagnostic support is inadequate; trace residual carbonate therefore remains possible but is not determined reliably. W18O49 took about 1.8% in a trial, but its strongest calculated line fell in an already overpredicted region and the small gain was spread over a dense 1,071-reflection forest, so it was rejected as a residual surrogate rather than identified as a phase.

Positive residuals remain, especially near 22.1, 29.8-30.9, 32.6, 44.7, 52.0, and 56.1 degrees. They may reflect imperfect minor-phase coordinates, disorder, or an unresolved trace component. The reported fractions exclude amorphous material and unidentified crystalline intensity, and no absolute absorption correction or external standard was available. Thus the decimal values preserve the submitted project exactly, but should not be interpreted as corresponding accuracy; model-choice uncertainty dominates for Sr3WO6, Sr3W2O9, and SrO.
