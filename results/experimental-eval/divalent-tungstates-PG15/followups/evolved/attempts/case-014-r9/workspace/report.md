# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is best described by three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized only over these modeled crystalline phases.

| Phase | Structural model | Refined cell | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| SrWO4 | scheelite, I 41/a | a = 5.41867 Å, c = 11.96109 Å | 80.09 ± 0.30 wt% |
| WO3 | monoclinic gamma-WO3, P 21/c | a = 7.69210 Å, b = 7.53839 Å, c = 10.53338 Å, beta = 136.1110° (equivalent skewed setting) | 10.35 ± 0.20 wt% |
| Sr3WO6 | monoclinic double-perovskite-related model, P 21/n | a = 6.10027 Å, b = 6.22430 Å, c = 8.42981 Å, beta = 90.4285° | 9.56 ± 0.25 wt% |

The quoted uncertainties are GSAS-II covariance estimates conditional on this model; systematic uncertainty is larger.

## Evidence and model selection

SrWO4 accounts for the dominant peak families throughout the pattern and is chemically expected from SrCO3 + WO3 after heating at 900 °C. Refining only its oxygen positional block was stable on replay and improved both the fit and geometry, giving four W–O distances of 1.976 Å and an eight-coordinate Sr environment of 2.430–2.514 Å.

Residual peaks at approximately 23.45, 23.97, and 24.73° 2theta, together with additional families across the full range, require WO3. Exact-composition monoclinic and triclinic records were compared under the same final profile. The conventional monoclinic model gave the lowest final-condition Rwp; the triclinic metric relaxed close to monoclinic symmetry. A neutron-derived monoclinic coordinate set (COD 2311041) was selected over a marginally better-fitting legacy record because it retains six physical W–O contacts at both W sites. The powder data do not distinguish the monoclinic/triclinic distortion or individual WO3 coordinates with high confidence, so the submitted WO3 coordinates are source coordinates and only its cell and scale were refined.

Coherent residual families near 29.3–29.8, 42.1, 51.9, 53.4, 61.1–61.4, 69.6, 76.4, and 94.4° support Sr3WO6. No exact Sr3WO6 database entry was available, so an isovalent W6+ substitution into the room-temperature P 21/n Sr3UO6 prototype (COD 2001752) was used; an independently substituted Ca3WO6 parent gave essentially the same phase assignment. The refined cell is supported by multiple partly isolated families and the model retains a regular WO6 octahedron (W–O = 2.066–2.106 Å). However, its oxygen coordinates could not be refined stably from this approximately 10 wt% contribution. Thus Sr3WO6 presence is credible, but its detailed structure and absolute fraction cannot be determined reliably from this pattern alone.

SrCO3, SrO, SrO2, Sr2WO5, Sr3W2O9, high-pressure fergusonite SrWO4, alternative WO3 polymorphs, and reduced tungsten oxides were screened. Their scales collapsed, their cells became unstable, they lacked coherent unique families, or their synthesis conditions/geometry were incompatible. Rechallenging the plausible weak-phase watch list after establishing the common profile did not justify another crystalline phase.

## Refinement quality and limitations

The final physical baseline uses the Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 doublet, a smooth 10-term Chebyshev background, one specimen-displacement correction, and nonnegative common W/X peak-width terms. Roughness, texture, extinction, phase size/strain, and extra asymmetry terms were tested from neutral values and rejected as unsupported, unstable, or bound solutions. Representative isolated-family observed/calculated FWHM values are 0.244/0.226° at 23.45°, 0.253/0.270° at 45.45°, and 0.369/0.366° at 72.98°; peak positions remain aligned across low, middle, and high angle. Eight save/reopen replays changed any phase fraction by less than 0.02 wt%.

Final statistics are Rwp = 15.337%, Rp = 10.484%, Rexp = 3.538%, and GOF = 4.343 for 8265 points and 29 refined parameters. Systematic intensity residuals and broad shoulders remain, especially around 29–33° and in several middle-angle families. The polarization correction reached its physical upper limit, and no calibrated instrument profile or supplied pointwise uncertainties were available. Alternate physically plausible WO3 records shift the WO3 fraction by about 0.6 wt% and the Sr3WO6 fraction by about 0.2 wt%; these model spreads are more realistic than the formal errors. Amorphous content, if present, is not measured, and all reported fractions are normalized over the three modeled crystalline phases.
