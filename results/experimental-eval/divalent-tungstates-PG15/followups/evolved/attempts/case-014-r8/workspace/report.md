# PXRD phase analysis

Four crystalline phases were retained. Weight fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases; no amorphous fraction can be obtained from this measurement.

| Phase | Space group | Weight % | Assignment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| SrWO4 scheelite | I 41/a | 77.87 ± 0.28 | identified, COD 1546036 |
| monoclinic WO3 | P 21/c | 11.11 ± 0.20 | identified, neutron model COD 2311041 |
| Sr3WO6-like phase | P 21/c | 9.44 ± 0.24 | provisional Sr3UO6-derived prototype |
| SrO rocksalt | F m -3 m | 1.58 ± 0.15 | identified trace phase, COD 7200689 |

The uncertainties in the table are covariance ESDs, not total uncertainties. Source-record choice, inventory ambiguity, peak overlap, and especially the provisional Sr3WO6-like model produce larger systematic uncertainty. The modeled inventory has Sr:W = 1.007:1 by moles, which is chemically coherent with the listed SrCO3/WO3 precursors, although their starting proportions were not supplied.

## Evidence and structural screening

- Scheelite SrWO4 accounts for the dominant families throughout the pattern. Its refined cell is a = 5.41866 Å and c = 11.96144 Å. The fixed source geometry is sound (four W–O distances of 1.882 Å and eight Sr–O distances of 2.503–2.562 Å).
- Monoclinic WO3 accounts for a distinct dense set of low-, middle-, and high-angle reflections. Several WO3 records were challenged under the same restrained nuisance model. The old room-temperature record COD 1528915 gave a slightly lower provisional Rwp but contains an unphysical 1.58 Å O–O contact, so the record—not the phase—was rejected. The accepted neutron model COD 2311041 has physically credible distorted WO6 octahedra (W–O = 1.714–2.159 Å; shortest O–O = 2.539 Å). The physical triclinic COD 2106293 fit almost equivalently, so the precise WO3 polymorph remains a smaller source of model uncertainty.
- No exact ambient-pressure Sr3WO6 entry was available. Stoichiometric analogue screening favored monoclinic Sr3UO6 (COD 2001752) over the other inspected Ca/Ba parents. Replacing U(VI) by isovalent W(VI) gives sensible octahedral W–O distances of 2.066–2.108 Å and no short contacts. Its positive scale is supported by several partly separated families around 29.6–29.9°, 41.7–43.2°, 51.5–52.9°, and 61.2°. However, its Bragg Rf is 29.45%, many reflections overlap the other phases, and no exact Sr3WO6 structure was found. The assignment is therefore only **Sr3WO6-like**: its exact structure and 9.44 wt% fraction cannot be determined reliably from this pattern.
- SrO is supported by the coherent rocksalt 111, 200, 220, 311, 222, 400, 331, and 420 sequence at approximately 30.44, 35.23, 50.51, 59.96, 62.90, 74.01, 81.92, and 84.51°. Its refined a = 5.13500 Å gives six Sr–O contacts of 2.568 Å. At 1.58 wt%, its fraction remains sensitive to overlap and profile assumptions.

All retained sites are fully occupied. Atomic coordinates and displacement parameters were held at their screened source/prototype values because coordinate trials were underdetermined and generated implausible geometry.

## Refinement model and residual-led tests

Repeated high-angle doublets require the reported Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths (1.540598/1.544426 Å) and 0.5 intensity ratio, despite the metadata saying Kα1 was intended. A single-line model broadened these doublets and was rejected. The accepted common profile is nonnegative: U = V = Y = 0, W = 52.1657, X = 7.4505, with near-zero SH/L = 0.002. Isolated low-, middle-, and high-angle families show no coherent remaining position or width drift under this profile. A smooth six-coefficient Chebyshev-1 background, polarization factor 1.0, and one effective specimen-displacement correction of −579.0 µm were used. The large displacement is treated as a combined calibration/sample-mount correction, not a precise physical specimen height.

Cells, the common background/profile, all scales, and the displacement correction were reconverged after the WO3 source change. One-phase-at-a-time size and strain trials were then restarted from neutral values. They either worsened the fit, hit a size bound, refined negative or insignificant strain, or coupled width to phase fraction. A replay-stable Sr3WO6-like isotropic-strain trial lowered Rwp by only 0.014 percentage point, worsened the high-angle region where strain should be most diagnostic, was 59% correlated with scale, and moved its fraction to 10.15%; it was rejected. The SrO strain trial doubled that trace fraction and was also rejected. There was no coherent family-dependent width pattern warranting anisotropic broadening.

No preferred-orientation term was retained. SrWO4 and WO3 axis trials changed Rwp by at most 0.006 percentage point and refined close to unity. For the provisional Sr3WO6-like phase, the replay-stable [100] trial drew more than the entire net gain from the crowded 29–30° region while worsening major low-, middle-, and high-angle families. [110] did not replay stably, and [011]/[111] behaved as scale surrogates and collapsed the phase fraction. SrO texture trials diverged because the trace scale could not support an independent orientation parameter. Roughness and additional asymmetry terms were likewise unsupported.

The submitted project was saved, reopened, and replayed over 10.02–99.98° with only the four phase scales active. The final replay converged with maximum shift/ESD = 5.3×10⁻7. The fit has Rwp = 15.768%, Rp = 11.059%, Rexp = 3.535%, and GOF = 4.461 for 8,280 observations. The elevated GOF reflects Poisson weights applied to raw counts without supplied pointwise uncertainties, plus remaining structural/profile mismatch; it does not by itself establish another resolved phase.

## Remaining uncertainty

The high-pressure Sr3W2O9 record COD 2311977 refines to 1.32 wt% in a scale-only watch-list challenge. Almost all improvement comes from the residual at 32.47°, while its strong predicted 29.25° and 44.09° families worsen the fit; its synthesis conditions also conflict with the reported ambient-pressure 900 °C preparation. Orthorhombic SrCO3 tests at 1.65 wt% but has poor Bragg agreement (Rf = 28.5%) and its strongest 25.56° family worsens the residual. W3O8 tests below 0.3 wt% and below two formal ESDs; fergusonite SrWO4, rhombohedral SrCO3, and the Sr2WO5 analogue collapse to zero scale.

Thus an unidentified or unreacted crystalline trace at roughly the 1–2 wt% level is possible, but no fifth structure or fraction is reliable enough to report. The four-phase fractions also exclude any amorphous material and should not be interpreted as bulk absolute phase fractions without an internal standard.
