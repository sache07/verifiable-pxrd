# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is best described by seven crystalline phases. Weight fractions below are normalized to those seven phases; they do not measure amorphous or otherwise unmodeled material. Parentheses are formal 1-sigma covariance uncertainties and are smaller than the likely model-dependent uncertainty.

The fit over 10.02-99.98 degrees 2theta has Rp = 7.102%, Rwp = 9.993%, Rexp = 3.502%, and goodness of fit = 2.854 for 8,280 points and 44 refined parameters. The persisted project replay converged with maximum shift/esd 0.014.

## Evidence

The chemical inventory is consistent with partial reaction of PbO and WO3 at 500 degrees C: dominant PbWO4, residual PbO and WO3, lead-rich Pb-W-O intermediates, and a small oxidized lead phase. Multiple families, rather than single peaks, support each retained component. Diagnostic calculated reflections include 27.66, 44.99, and 51.45 degrees for stolzite; 29.34, 45.37, and 53.32 degrees for massicot; 28.88, 32.06, and 48.85 degrees for litharge; the 23.35/23.84/24.63-degree WO3 cluster; and 26.78, 32.66, and 47.74 degrees for minium. The prototype phases account for coherent families at 25.88, 29.22, 43.36, and 47.22 degrees (Pb2WO5-type) and 27.48, 28.68, 30.19, and about 51 degrees (Pb3WO6-type), including partly isolated reflections that the precursor/product phases do not reproduce.

No suitable exact database entries were available for the two lead-rich tungstates. The Pb2MoO5 parent permits an isovalent, closely size-matched Mo6+/W6+ substitution; its refined cell remains close to the parent and gives W-O = 1.765-1.792 A and Pb-O = 2.307-2.877 A. The Pb3UO6-derived model is a less conservative isovalent substitution, but its refined geometry remains physical (W-O = 1.876-2.184 A and Pb-O = 2.210-2.957 A). Atomic coordinates were held at the source/prototype values while cells and phase scales were refined, so these data do not establish the exact atomic arrangements of either phase.

Exact and alternate records were challenged under the same common nuisance model. Raspite PbWO4 and a physically screened alpha-Bi2O3-type Pb2O3 model refined to zero scale. An exact Pb2O3 database record was rejected before acceptance because it contains an implausible approximately 1.34 A Pb-O contact; its physical alternate also refined to zero. Alternate PbWO4, PbO, and WO3 records/polymorphs gave poorer or chemically inappropriate models. A PbO2 trial formally refined to about 0.64 (6) wt% and lowered Rwp to 9.929%, but its strongest expected families were internally inconsistent: support was mainly at overlapped positions, while prominent 25.59- and 36.39-degree lines were absent or overpredicted. PbO2 is also incompatible with the PbO + WO3, 500 degrees C context. It was therefore excluded as a likely residual/profile surrogate; a trace PbO2 fraction cannot be determined reliably from this pattern.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The measurement was modeled as Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 with the supplied wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. Because uncertainties were absent, sigma = sqrt(counts) was used. The accepted baseline uses a smooth eight-term Chebyshev background, one specimen-shift correction (zero fixed), nonnegative common U/W/X broadening, and refined polarization. Roughness, transparency, unused size/strain terms, and other phase corrections are neutral. SH/L and roughness trials were unsupported. The only accepted phase broadening is isotropic strain for the Pb2WO5-type phase, 2289 (267) microstrain. Replay-stable March-Dollase terms were retained for massicot [011] (1.293), Pb2WO5 [100] (1.195), and Pb3WO6 [001] (1.931); symmetry-distinct alternatives and bound width terms were pruned.

The large Pb3WO6 texture correction and its substituted prototype make its identity and fraction notably more model-dependent than the formal uncertainty suggests. Pb2WO5 is supported more strongly, but its exact structure is still not determined by this powder pattern. Residuals are serially correlated (Durbin-Watson 0.278), and the elevated goodness of fit shows remaining systematic mismatch, so all quoted covariance errors should be treated as lower bounds. Without an internal standard, no amorphous fraction or absolute crystalline yield can be reported.
