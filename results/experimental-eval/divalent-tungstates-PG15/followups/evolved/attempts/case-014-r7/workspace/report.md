# PXRD phase analysis

The modeled crystalline material is 64.13 wt% scheelite SrWO4, 26.59 wt% Sr2WO5, and 9.28 wt% monoclinic WO3. Fractions are normalized only over these crystalline phases.

## Evidence

SrWO4 accounts for the dominant scheelite families, including the strong features near 18.32, 28.00, 33.39, 45.44, 51.98, 56.08, and 57.34° 2θ. Monoclinic WO3 is required by its diagnostic 23.43/23.91/24.68° triplet and corroborating families near 34, 42, 50–51, and 56°. The Pnma Sr2WO5 model is supported by coherent partly isolated families near 15.00, 16.60, 22.07, 30.87, 32.56, 34.09, 35.25, and 59.92°; removing it leaves these peaks unexplained.

SrWO4 started from COD 1546036 and WO3 from the monoclinic neutron model COD 2311041. The unusual WO3 cell is an alternate P21/c setting. No exact Sr2WO5 entry was available. A stoichiometric-analogue search selected isovalent Ba2ReO5 (COD 1529756): Ba2+→Sr2+ and Re6+→W6+ preserve charge and site roles. Its geometry remains plausible after cell refinement (W–O 1.76–2.01 Å; Sr–O 2.60–2.91 Å). An Nd2TiO5-derived Pnma alternate gave Rwp about 14.5%, the Gd2ReO5 topology produced implausibly long W–O contacts, and the nominal Sr2MnO5 search hit was actually Sr2Mn2O5.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The full 10.009–99.994° range was refined in GSAS-II with the reported Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and fixed 0.5 intensity ratio. The accepted baseline uses a smooth eight-coefficient Chebyshev background, one specimen-displacement correction (−0.598 mm for the 200 mm project radius), nonnegative common W/X profile terms, SH/L asymmetry, surface roughness, and isotropic size terms only for WO3 and Sr2WO5. Strain and texture trials were not retained. Atomic coordinates were held to the source/prototype models; cells, scales, and the stated profile terms were refined. The final fit is Rwp = 7.335%, Rp = 5.647%, and GOF = 2.078.

The parenthesized fraction uncertainties are GSAS-II formal 1σ values and do not include model uncertainty. The Sr2WO5 phase assignment and metric are well supported, but its detailed atomic structure is not independently determined: the coordinates are prototype-derived, so its fraction is best read as about 27 wt%, with roughly 1–2 wt% plausible systematic uncertainty. Re-challenges with SrCO3, Sr3WO6 prototypes, Sr3W2O9, and fergusonite SrWO4 either remained statistically marginal or gained intensity only at overlaps while predicting unsupported unique families; none can be quantified reliably. A sub-percent additional crystalline phase cannot be excluded. Amorphous content cannot be determined without an external standard.
