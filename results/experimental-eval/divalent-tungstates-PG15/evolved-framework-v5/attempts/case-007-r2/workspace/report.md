# Powder XRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is explained by three crystalline phases. Fractions are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction | Formal 1σ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MgWO₄ | P2/c | 76.253 wt% | 0.172 wt% |
| WO₃ | P2₁/c | 15.598 wt% | 0.090 wt% |
| MgO | Fm-3m | 8.150 wt% | 0.183 wt% |

The full-grid GSAS-II refinement used all 8,301 points from 9.9034–100.0949° 2θ and the supplied Cu Kα₁/Kα₂ wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. It converged with Rwp = 7.6160%, Rp = 5.2909%, Rwp,min = 2.8626%, and GOF = 2.6665. The final replay had no SVD-zero parameters and a maximum shift/σ of 0.0086.

## Evidence and structural model

The dominant reflection family is wolframite-type MgWO₄: diagnostic peaks near 15.93°, 19.24°, and the 30.81/31.12/31.82° group are reproduced by the refined P2/c cell. The 23.43/23.93/24.67° group and its higher-angle companions require monoclinic WO₃. The isolated lines near 43.21°, 62.54°, and 75.00° require rocksalt MgO. This inventory is also chemically ordinary for MgO + WO₃ heated at 900 °C.

Starting structures were COD 9013902 (MgWO₄), 2311041 (room-temperature WO₃), and 9000499 (MgO). Refined cells and physically checked coordinates are in the submitted CIFs. MgWO₄ has W–O distances of 1.797–2.133 Å and Mg–O distances of 2.020–2.178 Å; MgO has Mg–O = 2.106 Å. The retained WO₃ model has distorted W–O distances of 1.709–2.158 Å, consistent with monoclinic WO₃.

A residual-family audit matched 63 of 64 detected features with prominence above 300 counts to an accepted-phase reflection within 0.25° 2θ. The only unmatched feature is a weak, isolated bump near 11.56° without higher-angle family support. It was treated as background/noise; there is no evidence warranting a fourth crystalline phase.

## Uncertainty and limits

The tabulated 1σ values are covariance precision, not total accuracy. Physically acceptable profile/roughness branches changed the MgWO₄/WO₃ split by roughly 1–2 weight-percentage points, larger than the formal errors. Effective polarization was not supplied and correlated surface/profile terms had to be bounded, so the relative crystalline fractions should be interpreted at that systematic scale. Without an internal standard, an amorphous fraction—and therefore absolute whole-specimen phase fractions—cannot be determined reliably.

WO₃ oxygen coordinates also cannot be determined reliably from this pattern: freeing them produced correlated 0.2–0.4 Å shifts for only a marginal fit improvement. The published oxygen sublattice was therefore retained; W positions were adjusted before the final stable profile/cell refinement. MgWO₄ coordinate refinement remained physically stable. Residual systematic line-shape/intensity error remains, as reflected by GOF > 1, but every coherent reflection family is assigned.
