# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is modeled as unreacted monoclinic WO3 and spinel Co3O4; no crystalline cobalt tungstate is required. The refined crystalline-phase fractions are **75.64 wt% WO3** and **24.36 wt% Co3O4**, normalized over these two modeled phases.

## Evidence

The WO3 model accounts for the strong 23.46/23.95/24.71 degree triplet and the associated 33.6–34.5, 42.2, 47.6, 50.3 and 56.2 degree families. Co3O4 is independently fixed by its 31.64, 37.20, 45.14, 59.68, 65.56, 77.20 and 94.34 degree reflections. Their refined fraction ratio is also close to the 74.3:25.7 mass ratio expected for an unreacted 1:1 Co:W mixture of WO3 and one-third mole Co3O4.

The selected WO3 reference is the physical room-temperature P21/c neutron model COD 2311041. A triclinic WO3 checkpoint was slightly worse under the same physical profile constraints. A malformed short-cell WO3 reference with a 0.46 Angstrom contact was rejected. W coordinates were refined first; O coordinates were accepted only from a bounded start (about 0.054 Angstrom O-site RMS movement from the reference). The final structures retain sensible nearest distances (WO3 W-O >= about 1.73 Angstrom; Co3O4 Co-O >= about 1.91 Angstrom).

An analogue-derived wolframite CoWO4 test (ZnWO4 COD 1520641 with Zn replaced by Co) refined to the positive-scale floor, and the diagnostic 30.4–30.7 degree wolframite doublet is absent. Adding a second exact-composition WO3 model produced only 1.4–2.0 wt% with strong scale anticorrelation (-0.81 to -0.91) against the main WO3 phase; this cannot be distinguished reliably from reference/intensity mismatch and is not reported as a separate phase.

## Fit and uncertainty

The full-grid fit has Rwp = **3.181%**, Rp = **2.275%**, and goodness of fit = **1.850** for 8301 points with Poisson count weights. Residual structure is concentrated around the strongest WO3 peaks, so the exact oxygen coordinates and any sub-2 wt% WO3 polymorph are not uniquely determined. Common-Uiso/profile trials move the phase fractions by roughly 1–2 percentage points; that is a more realistic fraction uncertainty than the small least-squares scale e.s.d. No amorphous fraction is quantified, and the reported fractions are only over modeled crystalline material.

The submitted CIFs, fractions and `fit.csv` were exported from `final.gpx`.
