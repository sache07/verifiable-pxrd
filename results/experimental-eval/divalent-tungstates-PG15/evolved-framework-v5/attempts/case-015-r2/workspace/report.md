The modeled crystalline material is 73.61 wt% monoclinic wolframite ZnWO4, 19.38 wt% monoclinic WO3, and 7.02 wt% wurtzite ZnO. Fractions are normalized over these three crystalline phases.

## Evidence and refinement

The ZnO + WO3 precursor chemistry and 900 °C treatment support ZnWO4 as the ordinary reaction product. The dominant reflection set indexes as wolframite ZnWO4. After fitting it, a broad coherent residual family near 23–29°, 33–34°, 47–50°, and 54–56° is accounted for by monoclinic WO3. A separate family near 31.9°, 34.4°, 36.5°, 47.7°, 56.8°, 63.0°, and 68–69° identifies wurtzite ZnO. All coherent reflection families are represented by these phases; remaining strong residuals are chiefly alternating peak-shape/intensity errors at already indexed reflections rather than a fourth family.

Physical COD reference topologies 1520641 (ZnWO4), 2311041 (WO3), and 9004178 (ZnO) were refined against the full Cu Kα1/Kα2 scan. Refined terms include phase scales and cells, phase-specific isotropic crystallite size, one isotropic displacement parameter per phase, axial divergence, specimen displacement and roughness, and a smooth positive background. Unsupported preferred orientation and microstrain terms were omitted. Bounded coordinate trials either worsened the fit or pushed many oxygen coordinates to limits, so the physical reference coordinates were retained. The final atomic replay gives Rwp = 5.3221%, Rp = 4.1260%, and χ² = 16354.9 over 8,301 points using weights 1/max(counts, 1).

## Remaining uncertainty

The WO3 chemistry is secure, but its exact closely related monoclinic/triclinic setting is less strongly determined than the phase identity. The selected P21/c model has physical contacts and ambient-temperature plausibility; a close P21/n candidate contained an implausible 1.57 Å O–O contact, while a triclinic model gave only a marginal fit change. Oxygen coordinates are consequently reference-constrained rather than independently precise.

The reported fractions are reliable as a three-phase crystalline normalization, but are model-dependent because phase scale, surface roughness, background, and displacement parameters are correlated. No internal standard was supplied, so amorphous content and absolute crystallinity cannot be determined; the values should not be interpreted as total-specimen fractions if an amorphous component is present.
