# Powder X-ray diffraction phase analysis

## Result

The specimen is a three-phase crystalline mixture. Weight fractions are normalized over the modeled crystalline material.

| Phase | Structure model | Refined cell (Å, °) | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| ZnWO4 | wolframite, P 2/c, COD 1520641 | a=4.69390, b=5.72240, c=4.92951, β=90.6280 | 74.57(12)% |
| WO3 | monoclinic, P 21/c, COD 2311041 | a=7.69714, b=7.54192, c=10.5380, β=136.086 | 18.03(8)% |

## Evidence and refinement

The dominant reflection family is indexed by wolframite ZnWO4, the ordinary reaction product for the ZnO–WO3 precursor context after firing at 900 °C. A separate family around 23–29°, 33–34°, and 41° 2θ requires monoclinic WO3. The remaining coherent peaks at approximately 31.8°, 34.4°, 36.3°, 47.6°, and 56.6° are jointly accounted for by wurtzite ZnO. All three phases have positive, stable scales and broad support across the scan; no coherent residual family remains that justifies another crystalline phase.

Several exact-composition WO3 references were tested in the complete multiphase state. COD 2311041 gave the best combination of fit and a physically established neutron-diffraction WO3 topology; the close older monoclinic alternative did not justify replacing it, and triclinic/orthorhombic models fit worse. Bounded heavy- then light-atom coordinate refinement retained plausible coordination: ZnWO4 W–O = 1.86–2.13 Å and Zn–O = 2.03–2.18 Å, WO3 W–O = 1.67–2.24 Å, and ZnO Zn–O = 1.96–2.05 Å.

The final GSAS-II model uses the stated Cu Kα1/Kα2 doublet, a refined zero shift of +0.14013°, surface roughness, separate isotropic crystallite sizes, background, phase scales, and cells. Preferred orientation and microstrain tests were unsupported and were omitted. A fresh atomic forward replay over all 8,301 measured points (10–100° 2θ) gives Rwp = 6.228%, Rp = 4.757%, χ² = 22,398.95, and goodness-of-fit = 1.645. The replay reported no negative-background, nonphysical-width, or displacement-tensor flags.

## Remaining uncertainty

The phase identities and three-phase inventory are reliable. The quoted fractions are crystalline-only: without an internal standard, amorphous content cannot be measured and is not included. Peak overlap, broadening, roughness, and reference/coordinate correlations make practical fraction uncertainty larger than the formal errors, plausibly about 1–2 percentage points. The WO3 topology and cell are well supported, but its individual oxygen coordinates and displacement parameters are less directly constrained by this broad laboratory X-ray pattern than the phase identity and fraction.
