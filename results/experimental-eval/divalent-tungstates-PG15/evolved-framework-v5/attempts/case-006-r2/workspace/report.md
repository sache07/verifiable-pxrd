# PXRD phase analysis

The CuO + WO3 precursor mixture fired at 800 °C is modeled as **97.92 wt% triclinic CuWO4 (P -1)** and **2.08 wt% monoclinic WO3 (P 21/c)**, normalized over the modeled crystalline phases. No CuO phase was accepted: tested CuO models converged to zero scale and did not explain a coherent residual family.

## Evidence and structural models

Fifteen independent strong reflections index a triclinic CuWO4 cell. The refined cell is a = 4.70810 Å, b = 5.84223 Å, c = 4.88109 Å, alpha = 91.6538°, beta = 92.5332°, gamma = 82.7523° (V = 133.019 Å³). The P -1 atom topology was initialized from exact-composition COD 4000806, but that entry's high-pressure metric was not retained: the lattice was independently indexed from this specimen and then refined. Cu and O coordinates were refined with W retained as the origin anchor. The resulting six-coordinate ranges, Cu–O = 1.871–2.419 Å and W–O = 1.829–2.276 Å, are physically reasonable.

After the CuWO4 refinement, a coherent residual family at approximately 23.4–24.7°, 34.5°, 42.2°, 50–56°, and 61–63° 2theta was jointly indexed by monoclinic WO3. A room-temperature neutron model (COD 2311041, P 21/c) gave a stable positive scale and broad-family support. Its refined nonstandard cell is a = 7.68513 Å, b = 7.54169 Å, c = 10.51764 Å, beta = 136.0494° (V = 423.079 Å³); both W sites retain physical sixfold W–O coordination (1.715–2.160 Å). Once these two phases accounted for all coherent reflection families, no further phase search was performed. The remaining largest residuals are predominantly derivative-shaped around strong CuWO4 peaks rather than a new indexable family.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The final GSAS-II Rietveld model covers all 8,301 measured points from 9.9034° to 100.0949° 2theta and uses the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. The independently regenerated atomic profile gives Rwp = 7.1875%, Rp = 5.2023%, Rexp = 2.4333%, and goodness of fit = 2.9538. Replay found positive phase scales, nonnegative background and analytic peak widths, valid displacement parameters, and no physical-review flags.

The formal scale-derived standard uncertainty is about 0.06 wt% for each normalized fraction, but it understates model systematics. Holding plausible common WO3 displacement factors between 0.003 and 0.020 Å² changes the WO3 result from 2.02 to 2.23 wt% with essentially unchanged Rwp; a practical value is therefore **2.08 ± about 0.2 wt% WO3**. The detailed WO3 coordinates and displacement parameters cannot be determined reliably from a roughly 2 wt% contribution and were retained from the physical reference model while its cell and scale were refined. CuWO4 coordinates are profile-supported, although common displacement and polarization parameters remain correlated and should not be interpreted as a precise thermal-motion determination.

Because pointwise experimental uncertainties were not supplied, Poisson count weights were used and the formal fit statistics are not rigorous uncertainty estimates. Failure to detect CuO means only that no CuO diffraction contribution was supported at this data quality; a quantitative CuO detection limit was not established.
