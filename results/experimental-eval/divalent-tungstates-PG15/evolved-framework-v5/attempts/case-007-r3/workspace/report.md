# Powder X-ray diffraction analysis

## Result

The crystalline specimen is modeled as three phases. Weight fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases; they do not include any amorphous material.

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction | Refined cell |
|---|---|---:|---|
| MgWO4 | wolframite type, P2/c | 77.02 wt% | a = 4.68994 A, b = 5.67407 A, c = 4.92859 A, beta = 90.7724 deg |
| WO3 | room-temperature monoclinic, P21/c (nonstandard cell) | 15.05 wt% | a = 7.68986 A, b = 7.53422 A, c = 10.52960 A, beta = 136.0942 deg |
| MgO | rocksalt, Fm-3m | 7.93 wt% | a = 4.21219 A |

The full-pattern fit over 10-100 deg 2theta gives Rwp = 8.3200%, Rp = 5.9045%, Rexp = 2.8569%, and goodness of fit = 2.9122. The final project replays on all 8,301 measured points and reproduces the exported calculated profile and phase fractions.

## Evidence

The phase inventory is chemically consistent with the MgO + WO3 precursor context and partial formation of MgWO4 at 900 deg C. The wolframite MgWO4 model accounts for the dominant families near 15.93, 19.24, 24.21/24.92, 30.81/31.11, 36.79, and 41.43/41.86 deg 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the characteristic clusters near 23.43/23.93/24.67 and 33.57/33.87/34.47 deg. Rocksalt MgO accounts for the residual family near 43.21, 62.54, 75.00, and 94.26 deg. After these three phases are included, the remaining extrema are principally positive/negative profile lobes at modeled reflections rather than an independent coherent reflection family.

Exact-composition WO3 references were compared in the same three-phase fit. The selected neutron-derived monoclinic model (COD 2311041) gave the best combination of fit and physical local geometry. A numerically close alternative was rejected because it contains an approximately 1.575 A O-O contact. MgWO4 and MgO use COD 9013902 and COD 9000499, respectively. Refined structures pass the final short-contact screen.

## Remaining uncertainty

The covariance-only one-sigma fraction uncertainties are 0.15 wt% for MgWO4, 0.08 wt% for WO3, and 0.16 wt% for MgO, but these are conditional on the selected structural and profile models. They understate realistic systematic uncertainty from peak overlap, preferred orientation/microstructure, correlated displacement parameters, and the absence of an external intensity standard. In particular, the MgO common displacement parameter reached its imposed upper bound. The phase inventory and approximate fractions are reliable, but sub-percent absolute accuracy and the last reported decimal places cannot be established reliably from this pattern alone.

The heavy-atom framework and unit cells are well supported. Individual oxygen coordinates, especially in WO3 and MgWO4, are not independently precise from this laboratory powder pattern; physical reference geometry and bounded coordinate refinement were therefore retained where free refinement overfit intensity. No fourth crystalline phase is required by a coherent reflection family, although a trace phase below the pattern's detection limit or an amorphous fraction cannot be excluded or quantified.
