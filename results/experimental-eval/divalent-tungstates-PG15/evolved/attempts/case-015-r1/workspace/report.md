## Evidence and model selection

ZnWO4 accounts for the dominant families near 15.6, 19.0, 23.9/24.6, 30.7, 36.5, 51.9, 53.8, and 64.9° 2θ. Coherent residual families near 23.27, 26.69, 28.89, 33.35, and 34.31° require WO3. Peaks near 31.9, 47.8, 56.7, and 63.0° require residual ZnO; adding ZnO under the common profile lowered Rwp from about 13.11% to 12.28% before the later baseline improvements and gave a stable positive scale.

Composition, full occupancy, symmetry, source conditions, and coordination geometry were screened before fitting. The final models give W–O = 1.79–2.14 Å and Zn–O = 2.02–2.22 Å in ZnWO4, W–O = 1.72–2.16 Å in WO3, and tetrahedral Zn–O = 1.98–1.99 Å in ZnO. The room-temperature monoclinic neutron WO3 record was preferred to an older monoclinic record and to triclinic, orthorhombic, tetragonal, hexagonal, and high-pressure alternatives. Under the same final nuisance model, monoclinic WO3 gave Rwp = 5.017% versus 5.041% for triclinic before strain, and 4.850% versus 4.886% with the same isotropic strain freedom. Other WO3 polymorphs gave substantially poorer fits. Reduced tungsten oxides did not have coherent support at their strongest families.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The full 9.903–100.095° range was replayed after saving and reopening `final.gpx`. The baseline uses a smooth, positive ten-term Chebyshev background, one specimen-height correction, U = V = 0 with positive W/X broadening, and one SH/L axial-divergence term. A physical Suortti roughness correction was needed for the shared cross-phase low-angle intensity trend. The reported Kα2/Kα1 ratio of 0.5 was retained; refining it gave 0.473 with negligible improvement. Free polarization was rejected because it either exceeded its physical range or became highly correlated with roughness and scale.

Only WO3 required phase-specific broadening: a replay-stable [100] uniaxial microstrain model. Size terms ran to bounds or were unsupported, and competing preferred-orientation axes produced only tiny, non-unique improvements, so size and texture were fixed at neutral values. Atomic coordinates, occupancies, and displacement parameters were kept at the physically screened reference values; this powder pattern does not determine independent coordinate refinements reliably. Unit cells and phase scales were refined.

The final fit has Rwp = 4.8177%, Rp = 3.7281%, Rexp = 3.7916%, and goodness of fit = 1.2730. Formal covariance-only fraction uncertainties are about 0.10, 0.08, and 0.08 wt% for ZnWO4, WO3, and ZnO, respectively, but these are optimistic. Alternate physical profile, strain, and symmetry models indicate practical uncertainties of roughly ±1.5 wt% for ZnWO4 and WO3 and ±0.5 wt% for ZnO. The main remaining mismatch is localized around heavily overlapped ZnWO4/Ka-doublet profiles near 30.6–31.0°. No additional crystalline phase can be identified reliably from the residual, and an amorphous fraction cannot be determined without an internal standard.
