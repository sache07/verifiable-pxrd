# PXRD phase analysis

The modeled crystalline material is **65.10 wt% triclinic WO3** and **34.90 wt% tetragonal BaO2**, normalized over those two phases. The covariance-only standard uncertainty is 0.14 wt% for each fraction; a more realistic uncertainty from profile and structure choice is about 0.5 wt%. No crystalline reaction product is established at a reliable level.

## Evidence and refinement

The 23.4–24.7° WO3 multiplet and its middle- and high-angle families require the distorted triclinic model. Under the same restrained isotropic nuisance model, COD 2106293 gave Rwp about 7.44%, compared with 7.90% for monoclinic COD 2311041, 8.80% for monoclinic COD 1528915, and 9.70% for orthorhombic COD 2107312. BaO2 is independently identified by its strong 26.28°, 27.02°, and 33.47° families. This inventory is also chemically consistent with largely unreacted BaO2 and WO3 after the reported 200 °C treatment.

The final full-range Cu Kα1/Kα2 refinement used Poisson count weights, a smooth 10-coefficient Chebyshev background, one zero correction, a positive common Gaussian U term, phase-specific size/strain broadening, and only weak WO3 [001] March–Dollase texture. W, X, and SH/L trials were rejected when they became negative, singular, or strongly correlated. A specimen-displacement-only alternative was much worse (Rwp 11.51%). The replay-stable profile used WO3 [011] uniaxial strain and BaO2 [001] uniaxial strain; a lower-Rwp six-term triclinic strain model was rejected because it did not replay without ~90% correlations. Unused roughness, extinction, Babinet, H-strain, and anisotropy terms are neutral.

Only the BaO2 oxygen coordinate was supported for coordinate refinement: z=0.3942(9), giving a physically sensible peroxide O–O distance of 1.450 Å. WO3 coordinates were retained from the precise source structure; its six nearest W–O distances span 1.716–2.220 Å. Peak positions and widths are tracked from the low-angle 23–27° families through the 43–56° and 69–93° regions. Remaining residuals are mainly intensity/shape errors in overlapping WO3 peaks, not coherent unindexed sharp peaks.

Final statistics are Rwp=7.0839%, Rp=5.3439%, Rexp=3.6408%, and GOF=1.9493 (8301 observations, 31 variables). A save/reopen/full-range replay converged with zero singular values and maximum shift/esd 0.0037.

## Remaining uncertainty

WO3 phase identity is secure, but the exact P -1 distortion is less secure than the composition: broad anisotropic peaks and the nearly monoclinic metric mean that a subtly distorted or intergrown monoclinic form cannot be excluded, despite the substantially worse tested monoclinic records. The submitted WO3 CIF is therefore the best-supported powder model, not a unique single-crystal-level structure determination.

BaWO4 and other Ba–W–O products were re-challenged after the common profile was fixed. BaWO4 refined to only 0.086 wt% (2.2σ) when forced narrow and to zero when broadened; its diagnostic 32.16° reflection lies on a negative residual. Ba3WO6 varied from 0.04 wt% (narrow) to 0.30 wt% (broad), with support confined to overlapped residuals. These width-dependent results are not reliable phase detections, so neither was included; roughly 0.4 wt% is a model-dependent trace-phase ceiling, not a formal detection limit. Amorphous or extremely nanocrystalline material is not quantified, and all submitted fractions apply only to the modeled crystalline phases.
