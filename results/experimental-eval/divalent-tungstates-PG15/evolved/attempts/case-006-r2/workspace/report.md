# Phase analysis and refinement report

## Result

The modeled crystalline material is triclinic CuWO₄ with a minor monoclinic WO₃ residue. Fractions are normalized only over these modeled crystalline phases; no amorphous fraction can be obtained without an internal standard.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| CuWO₄ | P -1 | a = 4.70836 Å, b = 5.84266 Å, c = 4.88133 Å; α = 91.6533°, β = 92.5329°, γ = 82.7511° | 97.464% |
| WO₃ | P 21/c | a = 7.69040 Å, b = 7.53711 Å, c = 10.52225 Å; β = 136.0585° | 2.536% |

The final whole-pattern fit has Rwp = 5.4808%, Rp = 4.3108%, GOF = 2.2578, and Durbin–Watson = 0.4588 for 8,301 observations and 40 refined parameters.

## Evidence

The CuO + WO₃ starting chemistry and 800 °C treatment make CuWO₄ the expected reaction product. The ambient-type P -1 CuWO₄ model accounts coherently for isolated and overlapping families from low through high angle. Its refined metric differs substantially from the high-pressure literature seed and was determined from this pattern. The alternative P 2/c CuWO₄ record and candidate CuO, Cu₂O, and Cu₂WO₄ phases refined to effectively zero scale.

Residual shoulders near 24.69° and 26.91° 2θ, together with consistent mid- and high-angle families, support minor WO₃. A room-temperature neutron P 21/c model (COD 2311041, expressed in its published β ≈ 136° setting) gives six physically normal W–O bonds per W and fits better than the coarser same-symmetry COD 1528915 model. It also fits better than a triclinic WO₃ alternative despite having fewer cell freedoms.

A final Cu₃WO₆ watch-list replay gave only about 0.05 wt% equivalent scale at 1.5σ, and its strongest unique low-angle family is absent. It was therefore excluded rather than reported as a phase.

## Refinement and physical checks

The Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio were fixed to the metadata. The accepted baseline uses a smooth six-term Chebyshev background, one specimen-height correction (−593.69 µm; zero fixed), nonnegative common profile W with U/V/X/Y/Z fixed at zero, and refined SH/L asymmetry. Isotropic microstrain is replay-stable for both phases; crystallite-size trials were rejected after collapse or unstable replay. Weak [110] CuWO₄ preferred orientation is supported relative to no texture and all tested low-index axial/diagonal alternatives (March–Dollase = 1.0191).

CuWO₄ has no short contacts, with Cu–O = 1.86–2.43 Å and W–O = 1.84–2.29 Å. Cu and O coordinates were refined relative to a fixed W origin; displacement parameters remained fixed because their refinement was unphysical and strongly correlated. WO₃ coordinates were held at the neutron-source values, so this dataset establishes its phase identity, cell, scale, and broadening but does not independently determine its atomic coordinates.

## Remaining uncertainty

The formal GSAS-II fraction uncertainty is about 0.063 percentage point for each normalized fraction, but it excludes structural-record, absorption, preferred-orientation, background, and specimen-geometry systematics. Reasonable profile/source variants explored during model selection moved the WO₃ estimate by roughly 0.7 percentage point, so 2.5 wt% should not be interpreted to formal-ESD precision. The presence of WO₃ is reliable; its exact fraction and microstrain are less certain. A crystalline Cu₃WO₆ contribution below roughly 0.1 wt% cannot be determined reliably from this pattern.
