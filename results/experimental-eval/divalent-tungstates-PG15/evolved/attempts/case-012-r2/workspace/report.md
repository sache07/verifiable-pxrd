# Quantitative crystalline-phase analysis

## Result

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| NiWO4, wolframite | P 2/c (No. 13) | 76.48% |
| WO3, monoclinic gamma form | P 21/c (No. 14, alternate setting) | 23.52% |

Fractions are normalized over the two modeled crystalline phases. The formal GSAS-II standard uncertainty is 0.08 percentage point for either fraction, but model/source choices dominate; a more realistic uncertainty is about ±1 percentage point. No amorphous fraction can be obtained without an internal standard.

The final full-range replay gives Rwp = 4.849%, Rp = 3.821%, Rexp = 3.479%, and GOF = 1.396 for 8282 points. Because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied, count-statistical weights (1/count) were used; formal uncertainties and GOF should therefore not be over-interpreted.

## Evidence and structural choices

Wolframite NiWO4 accounts for the dominant families (including 19.19°, 30.85°, 36.5°, and 54.6° 2theta). Coherent residual families at 23.03–24.27°, 33.2–34.1°, 49.9°, and 55.9° require WO3. Monoclinic room-temperature WO3 records reproduce these families; orthorhombic/hexagonal candidates fail positionally. A triclinic WO3 record gives a nearly indistinguishable small residual improvement, so the subtle monoclinic-versus-triclinic distortion is not reliably resolvable here; the physically expected room-temperature monoclinic model was retained.

The exact older NiWO4 database record has an incomplete oxygen asymmetric unit. A complete isostructural ZnWO4 parent (COD 1520641) was therefore screened with Ni substituted on the octahedral divalent-cation site. It outperformed the MnWO4 analogue and gives physical final geometry (Ni–O 2.064–2.083 Å; W–O 1.785–2.108 Å). Only Ni and W positional coordinates were refined. Oxygen coordinates in NiWO4, and all WO3 atomic coordinates, are source-model values and cannot be determined reliably from this Cu X-ray powder pattern.

NiO was reintroduced under the final common profile as a watch-list phase; its scale collapsed to 10^-12 and its unique 43.3°, 62.9°, and 75.5° families are absent. It was excluded. This rejects crystalline NiO at the sensitivity of this pattern, not every possible trace phase.

## Profile adequacy and remaining uncertainty

The accepted baseline uses a smooth 8-term Chebyshev background, one specimen-displacement correction, nonnegative common W/X broadening (U fixed at its zero bound), fixed minimum SH/L, and one refined effective-polarization term. WO3 alone requires replay-stable isotropic microstrain. Size terms hit bounds, SH/L was 97% correlated with displacement, and tested preferred-orientation axes were not supported coherently across multiple families, so these terms were pruned. A surface-roughness alternative was rejected because it was more scale-correlated and its two-parameter form collapsed to a boundary.

For isolated NiWO4-dominated groups sampled at low, middle, and high angle, observed/calculated centroids agree within 0.006° and local FWHM values within 0.024°. No repeated asymmetry residual remains across those groups. Remaining systematic uncertainty comes mainly from the uncalibrated instrument/intensity correction, fixed light-atom coordinates, WO3 symmetry ambiguity, peak overlap, and normalization only over modeled crystalline material.
