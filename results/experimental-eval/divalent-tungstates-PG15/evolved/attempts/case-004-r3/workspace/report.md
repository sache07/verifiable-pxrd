# Crystalline-phase analysis

The pattern is adequately modeled by two crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over those phases only:

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (Å, °) | Crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Triclinic WO₃ | P -1 (No. 2) | a=7.31065, b=7.53861, c=7.69502; α=89.7691, β=90.8043, γ=90.1361 | 70.28(13)% |
| Wolframite CoWO₄ | P 2/c (No. 13) | a=4.67207, b=5.68635, c=4.95087; β=90.0000 | 29.72(13)% |

The parenthetical uncertainties are covariance-derived statistical standard uncertainties. Because no internal standard, calibrated instrument profile, or pointwise measurement uncertainties were supplied, systematic uncertainty is larger; about 1 wt% absolute is a more realistic precision for comparing these crystalline fractions. An amorphous fraction, if present, cannot be measured from this data set, so the values are not absolute bulk-product fractions.

## Evidence and model selection

Peak positions and coherent families across the full 9.903–100.095° 2θ range support room-temperature triclinic WO₃ and wolframite CoWO₄. The WO₃ model is the room-temperature structure in COD 2106293 (Brandt and Salje, *The crystal structure of triclinic WO3*); under the same provisional nuisance model it outperformed the tested monoclinic WO₃ records. Its refined W–O distances are 1.716–2.219 Å.

The available nominal CoWO₄ record was incomplete for refinement, so chemically and structurally compatible wolframite analogues were screened as requested. The selected parent was FeWO₄ COD 9008124, with the isovalent Fe²⁺→Co²⁺ site substitution; it fit better than the tested CoMoO₄ and alternate FeWO₄ parents. Coordinates were held at the parent values while scales and cells were refined. The final Co–O distances are 2.041–2.172 Å and W–O distances are 1.764–2.115 Å, with no short-contact or occupancy problem. Thus CoWO₄ is reliable as a phase assignment, but its CIF should be treated as a physically screened powder model rather than an independent atomic-structure determination.

CoO and W₁₈O₄₉ both refined to the zero-scale bound when re-challenged under the final common profile. Co₃O₄ gave only 0.18(16) wt% and reduced χ² by about 2.6 for one extra parameter; it lacks sufficient unique-family support and was excluded. Minor phases below roughly 0.5–1 wt% therefore cannot be ruled out reliably.

## Refinement and limitations

The final GSAS-II model uses the reported Cu Kα₁/Kα₂ wavelengths and fixed 0.5 intensity ratio, one zero correction, a common nonnegative W/X profile, supported SH/L asymmetry, and a smooth 12-term Chebyshev-1 background. The polarization factor was tested before texture and fixed at the physically constrained best value of 1.0. Unused size, CoWO₄ strain, roughness, transparency, displacement, extinction, and Babinet terms were held neutral. Replay-stable terms retained after controlled comparisons were WO₃ [111] uniaxial microstrain and weak March–Dollase corrections for WO₃ [101] and CoWO₄ [001]. The SH/L and zero terms are strongly correlated (97.4%), so their individual values and the last digits of the cells should not be overinterpreted.

The saved full-range refinement converged with Rwp=4.883%, Rp=3.764%, GOF=1.492, χ²=18397.9, 8301 observations, and 31 variables. Remaining residuals are dominated by imperfect intensity/shape modeling in overlapping peak groups rather than coherent peaks of a third identified phase. Atomic coordinates and displacement parameters were not refined from this uncalibrated laboratory pattern.
