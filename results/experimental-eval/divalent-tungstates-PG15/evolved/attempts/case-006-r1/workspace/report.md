# Quantitative phase analysis

The pattern is modeled by two crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases; no amorphous content or absolute fraction can be obtained without an external standard.

The covariance-derived 1σ uncertainty is 0.00095 in fraction (0.095 wt%) for each phase. This is only a counting-statistical estimate. Model-choice trials placed WO3 at roughly 2.3–3.2 wt%, and the final dominant-phase scale is 95.1% correlated with the required low-angle roughness correction. A more realistic practical uncertainty for the WO3 amount is therefore about ±1 wt%; the presence and identity of WO3 are more secure than its last decimal place.

## Evidence and model selection

The CuO + WO3 precursor context and the dominant peak set support copper tungstate formation. Exact-formula COD CuWO4 records 4000806 and 4000807 are contracted high-pressure structures and gave systematic peak-position disagreement. The required stoichiometric-analogue search instead selected ambient P -1 COD 2101690, a neutron model for (Cu0.9Zn0.1)WO4. Replacing its single mixed Cu/Zn-site chemistry with fully occupied Cu is a direct isostructural substitution appropriate to the precursor composition. Selective coordinate refinement gave plausible distorted octahedra: Cu-O 1.93–2.42 Å and W-O 1.78–2.23 Å.

After the CuWO4 baseline was rebuilt, coherent residual families near 23–25, 33–34, 41–42, 50, and 56° 2θ were fitted by room-temperature monoclinic WO3, COD 1528915. On the common 10–100° trial range this reduced Rwp from about 7.17% for CuWO4 alone to about 6.47%. The WO3 cell, scale, and isotropic size (about 56 nm) were supported, but its coordinates and positive neutral Uiso values were fixed because a 3 wt% phase cannot support independent atomic refinement reliably.

CuO (C2/c and Cc records) and Cu2O refined to zero or statistically zero scale. Cu2WO4 was below 0.1 wt% with uncertainty larger than its value. Cu3WO6 and extra triclinic/tetragonal WO3 trials returned only about 0.1–0.2 wt%, negligible fit improvement, and no coherent unique peak family after the common profile was rebuilt. They were not retained; trace phases at approximately this level cannot be identified or quantified reliably from this pattern.

## Refinement quality and limitations

The final full-range replay used all 8,301 points from 9.9034 to 100.0949° 2θ and the supplied Cu Kα1/Kα2 doublet. It converged after save/reopen replay with Rwp=6.4772%, Rp=4.7878%, Rexp=2.4280%, goodness of fit=2.6677, and 36 refined parameters. Poisson uncertainties, σ=sqrt(counts), were assumed because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied.

The accepted baseline uses a smooth six-term Chebyshev background, one specimen-displacement correction, one nonnegative common Gaussian width term, polarization, one-dimensional reflection-geometry roughness, and phase size broadening. CuWO4 required uniaxial size broadening (approximately 110–148 nm); WO3 used isotropic size broadening. Microstrain, preferred orientation, extra asymmetry, and additional profile terms were rejected as bound, near-neutral, strongly correlated, or not replay-stable. The background remains positive and smooth.

Residuals are correlated (Durbin-Watson 0.379), especially around intense low-angle CuWO4 peaks, so the formal goodness of fit and covariance errors should not be read as high-precision structural uncertainties. The CuWO4 CIF is a restrained powder-refined model with only supported oxygen-coordinate components released, not an independent high-precision structure determination. The phase identities are reliable, the WO3 fraction is semiquantitative at the uncertainty stated above, and amorphous material cannot be assessed.
