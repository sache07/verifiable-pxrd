## Evidence

The chemistry (PbO + WO3 heated at 500 °C) and peak families both support dominant scheelite-type PbWO4, with its characteristic reflections near 27.7, 29.9, 33.0, 45.0, 47.3, and 55.5° 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 is required by the resolved low-angle family near 23.4–24.6° and many higher-angle lines. Orthorhombic massicot is supported by families near 29.3, 30.5, 32.8, 38.0, 45.3, 49.4–51.0, and 60.5°. The long-period alpha-PbO model accounts for superstructure/fundamental families near 17.9, 25.9, 32.1, 36.0, 48.9, and 55.1°. A weak conventional litharge component improves coherent families near 32.1, 36.0, 48.9, and 59.5° after its cell is released.

Exact-composition, fully occupied COD records were used: 9009811, 2310433, 1533609, 2311041, and 9012700, respectively. The P4/nmm origin-choice conversion for COD 9012700 was corrected to the equivalent Pb 2c and O 2a sites before refinement; the submitted CIF is stoichiometric PbO. Exported structures pass composition and short-contact checks (minimum W–O/Pb–O distances 1.72–2.31 Å). Atomic coordinates and occupancies were held at the source values; only cells, scales, and justified profile terms were refined.

The disordered Pb7W8O28.8 record refined to zero scale. Raspite replayed at only 0.52(23)% and did not improve coherent unique families, so it was not included. Alternate PbWO4, PbO, and WO3 records/polymorphs gave poorer or non-identifiable fits under the same nuisance model.

## Refinement and fit

The final full-range GSAS-II replay uses Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths 1.540598/1.544426 Å with the reported 0.5 ratio, an eight-coefficient smooth Chebyshev background, zero offset 0.2694°, refined polarization 0.7484, and a common nonnegative W/X profile (15.64/12.61; U, V, Y, and Z fixed at zero). SH/L, roughness, specimen shift, transparency, and unsupported size terms were pruned.

Massicot required anisotropic Gaussian microstrain (about 1.18% equatorial and 6.11% axial for a [100] model) and March–Dollase texture along [011] (3.32). The complementary [011] strain-axis model gave nearly the same fit, so the existence of family-dependent broadening is reliable but its direction is not unique. The modulated PbO approximant required about 2.03% isotropic Gaussian microstrain. These large values should be regarded as empirical peak-shape descriptors, not precise intrinsic microstrain measurements.

Final statistics are Rwp = 13.000%, Rp = 9.053%, Rexp = 3.502%, and goodness-of-fit = 3.713 over 8,282 points. Since measured point uncertainties were unavailable, sigma = sqrt(counts) was used; the elevated goodness-of-fit and correlated residuals show that statistical uncertainties understate systematic error.

## Remaining uncertainty

The dominant PbWO4 and WO3 assignments are robust. Massicot abundance is profile/texture sensitive; a practical model uncertainty is several weight-percent, larger than its formal 0.15% error. Modulated alpha-PbO and litharge share fundamental reflections and their refined scales are strongly anticorrelated (correlation about -0.89). Their individual fractions therefore cannot be considered highly reliable; the combined alpha-PbO-like amount, about 7.75 wt%, is better determined, while the litharge fraction should be viewed as approximately 0–2 wt%. The long-period approximant establishes a modulated PbO-like contribution, but this laboratory pattern cannot determine the modulation or atomic displacements independently. Unmodeled crystalline phases below roughly 0.5–1 wt% and amorphous material cannot be excluded.
