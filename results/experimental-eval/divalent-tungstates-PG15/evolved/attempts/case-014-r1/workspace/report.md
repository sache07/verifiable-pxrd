# PXRD phase analysis and refinement

The best residual-led crystalline model contains five phases. Fractions are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases only; amorphous or otherwise unmodeled material is not included.

The uncertainties in parentheses are GSAS-II least-squares e.s.d.s. They do not include phase-model, background, absorption, or specimen-preparation systematics and are especially optimistic for the two provisional ternary phases.

## Evidence and model selection

Scheelite SrWO4 accounts for the dominant families across the full range, including isolated low-, middle-, and high-angle reflections near 18.29, 45.42, and 72.98 degrees 2theta. Its oxygen coordinate was refined; the resulting W-O distance is 1.752 Å and the tetrahedral geometry is chemically plausible. Ambient monoclinic WO3 reproduces the characteristic 23.44/23.91/24.68-degree cluster and additional families through high angle. Alternative triclinic, orthorhombic, monoclinic, and tetragonal WO3 records were tested under the same nuisance model; the selected P21/c record gave the most coherent physical ambient model. A stable isotropic WO3 microstrain of about 1.7(5)x10^3 microstrain was retained.

Two independent residual families require Sr-rich ternary models. The exact-composition C2/c Sr3W2O9 record supplies coherent lines near 32.60, 44.66, and 48.62 degrees. A separate family near 18.1, 29.75, 42.15, 52, and 61.2 degrees is best indexed by an Sr3WO6-type cell. No exact Sr3WO6 record was available, so cross-chemistry prototypes were screened. The submitted model uses the newer centrosymmetric Sr3UO6 P21/c parent with the isovalent U(VI)-to-W(VI) substitution. Its final W-O distances are 2.080-2.089 Å. An older P21 Sr3UO6 analogue lowered Rwp further but left unphysical 2.34-2.42 Å W-O contacts and was rejected; Ca3WO6 and Ba3WO6 parents were also inferior or disordered.

SrO remains at nonzero scale and is supported by partly isolated families near 35.23, 50.50, 59.95, 62.89, and 81.9 degrees. Residual SrCO3 was re-tested because it was a precursor, but its strongest isolated model line near 25.56 degrees is absent; it was therefore rejected despite a nominal nonzero trial scale. A fergusonite SrWO4 trial collapsed to zero scale.

## Profile and refinement checks

The model uses the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and fixed 0.5 intensity ratio, one refined zero correction, a smooth six-coefficient Chebyshev background, positive common W/X profile terms, fixed U=V=0, fixed SH/L=0.002, and one physical surface-roughness correction. Isolated dominant-phase peaks at low, middle, and high angle show consistent positions, widths, and asymmetry. Phase size trials either returned to the 10 micrometre neutral limit or collapsed to a bound. Additional phase strains, preferred-orientation axes, polarization, SH/L, and U trials were rejected when they were bound, unphysical, unstable on replay, or unsupported by multiple isolated families. The final project was saved, reopened, and replayed over 10.02-99.98 degrees before export; its maximum shift/e.s.d. was 0.053.

Final statistics are Rwp = 14.665%, Rp = 10.142%, Rexp = 3.535%, and GOF = 4.157 for 8280 fitted points and 34 refined parameters. The smooth baseline and main-phase peak shapes are adequate, but coherent intensity residuals remain around several minor-phase peaks. Raw counts had no supplied uncertainties, so counting-statistics weights make these systematic residuals prominent.

## Remaining uncertainty

The Sr3W2O9 assignment is not uniquely reliable: its database structure was obtained from high-pressure synthesis, while this specimen was heated at ambient pressure, and its refined beta angle (101.29 degrees) differs materially from the source value (103.50 degrees). The Sr3WO6 assignment is likewise a physically screened cross-chemistry prototype rather than an exact entry, and its atomic coordinates could not be refined reliably from its weak, overlapped contribution. Their listed fractions are the best supported values within this five-phase model, but a unique structure and fully reliable fraction for either phase cannot be claimed from this pattern alone. The approximately 1 wt% SrO fraction is also near the practical minor-phase limit. Additional high-resolution data or phase-selective chemical analysis would be needed to resolve these ambiguities.
