# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is best described by three crystalline components: tetragonal scheelite SrWO4 (77.07 wt%), monoclinic WO3 (11.08 wt%), and a monoclinic fergusonite-type SrWO4 component (11.85 wt%). Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases; amorphous material, if present, is not quantified.

## Evidence and refinement

The dominant lines at 18.33, 27.99, 30.22, 33.39, 45.47, 51.98, and 56.08 degrees 2theta index as scheelite SrWO4 (I 41/a). The weak triplet at approximately 23.46, 23.97, and 24.73 degrees, together with its higher-angle lines, identifies room-temperature monoclinic WO3 (P 21/n). After these two phases were fitted, a coherent residual family at approximately 29.75, 32.60, 44.66, and 48.62 degrees matched monoclinic fergusonite SrWO4 (I 2/a); adding this phase reduced Rwp from 19.00% to 15.69%.

Starting atomic models were COD 1546036, 1528915, and 1546037. The unit cells, phase scales, zero offset, common profile terms, and a 10-term Chebyshev background were refined against the original count data using the supplied Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. Literature atomic coordinates were retained. Trials with preferred orientation and phase-specific size broadening did not improve the fit. A free scheelite oxygen-coordinate trial lowered Rwp slightly but gave an implausibly long W-O distance, so it was rejected.

Final fit statistics are Rwp = 15.688%, Rp = 11.786%, and GOF = 4.444. The refinement converged with maximum shift/esd = 0.050.

## Remaining uncertainty

The scheelite and monoclinic WO3 assignments are strong. The fergusonite match is diffraction-based and internally consistent, but this polymorph is usually pressure-stabilized and no high-pressure synthesis context was provided. It may therefore represent a closely related low-symmetry Sr-W-O phase rather than unambiguous ambient fergusonite. Several modest residual intensity mismatches remain, and the instrument profile was not calibrated independently. Formal fraction standard uncertainties are about 0.2-0.3 wt%, but model/profile systematics are larger; a practical uncertainty of roughly 2-3 wt% absolute should be attached to the minor-phase fractions. No SrCO3 phase was included because its strongest expected reflections near 25.3 and 25.9 degrees are absent.
