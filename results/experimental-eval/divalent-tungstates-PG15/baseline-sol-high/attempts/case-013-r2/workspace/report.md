# Powder diffraction phase analysis

The supported crystalline model contains three phases (fractions normalized only over these modeled crystalline phases):

- PbWO4, scheelite/stolzite type, I 41/a: **77.27 wt%**
- WO3, monoclinic, P 21/c: **11.57 wt%**
- PbO, massicot, P b c m: **11.15 wt%**

## Evidence

The dominant scheelite PbWO4 assignment is fixed by its complete strong-line sequence at approximately 27.70, 29.91, 33.06, 45.03, 47.28, 51.49, 55.54, and 56.82 degrees 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the diagnostic low-angle group near 23.39, 23.89, and 24.64 degrees and lines near 26.77, 34.43, and 41.91 degrees. Orthorhombic massicot PbO accounts for the residual family near 29.35, 30.62, 38.09, and related higher-angle features. Trial models containing raspite PbWO4, tetragonal litharge PbO, or the older P 21/m WO3 model did not improve the complete multiphase fit; the raspite scale became effectively zero once monoclinic WO3 and massicot were included.

The final GSAS-II fit over 10.0094-99.9944 degrees gives Rwp = 18.33%, Rp = 13.43%, Rexp = 3.50%, and goodness-of-fit = 5.24. A 2theta zero offset was refined because no calibrated instrument profile was supplied. Background, phase scales, unit cells, zero, and two profile coefficients were refined; atomic coordinates were held to the reference structures and a common fixed Uiso = 0.01 A^2 was used.

## Remaining uncertainty

Residual Bragg intensity remains, most clearly at about 25.91 degrees and across 30-31 degrees. Chemistry permits a Pb-rich ternary intermediate (for example Pb2WO5), but no available structural candidate gave a defensible Rietveld model, so no CIF or fraction is reported for it. The reported fractions therefore exclude any unidentified crystalline material and any amorphous content. The formal least-squares fraction uncertainties are only about 0.2-0.4 wt%, but they do not include this model uncertainty; the practical uncertainty is several wt%. The comparatively high goodness-of-fit and phase Bragg R factors for the minor phases reinforce that the minor-phase fractions are semiquantitative rather than high-accuracy assay values.
