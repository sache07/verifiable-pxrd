# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is modeled as a single crystalline phase: monoclinic wolframite-type **MnWO4**, space group **P 2/c** (No. 13). Its reported fraction is **100 wt% of the modeled crystalline phases**. This normalization does not determine an absolute crystalline yield or exclude amorphous material.

## Evidence

The MnWO4 model accounts for the complete diagnostic peak sequence, including the low-angle reflections near 15.7 and 18.7 degrees, the pair near 23.9/24.4 degrees, the intense split pair near 30.1/30.6 degrees, and the many resolved reflections through 100 degrees. This matches the MnO + WO3 precursor chemistry and the expected solid-state product at 900 degrees C. Candidate residual MnO, WO3, and Mn3WO6 structures were rejected because their strongest unique reflections (for example MnO near 35.2 degrees, monoclinic WO3 near 26.3/26.8 degrees, and Mn3WO6 near 20.0/20.5 degrees) are absent. Residual features occur mainly on the flanks of MnWO4 peaks and are consistent with imperfect peak-shape modeling rather than a coherent second-phase pattern.

The starting structure was COD 1520200 and was refined in GSAS-II against 10-100 degrees two-theta. The final cell is a = 4.83433, b = 5.76044, c = 4.99944 angstrom, beta = 91.21999 degrees, V = 139.19194 angstrom^3. Cell, zero offset, scale, background, constrained profile terms, effective polarization, and atomic coordinates were refined; occupancies and displacement parameters were held to the reference structure. The metadata says K-alpha1 was intended, so the calculation used lambda = 1.540598 angstrom. Although source K-alpha2 information is also listed in the metadata, it was not included in the monochromated profile model.

## Fit and uncertainty

The converged fit gives Rwp = 9.110%, Rp = 6.597%, RBragg = 8.368%, RF = 2.954%, and goodness of fit = 3.308 using Poisson counting uncertainties inferred as sqrt(counts), since pointwise uncertainties were not supplied. The final maximum shift/esd was 0.054.

No second crystalline phase is supported reliably. The one-phase fraction is therefore exactly 1.0 only on the requested modeled-crystalline normalization; it has no meaningful independent standard uncertainty. Trace phases at roughly the few-weight-percent level, strongly overlapping phases, and amorphous content cannot be excluded or quantified from this pattern without an internal standard and a calibrated instrumental profile. The refined oxygen coordinates and cell/zero-offset separation also carry systematic uncertainty because the instrumental profile and effective polarization were not independently calibrated.
