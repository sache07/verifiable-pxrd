# Phase analysis report

The pattern is modeled as **monoclinic WO3** (space group P 21/c; 71.3 wt%) and **cubic Co3O4 spinel** (space group F d -3 m; 28.7 wt%). Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases. The final GSAS-II Rietveld fit gives Rwp = 3.61%, Rp = 2.59%, Rexp = 1.72%, and goodness of fit = 2.10.

## Evidence

The three dominant low-angle peaks at about 23.46, 23.95, and 24.71 degrees 2theta, together with the split groups near 33.6--34.5 degrees and the higher-angle WO3 lines, require the room-temperature monoclinic WO3 structure. Co3O4 is independently identified by its spinel sequence near 19.37, 31.63, 37.20, 45.15, 59.67, and 65.55 degrees. A cobalt tungstate model was screened but rejected: its characteristic strongest pair near 30--31 degrees and associated wolframite reflections are absent. No coherent set of residual peaks requires a third crystalline phase.

Refined cells are WO3: a = 7.69802 A, b = 7.54034 A, c = 10.55229 A, beta = 136.1624 degrees (the valid P21/c literature setting used by the source model); and Co3O4: a = 8.08767 A. The fitted zero offset is 0.3479 degrees 2theta.

## Remaining uncertainty

The counting uncertainties were not supplied, so sqrt(counts) was used. The instrument profile was also not calibrated; a compact physically positive profile model was refined while atomic coordinates and occupancies were retained from literature structures (COD 2311041 for WO3 and COD 9005896 for Co3O4). The formal fraction standard uncertainty is about 0.18 wt%, but profile and closely related WO3-model tests shift the result by roughly 1--1.5 wt%; a realistic estimate is therefore **WO3 71.3 +/- 1.5 wt% and Co3O4 28.7 +/- 1.5 wt%**. Amorphous material cannot be quantified without an internal standard, so the fractions apply only to modeled crystalline material.
