# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is best described by two crystalline phases:

- Monoclinic WO3 (P 1 21/n 1): **45.17 wt%** of the modeled crystalline material.
- Witherite BaCO3 (P m c n): **54.83 wt%** of the modeled crystalline material.

The fractions are normalized over the modeled crystalline phases. GSAS-II gives a formal one-standard-deviation uncertainty of about 0.15 wt% for each fraction, but a more realistic uncertainty is approximately 1–2 wt% because no calibrated instrumental profile or internal standard was supplied and preferred-orientation/microabsorption systematics cannot be excluded. Amorphous content, if present, is not quantified.

## Evidence

The monoclinic WO3 model accounts for the characteristic 23.4–24.7° multiplet and the strong groups near 33.6–34.9°, 42.3°, 50.2°, and 55–56°. Orthorhombic BaCO3 is required for the additional intensity near 19.77°, 24.21°, 28.04°, 45.18°, and related higher-angle lines. This is chemically consistent with the BaCO3/WO3 precursor context and the relatively low reported heating temperature of 400 °C.

Literature structures COD 1528915 (WO3) and COD 9010928 (BaCO3) were used as starting models. Unit-cell parameters, phase scales, background, zero offset, and a conservative U/W/X profile were refined over 9.903–100.095° 2theta. Atomic coordinates were retained at their literature values because this laboratory powder pattern does not constrain them independently. The final full-range fit has Rwp = 8.71%, Rp = 6.56%, and goodness of fit = 2.50.

## Remaining uncertainty

Scheelite BaWO4 was explicitly tested. Adding it produced a nominal 0.62 wt% and changed Rwp only from 8.70% to 8.62% in the 10–90° screening fit. Its strongest relatively independent reflection near 32.2° is not supported and is over-calculated by the added model; the apparent gain comes mainly from overlaps with the two major phases. BaWO4 is therefore not reported as a determined phase. A trace amount at roughly the 1 wt% level or below cannot be ruled out reliably.

The elevated goodness of fit relative to counting statistics indicates residual profile/model systematic error, so the formal fraction uncertainties should not be interpreted as total uncertainty.
