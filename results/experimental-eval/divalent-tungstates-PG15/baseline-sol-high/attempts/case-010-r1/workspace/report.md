# PXRD phase analysis

The crystalline sample is modeled as **72.52 wt% monoclinic WO3** and **27.48 wt% cubic bixbyite Mn2O3**, normalized over the two modeled crystalline phases. No manganese tungstate phase was required by the pattern.

## Evidence

- Monoclinic WO3 (space group P 21/c; starting model COD 2311041) accounts for the characteristic strong triplet at 23.43, 23.91, and 24.68 degrees 2theta and the dense higher-angle reflection sequence. Its refined nonstandard-setting cell is a = 7.6962, b = 7.5398, c = 10.5512 A, beta = 136.1667 degrees, V = 424.025 A3.
- Bixbyite Mn2O3 (space group Ia-3; starting model COD 1514230) is independently supported by peaks at 33.27, 38.53, 45.45, 49.60, 55.49, and 66.03 degrees 2theta. Its refined cubic cell is a = 9.4178 A, V = 835.299 A3.
- Candidate MnWO4 and Mn3WO6 structures gave poor database-screen matches, and their diagnostic reflections were absent. After adding Mn2O3, the principal previously unmodeled peaks disappeared from the difference profile.
- The final two-phase GSAS-II refinement gives Rp = 5.97%, Rwp = 8.47%, Rexp = 2.66%, and goodness of fit = 3.19. A common zero offset of +0.3031 degrees was refined, consistent across both phases.

## Quantification and uncertainty

The GSAS-II mass fractions are 0.7252(23) WO3 and 0.2748(23) Mn2O3; the parenthesized values are covariance-derived one-standard-uncertainty estimates. They are likely too optimistic because no calibrated instrument profile or internal standard was supplied and the remaining residuals at strong WO3 peaks indicate intensity/profile systematics. A practical uncertainty of roughly 1-3 wt% absolute is more realistic. Fractions exclude amorphous material, which cannot be quantified from these data alone.

Lattice parameters and phase identities are reliable. Atomic coordinates were retained from the reference structures rather than freely refined because laboratory powder data without geometric restraints do not determine all coordinates robustly, especially oxygen positions. Consequently, the CIFs are phase models suitable for the reported Rietveld fit, not new high-precision structure determinations.
