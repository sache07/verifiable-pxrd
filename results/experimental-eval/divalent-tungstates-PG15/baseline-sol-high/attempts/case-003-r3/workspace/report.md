# Quantitative phase analysis

The crystalline pattern is best described by three phases: monoclinic WO3, calcite CaCO3, and scheelite CaWO4. The GSAS-II quantitative result, normalized over these modeled crystalline phases, is:

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction | Refined cell (angstrom, degrees) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| WO3 | P 21/n | 0.5435 (formal s.u. 0.0021) | a=7.30922, b=7.54145, c=7.69639, beta=90.7183 |
| CaCO3 (calcite) | R -3 c | 0.3372 (formal s.u. 0.0028) | a=4.99047, c=17.07617 |
| CaWO4 (scheelite) | I 41/a | 0.1193 (formal s.u. 0.0015) | a=5.24426, c=11.38861 |

The fractions sum to 1.0000 and describe only the modeled crystalline material. An amorphous fraction cannot be measured without an internal standard.

## Evidence and refinement

The WO3 assignment is driven by the intense low-symmetry triplet at observed 2theta approximately 23.42, 23.90, and 24.67 degrees and the associated groups near 33.6--34.5, 41.9--42.1, and 50.2 degrees. The splitting pattern selects the monoclinic room-temperature WO3 model over the tested tetragonal, hexagonal, and orthorhombic alternatives. Calcite is independently identified by its lines near 29.71, 39.72, 43.47, 47.52/48.78, and 57.68 degrees. Scheelite CaWO4 is required by the lines near 18.89, 29.02, 31.72, and its higher-angle series. These assignments are chemically consistent with partial reaction of the stated CaCO3 and WO3 precursors after heating at 500 degrees C.

Reference structures from COD entries 1528915 (WO3), 1547347 (calcite), and 7219229 (scheelite) were refined against the original counts from 10 to 100 degrees 2theta. The GSAS-II model used the reported Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and fixed 0.5 intensity ratio. Refined terms were phase scales and cells, a 15-term Chebyshev background, zero offset, global profile coefficients, and isotropic coherent-domain broadening for WO3 and CaWO4. The final fit has Rwp=10.46%, Rp=7.66%, and goodness-of-fit=4.38. The final least-squares cycle converged without singular parameters.

## Remaining uncertainty

The phase identities and the conclusion that unreacted WO3 and CaCO3 dominate are reliable. The exact low-symmetry description of WO3 is less definitive: a very small triclinic distortion is difficult to distinguish from monoclinic P 21/n in this broadened, overlapping laboratory pattern. No atomic coordinates or displacement parameters were refined; the powder data, especially for oxygen in the W-rich phases, do not determine them reliably, so those values remain those of the reference structures while the unit cells were refined.

The formal fraction s.u. values above are least-squares precision only. The uncalibrated instrumental profile, the relatively large refined zero offset (about 0.30 degrees), peak-shape residuals around the strongest overlapping WO3 lines, and possible microabsorption/preferred-orientation effects imply a more realistic compositional uncertainty of a few weight percent. Trace phases below roughly 1--2 wt% could be hidden by overlap and background; no additional phase is supported strongly enough to quantify.
