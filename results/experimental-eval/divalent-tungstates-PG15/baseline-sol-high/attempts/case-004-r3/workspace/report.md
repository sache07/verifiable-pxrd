# PXRD phase analysis

The crystalline pattern is modeled by two phases: monoclinic WO3 (66.63 wt%) and wolframite-type CoWO4 (33.37 wt%), normalized over the modeled crystalline material. This is chemically consistent with partial reaction of the stated CoO and WO3 precursors at 900 °C, leaving substantial unreacted WO3.

## Evidence

Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the intense diagnostic triplet at 23.18, 23.67, and 24.42° 2θ and the dense groups near 33–34° and 50–56°. CoWO4 accounts for distinct reflections including those near 19.09, 30.71, 36.44, and 41.96° and improves the fit throughout the high-angle range. A CoO phase was not required: its expected strongest lines are not present as independent residual peaks.

The final GSAS-II Rietveld refinement used Cu Kα1/Kα2 radiation as specified in the metadata, a 12-term Chebyshev background, refined phase cells, zero offset, Caglioti profile terms, overall intensity scale, and the WO3/CoWO4 scale ratio. Atomic coordinates and occupancies were held at reference-structure values because the laboratory powder data do not support stable independent refinement of all coordinates. The final statistics are Rwp = 7.05%, Rp = 5.34%, Rexp = 3.28%, and GOF = 2.15 for 8,284 observations. Refined cells are:

- CoWO4, P2/c: a = 4.66828 Å, b = 5.68349 Å, c = 4.94933 Å, β = 90.0224°.
- WO3, P21/c (nonstandard but valid cell setting): a = 7.69243 Å, b = 7.53647 Å, c = 10.53655 Å, β = 136.1005°.

Monoclinic WO3 was preferred over a tested triclinic WO3 model: the monoclinic model gave the lower Rwp despite fewer cell parameters (7.05% versus 7.87%). Two monoclinic reference descriptions were also checked; the retained neutron-derived P21/c structure gave the better intensity fit.

## Uncertainty

The GSAS-II covariance result gives a formal fraction standard uncertainty of about 0.16 wt% for each phase, but this is an underestimate of total uncertainty. The uncalibrated instrumental profile, strong correlation between two width terms, fixed atomic coordinates, and possible preferred orientation or amorphous material introduce systematic uncertainty not measurable from this scan alone. A practical interpretation is therefore approximately 33 ± 2 wt% CoWO4 and 67 ± 2 wt% WO3 among crystalline phases. Amorphous content and absolute whole-sample fractions cannot be determined reliably without an internal standard. No additional crystalline phase is supported above the residual/background level.
