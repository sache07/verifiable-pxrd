# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The modeled crystalline material is **triclinic WO3 (P -1)** at **65.50 wt%** and **tetragonal BaO2 (I 4/m m m)** at **34.50 wt%**, normalized over those two crystalline phases. The final full-range Rietveld fit has Rwp = 7.66%, Rp = 5.70%, Rexp = 3.64%, and GOF = 2.10.

## Evidence

The WO3 model accounts for the diagnostic low-angle triplet at approximately 23.42, 23.92, and 24.66 degrees 2theta and its repeated split-peak families near 33.5-34.5, 42, 47-50, and 55-56 degrees. BaO2 is independently required by the peaks near 26.33/27.07, 33.54, 42.98, 46.64, and 55.86 degrees. A BaWO4 product is not supported: its characteristic 31.85-degree reflection is absent above the fitted background, and adding product-like peak positions is not needed to explain the profile.

The room-temperature monoclinic WO3 models are close alternatives because WO3 polymorphs are pseudosymmetrically related. Under the same physically constrained profile treatment, the best monoclinic P21/c model gave Rwp = 8.59%, versus 7.66% for P -1, and the triclinic model better reproduced the observed splitting. Higher-symmetry tetragonal/orthorhombic WO3 models fit substantially worse. This supports P -1, but the exact WO3 symmetry remains the principal structural uncertainty.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The GSAS-II model used Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths 1.540598/1.544426 A with the reported 0.5 intensity ratio. A 12-term Chebyshev background, zero offset, lattice parameters, phase scales, one angle-dependent Gaussian width term, and isotropic crystallite-size terms were refined. A positive fixed baseline instrumental width was retained because no calibrated profile was supplied. Refined apparent sizes are about 0.15 micrometers for WO3 and 0.10 micrometers for BaO2; these are profile parameters, not robust particle-size measurements.

Atomic coordinates were retained from COD 2106293 (WO3) and COD 1010113 (BaO2); the submitted CIFs contain the refined cells and those reference coordinates. The data do not justify an unconstrained atomic-coordinate refinement. Formal fraction e.s.d.'s are about 0.15 percentage points, but this understates uncertainty because pointwise errors and a calibrated instrumental profile were unavailable. A practical model-dependent uncertainty is several percentage points. Fractions exclude any amorphous content, which cannot be quantified without an internal standard.
