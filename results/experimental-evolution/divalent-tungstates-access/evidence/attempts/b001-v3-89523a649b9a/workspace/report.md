## Evidence

The dominant triclinic CuWO₄ model accounts for the split reflections near 18.81, 25.46, 29.09–30.08, 31.48, and 35.62–36.53° 2θ. Cubic Cu₃WO₆ is required by a separate coherent family including 15.18, 19.79, 32.49, 33.77, 49.69, 57.61, and 63.28°. Monoclinic WO₃ accounts for the characteristic 22.67/23.07/23.89° cluster and its higher-angle partners. After those three phases were refined, the remaining family at approximately 35.09, 38.31, 48.44, 53.04, 58.11, 61.21, and 67.67° indexed consistently as tenorite CuO. Adding CuO reduced Rwp from about 3.89% to 2.07% at that stage, and its scale remained nonzero through all later refinements.

Reference structures were adapted by staged cell, heavy-atom, oxygen-coordinate, displacement, and profile refinement. Distinct WO₃ coordinate starts were compared; the submitted P 21/c start gave the better comparable fit. Coordinate changes were retained only with physical geometry. In the final structures, the shortest WO₃ W–O and O–O distances are 1.727 and 2.477 Å, respectively. No preferred-orientation term was retained; a WO₃ uniaxial microstrain term was supported. Once all coherent residual families indexed to these four phases, no further phase was added.

The final full-grid replay from `final.gpx`, regenerated from the saved atoms rather than cached intensities, gives Rwp = 1.7271%, Rp = 1.2766%, Rexp = 1.6123%, and GOF = 1.0712. The weighted residual RMS is 1.0686 counting-noise units. The replay has a positive background and no negative-width, displacement-tensor, or polarization review flags. The phase amounts also reconstruct Cu:W = 1.006:1 on a molar basis, independently agreeing with the stated 1:1 precursor dosing.

## Remaining uncertainty

The phase identities, framework symmetries, and presence of the 2.43 wt% CuO component are supported by distinct reflection families. Practical systematic uncertainty is larger than the least-squares scale errors: approximately 1–2 absolute wt% for the major phases and about 0.5 wt% for CuO is a defensible range because profile overlap, specimen effects, and reference-coordinate choice are correlated. The fractions do not measure amorphous material or any phase below the diffraction detection limit.

The WO₃ topology, cell, and W sublattice are reliable, but individual oxygen coordinates are the least well constrained structural parameters because WO₃ is a minority phase with extensive overlap; they should not be treated as a high-precision single-crystal determination. Angular zero and specimen displacement are also strongly correlated and cannot be separated reliably from this scan alone. No additional crystalline structure or fraction remains explicitly undetermined by a coherent residual reflection family.
