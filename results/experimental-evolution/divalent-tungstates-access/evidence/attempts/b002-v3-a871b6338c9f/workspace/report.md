# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is described by a partially reacted ZnO–WO₃ mixture containing wolframite ZnWO₄ as the major product and residual monoclinic WO₃ and wurtzite ZnO.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (Å, °) | Crystalline-normalized wt.% |
|---|---|---|---:|
| ZnWO₄, wolframite | P 2/c | a = 4.70324, b = 5.73446, c = 4.93459, β = 90.6228 | 81.39 |
| WO₃, monoclinic | P 2₁/c | a = 7.71078, b = 7.52635, c = 10.53570, β = 136.0348 | 12.06 |
| ZnO, wurtzite | P 6₃mc | a = 3.25618, c = 5.18608 | 6.56 |

The fractions were calculated from the refined GSAS-II phase scales multiplied by unit-cell mass and normalized over these three modeled crystalline phases.

## Evidence and model selection

The dominant reflection family, including the lines near 15.81, 19.23, 24.14/24.83, and the split 30.75/31.00° region, indexes as monoclinic wolframite ZnWO₄. This is also the chemically economical product of the supplied 1:1 ZnO + WO₃ context. Because an exact ZnWO₄ reference was withheld, the atomic model was transferred from a high-quality P 2/c wolframite analogue by Co→Zn and Mo→W substitution, then refined. Alternative wolframite-related coordinate starts were tested with comparable cell, coordinate, profile, strain, and texture relaxation and fit less well.

A separate narrow family at approximately 23.43, 23.99/24.69, 29.24, and 33.61° requires monoclinic WO₃. A clean P 2₁/c WO₃ model fit better than triclinic alternatives, which relaxed toward a monoclinic metric. Wurtzite ZnO has a stable positive scale and accounts for its characteristic 32.07, 34.92, and 36.59° family plus corresponding high-angle lines. Together these minor phases are consistent with residual unreacted precursors after firing at 700 °C.

After refinement, the largest coherent residual maxima (for example near 24.82, 30.71/31.00, 41.56, 54.15, 61.94, and 65.19°) coincide with allowed reflections of the accepted phases. They are intensity/width errors at existing reflections rather than a new reflection family, so no fourth phase was added.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The final project uses the reported Cu Kα₁/Kα₂ doublet and all 8301 measured points. Refined specimen displacement is −661 µm. ZnWO₄ required [111] March–Dollase texture (ratio 0.711), [100] uniaxial microstrain, and an isotropic size of 0.088 µm; these physical profile terms reproduced their gains on a fresh full-grid replay. The final replay gives Rwp = 10.082%, Rp = 5.952%, Rexp = 2.641%, and goodness of fit = 3.82.

Phase identities and the three-phase inventory are well supported, but some model details are not independently reliable. In particular, oxygen coordinates in transferred ZnWO₄, all atomic coordinates of the weak WO₃ component, and the ZnO internal oxygen coordinate are only weakly constrained by this X-ray pattern. The ZnO oxygen coordinate was fixed at the conservative bound used in the staged trial after unconstrained refinement tried to move beyond it; it should not be treated as a precise structure determination. ZnO peak broadening also reached the lower supported crystallite-size limit. Formal least-squares fraction uncertainties are only about 0.1–0.2 wt.%, but they omit correlations with texture, broadening, displacement parameters, and structural-model bias; the true systematic uncertainty is larger, especially for ZnO. No amorphous fraction can be measured without an internal standard, so the reported values are not absolute whole-sample fractions.
