# Powder XRD phase analysis

The crystalline pattern is modeled by three phases: 61.65 wt% wolframite MnWO4, 19.35 wt% rhombohedral Mn3WO6, and 19.00 wt% monoclinic WO3. Fractions are normalized only over these modeled crystalline phases.

MnWO4 accounts for the dominant families at approximately 15.26, 18.25, 23.46, 29.78, 30.23, 37.23, 40.95, and 53.35 degrees 2theta. Mn3WO6 is required by the paired reflections near 19.67/20.20 degrees and their repeats near 28.48, 35.03, 45.75/46.43, and 54.75 degrees. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the residual precursor family near 22.89, 24.17, 26.41, 33.05/33.40, and higher angles. This inventory is chemically consistent with a 1:1 Mn:W charge: Mn-rich Mn3WO6 is accompanied by residual WO3, while MnWO4 is the principal product. The refined fractions imply an overall crystalline Mn:W molar ratio of 1.015. After inclusion of these phases, positive residuals remain centered on accepted reflections rather than forming a coherent unindexed family.

The models began from frozen COD references 1520200 (MnWO4), 2002703 (Mn3WO6), and 2311041 (WO3). Alternative exact-composition WO3 checks favored the physical P21/c model. A triclinic P-1 model reached a close Rwp but required cell-shear and Uiso limits; two higher-symmetry trials failed numerically and are therefore unknown rather than disproved. March-Dollase corrections along MnWO4 [111], Mn3WO6 [110], and WO3 [010] were supported by broad reflection-family improvements. A surface-roughness branch was rejected because its tiny gain required boundary values.

The final project is the converged, geometry-audited heavy/light branch from the prescribed staged polish. It has no coordinate-bound atoms, catastrophic contacts, negative background, or replay review flags. A fresh parameter-free GSAS-II replay gives Rwp = 3.6309%, Rp = 2.6144%, and goodness of fit = 2.5495 on all 8,301 points.

The phase identities are strong, but the quoted covariance-only fraction errors are unrealistically small. Preferred orientation, profile/Uiso correlation, and overlap imply a more defensible systematic uncertainty of roughly 2–3 wt% absolute per phase. Oxygen coordinates, especially in WO3 and Mn3WO6, are less reliably determined than the heavy-atom frameworks; bound-seeking oxygen shifts were rolled back during polishing. No amorphous fraction or absolute crystalline yield can be obtained from this scan alone.
