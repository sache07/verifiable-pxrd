# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases, normalized to the modeled crystalline material:

| Phase | Structural assignment | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| ZnWO4 | Wolframite, P2/c | 84.752(280) wt% |
| ZnO | Wurtzite, P63mc | 4.992(265) wt% |
| WO3 | Triclinic P-1 model | 10.256(138) wt% |

The parent chemistry (WO3 + ZnO heated at 700 °C) and the diffraction evidence both support ZnWO4 as the reaction product with residual precursor oxides. The refined phase inventory has a Zn:W molar ratio of about 1.05. A transferred wolframite model was the only tested ZnWO4 topology to retain a stable nonzero scale; scheelite, fergusonite, C2/m, C2/c, and alternative triclinic ABX4 controls collapsed toward zero scale. Distinct residual families then matched wurtzite ZnO and WO3. After adding both, every strong residual maximum lies within 0.065° 2theta of an accepted reflection, so there is no coherent peak family requiring another phase.

The final full-grid GSAS-II refinement used the stated Cu K-alpha doublet over 9.9034–100.0946° 2theta. It gives Rwp = 14.887%, Rp = 10.397%, Rexp = 2.646%, and goodness of fit = 5.634. A supported [101] March–Dollase term (0.835) and modest [101] size anisotropy were retained for ZnWO4. The refined ZnWO4 geometry remains chemically plausible (Zn–O 1.90–2.37 Å; W–O 1.88–2.21 Å).

ZnWO4 and ZnO identities are robust, but the exact WO3 polymorph is not reliable. P-1 and room-temperature monoclinic WO3 gave nearly indistinguishable checkpoint fits (Rwp 20.013% versus 20.039% in the same three-phase state). The available monoclinic reference also produced an unphysical 1.57 Å O–O contact, so the geometrically sound P-1 model was selected. Thus the approximately 10.3 wt% WO3 amount is better supported than its triclinic symmetry assignment. The ZnWO4 oxygen coordinates also reached imposed physical bounds and should not be interpreted as a high-precision structure determination.

Parenthesized uncertainties are formal least-squares standard uncertainties only. The elevated goodness of fit and structural-model ambiguity imply larger systematic uncertainty, especially for the minor-phase fractions. Fractions exclude any amorphous content because no internal standard was available.
