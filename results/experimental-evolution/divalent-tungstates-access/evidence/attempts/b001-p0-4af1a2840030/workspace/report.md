# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is a two-phase crystalline mixture of monoclinic WO3 and wurtzite ZnO. Rietveld refinement gives **74.02(6) wt% WO3** and **25.98(6) wt% ZnO**, normalized over the modeled crystalline material. The parenthetical uncertainties are the covariance-derived statistical values; a more realistic allowance for model/systematic effects is approximately ±0.5 percentage point. The result agrees closely with the 1:1 molar WO3:ZnO precursor charge, which corresponds to 74.01:25.99 wt%.

Evidence for WO3 is the characteristic monoclinic triplet at approximately 23.13, 23.58 and 24.39 degrees 2theta and the matching split reflection families throughout the scan. Wurtzite ZnO accounts for the reflections near 31.80, 34.58 and 36.32 degrees and their higher-angle counterparts. A wolframite ZnWO4 model was considered, but its strong approximately 18.9 degree and 30-31 degree features are absent. The final two-phase residual is at counting-noise level (Rwp = 2.853%, expected minimum Rwp = 2.799%, GOF = 1.023), and no coherent unmatched peaks justify another crystalline phase.

The refined structures are P21/c WO3 (submitted nonstandard setting: a = 7.68853 A, b = 7.53925 A, c = 10.51315 A, beta = 136.06515 degrees) and P63mc ZnO (a = 3.24712 A, c = 5.18400 A). Oxygen coordinates are less strongly constrained than the W/Zn sublattices, so their last reported digits should not be overinterpreted. Instrumental Lorentzian width and the large-crystallite size are correlated, but this has negligible effect on the phase fractions. A trace phase below the practical detection level or amorphous material cannot be excluded or quantified from this scan; the reported fractions therefore apply only to the modeled crystalline phases.
