# Powder XRD phase analysis

The modeled crystalline assemblage is 75.13 wt% wolframite MnWO4, 14.21 wt% monoclinic WO3, and 10.66 wt% of a tentative Pnnm Mn2WO5 intermediate. Fractions are normalized over these three crystalline phases. The final full-grid GSAS-II fit has Rwp = 14.812%, Rp = 8.781%, and goodness of fit = 8.358.

| Phase | Refined cell (A, degrees) | Crystalline wt% |
|---|---|---:|
| MnWO4, P 2/c | 4.79997, 5.71523, 4.98160; beta 91.1146 | 75.13 |
| WO3, P 21/c | 7.70278, 7.54662, 10.54871; beta 136.1418 | 14.21 |
| Mn2WO5, Pnnm (tentative) | 8.67138, 8.90374, 6.35688 | 10.66 |

The dominant peak set, including the lines near 15.87, 18.86, 24.05/24.61, 30.38/30.81, and 31.68 degrees 2theta, is indexed by the COD 1520200 wolframite MnWO4 structure. A second extensive family is fitted by monoclinic WO3; P21/c COD 2311041 and P-1 alternatives gave nearly tied profile fits, and the physically established monoclinic P21/c model was retained.

After those phases were fitted, a coherent residual family remained at approximately 20.32, 20.88, 29.14, 35.62, 47.02, and related higher angles. Precursor Mn3O4, Mn2O3, MnO, and rutile-type MnO2 did not account for this family with a stable physical scale. Distinct A2BO5 topology tests favored Pnnm. The submitted Mn2WO5 model is transferred from the Pnnm eveite parent Mn2(OH)(AsO4), COD 9000178: As(V)/OH(-) is replaced by W(VI)/O(2-), preserving both charge balance and the Mn sublattice. P21/c and Cmcm alternatives fit substantially worse; a tetragonal alternative required about 34% lattice expansion and implausibly long Mn-O distances. The refined Pnnm geometry remains reasonable (tetrahedral W-O about 1.68-1.74 A; Mn-O about 2.09-2.39 A).

The MnWO4 and WO3 identifications are reliable. The exact Mn2WO5 atomic structure and its fraction are not determined reliably to the formal least-squares precision: its model is analogue-derived, its [001] texture term reached the imposed physical bound, and several strong accepted-phase reflections remain underpredicted. A practical uncertainty of several weight-percent is more realistic for this phase and correspondingly affects the other fractions. The modeled crystalline inventory is mildly W-rich relative to the nominal 1:1 Mn:W feed (Mn/W about 0.90); no additional coherent Mn-oxide peak family was found, so an amorphous component or systematic error in the transferred-phase fraction cannot be excluded. No separate unresolved crystalline phase is asserted.
