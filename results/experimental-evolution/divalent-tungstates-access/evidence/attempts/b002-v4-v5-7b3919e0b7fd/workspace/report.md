# Powder XRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases.

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction |
|---|---:|---:|
| MnWO4 | P 2/c | 59.02 wt% |
| WO3 | P 21/c | 20.76 wt% |
| Mn3WO6 | R -3 | 20.22 wt% |

The full-range GSAS-II refinement used all 8,301 points from 9.903 to 100.095 degrees 2-theta and the stated Cu K-alpha doublet. It gave Rwp = 2.834%, Rp = 2.198%, Rexp = 1.994%, and goodness of fit = 1.421. No coherent unassigned reflection family remains.

## Evidence

The dominant reflection families, including their relative intensities, are accounted for by monoclinic MnWO4 and monoclinic WO3 models based on COD 1520200 and COD 2311041. The remaining reproducible families near 19.85, 20.40, 28.64, 31.77, 35.10, 45.7-46, 54-55, 59, 66.4, and 70.2 degrees, with corresponding higher-angle peaks, jointly index to an R -3 Mn3WO6 cell. That model uses the physical octahedral A3BO6 topology of COD 2242674, with Mn/W substitution and subsequent cell and coordinate refinement. Competing oxide and tungstate models either missed whole peak families or required nonphysical crystallite sizes or geometry.

The refined fractions also satisfy the synthesis chemistry: their implied bulk Mn:W molar ratio is 1.004, consistent with the nominal 1:1 precursor ratio. Refined metal-oxygen coordination remains physical. Instrument, specimen-displacement, crystallite-size, preferred-orientation, and supported anisotropic-strain terms were kept within the supplied experiment/generator bounds; branches with unstable or nonphysical geometry were rolled back.

## Remaining uncertainty

The MnWO4 and WO3 assignments are strong. The Mn3WO6 assignment is supported by a broad, internally consistent reflection family and chemical balance, but its structure was transferred from an isostructural A3BO6 analogue because no exact-composition reference was present in the frozen index. Its R -3 topology and phase amount are supported, but the exact oxygen coordinates cannot be determined reliably from this powder pattern alone.

The tabulated statistical fraction standard uncertainties are about 0.03 wt%, but they omit structural-model, absorption, microstructure, and reference-model uncertainty; the fractions should therefore not be interpreted more finely than roughly 1 wt%. They are crystalline-phase-normalized values only. Without an internal standard, an amorphous fraction cannot be measured, so these values are not absolute fractions of the whole specimen.
