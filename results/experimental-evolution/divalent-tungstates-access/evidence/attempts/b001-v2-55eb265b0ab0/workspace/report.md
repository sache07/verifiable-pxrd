# Powder XRD phase analysis

The crystalline product is a three-phase mixture dominated by wolframite ZnWO4, with unreacted monoclinic WO3 and wurtzite ZnO. Fractions below are normalized over the modeled crystalline phases; they do not measure any amorphous content.

## Evidence

The dominant peak set is indexed by a wolframite P2/c lattice. Because an exact ordinary ZnWO4 reference was unavailable in the supplied reference subset, the model was transferred from the same-stoichiometry wolframite CoMoO4-II framework by Co->Zn and Mo->W substitution, then refined in the complete mixture. It gives physically sensible Zn-O distances of 1.98-2.23 A and W-O distances of 1.75-2.12 A. Distinct target-composition alternatives were tested in that same multiphase state: FeWO4-derived P2/c gave 16.36% Rwp, while triclinic, scheelite, fergusonite and C2/m models gave about 53-55%, compared with 8.10% for the final physical model. A [100] March-Dollase correction was strongly supported for ZnWO4.

Residual peaks attributable to the starting reagents retain stable positive scales. Monoclinic P21/c WO3 is preferred over Pnma and the high-pressure monoclinic model (replacement Rwp 14.65% and 17.90%, respectively); a triclinic WO3 description was closer at 8.28% but less stable and not justified over the parsimonious P21/c model. The adopted WO3 model has sixfold W-O shells spanning 1.73-2.16 A. Wurtzite ZnO is decisively better than rocksalt ZnO (9.48% replacement Rwp), and its tetrahedral Zn-O distances are 1.96-1.99 A.

The largest remaining positive residuals occur on, or on the shoulders of, strong reflections already generated by these phases. They do not form a coherent unassigned peak series, so no fourth crystalline phase can be identified reliably. Prototype-transfer additions were therefore not retained.

## Fit and uncertainty

A fresh, no-refinement replay of `final.gpx` regenerated atomic structure factors and reflection lists on all 8301 measured points. It gave Rwp=8.0959%, Rp=4.6994%, chi-square=105862.28 and goodness-of-fit=3.5795, with no negative-background, negative-width or displacement-tensor flags. `fit.csv` is the resulting full-grid profile.

The phase identities are well supported, but the displayed fraction digits exceed the realistic precision. Preferred orientation, peak-shape correlations and background choice dominate the systematic uncertainty, and the input supplied no independent counting uncertainties or internal standard. The minor ZnO fraction is consequently less precise than the two major fractions; its crystallite size was not independently resolvable and was constrained at the physical upper limit. The subtle monoclinic-versus-triclinic distortion of WO3 and its oxygen coordinates are also not uniquely determined by this mixture, so the physically screened reference oxygen framework was retained. No amorphous fraction can be inferred from these normalized crystalline fractions.
