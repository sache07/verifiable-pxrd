# Powder XRD phase analysis

The crystalline sample is a partially reacted SrCO3 + WO3 mixture dominated by scheelite SrWO4. The refined crystalline-phase fractions are 81.29 wt% SrWO4, 11.67 wt% monoclinic WO3, and 7.05 wt% orthorhombic SrCO3 (normalized to the modeled crystalline material). The residual WO3 and SrCO3 amounts are approximately equimolar, which independently supports their assignment as unreacted 1:1 precursors.

Evidence for SrWO4 is the matched scheelite series at approximately 17.74, 27.49, 29.69, 32.94, 45.09, 47.45, 51.64, and 55.86 degrees 2theta. Because exact-composition SrWO4 entries were unavailable in the supplied library, its model was constructed from the same-framework SrMoO4 analogue (COD 2300460) by Mo-to-W substitution, followed by cell, oxygen-coordinate, displacement-parameter, and texture refinement. The result is a physically valid I 41/a model.

The weak triplet near 22.80/23.27/24.08 degrees and its higher-angle lines identify monoclinic WO3; full-pattern comparisons rejected the available tetragonal, orthorhombic, and lower-cell alternatives in favor of the P 21/c model based on COD 2311041. Peaks near 24.98, 25.55, 31.40, 35.09, and 36.33 degrees support orthorhombic strontianite, modeled from COD 9008198. No reproducible unmatched peak set requires a fourth crystalline phase.

The final native GSAS-II replay gives Rwp = 4.166%, Rp = 3.179%, and GOF = 1.049, close to the counting-statistics minimum Rwp of 3.981%. All exported structures pass occupancy and short-contact checks, and the CIFs and fractions agree with `final.gpx`.

Remaining uncertainty is mainly systematic. Absolute cell dimensions covary with the uncalibrated angular zero and specimen displacement, and the SrWO4 oxygen position/texture are less uniquely constrained than its Sr/W framework. Conservative fraction uncertainty is about 1–2 absolute wt% for SrWO4 and roughly 1 wt% for each minor phase; the identities and three-phase inventory are substantially more secure. These fractions exclude any amorphous material because no internal standard was measured.
