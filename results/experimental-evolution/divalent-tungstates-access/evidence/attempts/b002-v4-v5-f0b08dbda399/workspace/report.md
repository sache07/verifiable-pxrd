# Powder XRD phase analysis

## Result

The crystalline specimen is a three-phase mixture. Weight fractions are normalized over the modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Structure model | Weight fraction | Refined cell |
|---|---|---:|---|
| ZnWO4 | Wolframite, P 2/c | 82.30(17) wt% | a = 4.70683, b = 5.73910, c = 4.93863 A; beta = 90.6206 deg |
| WO3 | Monoclinic, P 21/c | 12.93(11) wt% | a = 7.31956, b = 7.53064, c = 10.54436 A; beta = 132.9812 deg |
| ZnO | Wurtzite, P 63mc | 4.78(16) wt% | a = 3.25833, c = 5.18891 A |

The full-range Rietveld fit (8301 points, 9.903-100.095 deg 2theta) has Rwp = 9.473%, Rp = 4.966%, and goodness of fit = 3.586. The quoted fraction uncertainties are one-standard-deviation covariance estimates; they do not include model systematics.

## Evidence

The precursor chemistry (WO3 and ZnO heated at 700 C) supports formation of ZnWO4 with residual starting oxides. The wolframite model accounts for the dominant reflection family across the entire scan. A positive, stable monoclinic WO3 scale accounts for its characteristic crowded low-angle group and many higher-angle reflections. A positive wurtzite ZnO scale accounts jointly for the 100/002/101 group near 32.05, 34.91, and 36.57 deg (positions include the refined zero correction) and its higher-angle family. Removing either minor phase leaves correlated residual families, whereas all three refined cells remain close to ordinary values for those phases.

All accepted structures have physical local geometry after bounded coordinate checks: ZnWO4 has six-coordinate Zn-O distances of about 2.00-2.28 A and W-O distances of about 1.80-2.23 A; the two WO3 octahedra span about 1.67-2.20 A; and tetrahedral ZnO has Zn-O distances of about 1.98 A. Alternative WO3 references with poorer octahedral geometry were rejected even when their profile residuals were close.

No coherent fourth reflection family remains. The largest localized mismatch, near 41.56 deg, coincides with a modeled ZnWO4 reflection and is paired with overprediction of the adjacent ZnWO4 reflection near 41.81 deg. Wider bounded coordinate trials did not improve this materially without adding unsupported freedom or worsening the octahedral geometry, so it is treated as a dominant-phase relative-intensity limitation rather than evidence for another phase.

## Remaining uncertainty

Preferred orientation is appreciable, especially for ZnWO4 and WO3, and the phase scales correlate with the fixed-at-boundary surface-roughness correction. Thus realistic fraction uncertainty is larger than the formal values in the table and is likely of order 1-2 wt% absolute. The 4.78 wt% ZnO fraction is consequently the least precise, although its identity is supported by multiple peaks. Fractions exclude amorphous material, which this analysis cannot quantify.

Because no calibrated instrument profile was supplied, a global zero correction of about +0.368 deg was refined along with the cells. Cell precision is therefore less certain than the formal standard uncertainties imply. Heavy-atom topology and phase identity are reliable, but the detailed oxygen coordinates, especially in low-symmetry WO3, cannot be determined independently to single-crystal precision from this laboratory powder pattern. A very minor additional phase below the pattern's practical detectability cannot be excluded.
