# Powder X-ray diffraction phase analysis

## Result

The 800 °C product made from WO3 and Mn3O4 is modeled as six crystalline phases. Fractions are normalized to the modeled crystalline material only.

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction (wt%) |
|---|---:|---:|
| MnWO4, wolframite | P 2/c | 60.73 ± 0.25 |
| WO3 | P 21/c | 21.26 ± 0.15 |
| Mn2WO6, Pbcn-type analogue | P b c n | 10.54 ± 0.19 |
| MnO | F m -3 m | 1.67 ± 0.17 |
| MnO2, rutile | P 42/m n m | 2.78 ± 0.20 |
| MnWO4, scheelite-type analogue | I 41/a | 3.02 ± 0.10 |

The final full-range refinement (9.9034–100.0946° 2theta, 8301 points) has Rwp = 14.054%, Rp = 9.077%, Rexp = 1.991%, and GOF = 7.057. The 19 varied quantities in the final equilibration were six phase scales, 12 background coefficients, and the zero offset; structural, texture, broadening, and surface-correction terms were held at their accepted checkpoints.

## Evidence

The dominant reflection families are reproduced by wolframite MnWO4 and monoclinic WO3. The selected WO3 reference has physical geometry; an alternative reference with similar profile agreement was rejected because it contained approximately 1.58 Å O–O contacts.

The largest initially unassigned family indexes on an orthorhombic Pbcn cell, refined to a = 4.58914, b = 17.8558, c = 4.95889 Å. Le Bail extraction on that cell lowered Rwp from 15.995% to 12.746% at the corresponding five-phase checkpoint, establishing the indexing independently of the structural intensities. A Fe2WO6 topology with Fe replaced by Mn and bounded coordinate adjustment was then used for the structural model. Its final coordination is physical: W–O = 1.77–1.92 Å and Mn–O = 1.89–2.34 Å, with no short O–O contacts. A competing P212121 analogue required crystallite size and strain at their imposed limits (40 nm and 8000 ppm) plus strong preferred orientation, so it was not selected despite a locally competitive profile fit.

MnO, rutile MnO2, and the scheelite-type MnWO4 analogue each gave stable positive scales and support from multiple separated reflections. Adding the scheelite-type model lowered Rwp from 15.643% to 15.194% before final profile/calibration refinement. Residual tests did not justify Mn3O4 (0.17 ± 0.22 wt% in its test) or Mn5O8: the latter's nominal improvement depended on several strong calculated reflections absent from the observed pattern. No remaining coherent reflection family required another crystalline phase.

## Uncertainty and interpretation

The Pbcn Mn2WO6 phase family and cell are well supported, but its exact atomic topology is not uniquely determined by these powder data; its CIF is a physically constrained analogue model. The approximately 3 wt% scheelite-type component is likewise a topology assignment transferred from CaWO4, so both its structure and fraction are more model-dependent than their formal scale e.s.d.s imply. The minor MnO and MnO2 fractions also carry appreciable profile-overlap uncertainty.

The reported e.s.d.s are GSAS-II counting-statistics estimates conditional on this six-phase model. The elevated GOF, lack of supplied pointwise uncertainties, preferred-orientation/profile corrections, and residual intensity mismatch indicate larger systematic uncertainty. No amorphous content was quantified, so the values must not be interpreted as absolute specimen fractions. Unreacted Mn3O4 is not reliably detected, but a quantitative detection limit was not established.
