# Powder XRD phase analysis

The Cu K-alpha reflection pattern from the nominal 1:1 Mn:W precursor mixture (WO3 + Mn3O4, heated at 800 °C) is described by three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases; no amorphous fraction was quantified.

| Phase | Space group | Crystalline wt.% | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| MnWO4, wolframite | P 2/c | 60.13 | High-confidence exact reference match |
| monoclinic gamma-WO3 | P 21/n | 19.76 | High-confidence exact reference match |
| Mn3WO6, Co3TeO6-type model | C 2/c | 20.11 | Composition and topology supported; detailed structure provisional |

The final whole-pattern fit has Rwp = 8.09%, Rp = 5.51%, Rexp = 1.99%, and GOF = 4.07. Refined cells are MnWO4: a = 4.79500, b = 5.70105, c = 4.97043 Å, beta = 91.1068°; WO3: a = 7.29460, b = 7.53662, c = 7.68289 Å, beta = 90.9335°; and C2/c Mn3WO6: a = 14.9028, b = 8.92060, c = 10.8620 Å, beta = 91.5073°.

## Evidence

Wolframite MnWO4 (COD 1520200) accounts for the dominant diagnostic reflections, including the families near 15.48, 18.44, 23.67, 29.98, 30.42, 31.30, and 37.44° 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 (COD 1528915) accounts for the coherent residual family near 26.56, 28.60, 33.2–34.1, 41.9, 50.7, and 54–59°.

The remaining coherent family is fitted by an Mn3WO6 model transferred from the physical C2/c Co3TeO6 topology (COD 2015468), with Co replaced by Mn and Te by W. Its principal residual assignments include 19.84, 31.75, 35.13, 46.49, and 54.34°. The refined model retains sensible coordination distances (W–O about 1.93–2.03 Å; shortest Mn–O about 1.94–2.53 Å) and a finite coherent-domain size of about 45 nm. The final phase inventory gives Mn:W = 1.016 on a molar basis, independently closing the nominal 1:1 precursor balance.

Distinct Mn3WO6 topology branches were compared (R3, C2/c, Pa-3, Ia-3, and P21/n parents), together with Pbcn and P42/mnm Mn2WO6 analogues. In a same-background/profile replay, the closest physical competitor, R3 Ni3TeO6-type, gave Rwp = 14.92% and only about 4 wt.% support, versus 8.09% and about 20 wt.% for C2/c. Exact Mn3O4/Mn2O3 precursor models and Mn2WO6 models collapsed to negligible scale or failed to account for the residual families. The strongest remaining positive residual maxima coincide with accepted-phase reflections rather than forming a new sharp family.

## Remaining uncertainty

MnWO4 and WO3 identities are robust. The Mn3WO6 formula and C2/c topology are strongly favored by reflection coverage, geometry, and molar balance, but no exact-composition Mn3WO6 reference was available; its submitted coordinates therefore remain a chemically transferred prototype rather than a uniquely determined structure. Unconstrained coordinate refinements for this weak phase produced nonphysical bonds and were rejected. WO3 oxygen coordinates were likewise retained from the reference because unconstrained light-atom refinement was unstable.

The formal GSAS-II fraction errors are only about 0.09–0.12 percentage points and do not include topology, background, or broadening-model uncertainty. A realistic uncertainty on the provisional Mn3WO6 fraction is several weight percent, with compensating uncertainty mainly in WO3. Broad non-crystalline/background intensity is present, so the reported values must not be interpreted as absolute fractions including amorphous material.
