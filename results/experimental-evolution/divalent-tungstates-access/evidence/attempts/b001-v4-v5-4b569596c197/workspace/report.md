# Quantitative crystalline-phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is described by two crystalline phases:

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (Å, °) | Crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| CaWO₄, scheelite | I 4₁/a (No. 88) | a = 5.24093, c = 11.34083 | 85.27 wt% |
| WO₃, monoclinic | P 2₁/c (No. 14) | a = 7.69886, b = 7.56164, c = 10.52475, β = 135.9477° | 14.73 wt% |

The full-range GSAS-II refinement (9.9034–100.0946° 2θ, all 8,301 points) converged at Rwp = 3.447%, Rp = 2.598%, Rexp = 3.129%, and goodness of fit = 1.102. The submitted `final.gpx`, CIFs, fractions, and `fit.csv` all come from this same final replay.

## Evidence and model choice

The dominant family (including peaks near 18.27, 28.43, 31.21, 33.85, 38.91, 46.89, 54.15, and 57.65°) is indexed by scheelite CaWO₄ (COD 7219229). After fitting it, the coherent residual family near 22.76, 23.18, 23.98, 26.19, 28.77, 32.88/33.13, 49.48, and 55.49° is indexed by monoclinic WO₃ (COD 2311041). This is chemically consistent with incomplete conversion of the WO₃ + CaCO₃ charge at 650 °C. No coherent reflection family remains that warrants a third crystalline phase; CaO, CaCO₃, other Ca–W–O products, and alternative WO₃ cells did not obtain broad-family support with a stable positive contribution.

The closest alternative was a triclinic WO₃ description with essentially the same Rwp, but its refined angles collapsed toward a monoclinic metric. The simpler P 2₁/c model was therefore retained. A second monoclinic reference and an orthorhombic WO₃ model fit less well. The CaWO₄ oxygen site refined reproducibly from three bounded starts and retained normal geometry (W–O ≈ 1.798 Å; Ca–O ≈ 2.420–2.454 Å). Releasing WO₃ W coordinates drove several shifts to bounds, and its oxygen coordinates were underdetermined; the physical reference coordinates were restored. Surface roughness refined to zero after this correction, while texture and anisotropic-size tests gave negligible, axis-dependent gains and were omitted.

## Uncertainty

Fractions are normalized only over the modeled crystalline phases. The covariance-matrix e.s.d. is about 0.09 wt% for either fraction, but it excludes structural/profile-model choice; an absolute uncertainty of roughly 1 wt% is more realistic. An amorphous fraction cannot be measured without an internal or external standard. The WO₃ phase identity, cell, and amount are reliable at this level, but its individual light-atom coordinates are not independently determined by this powder pattern and remain reference-based.
