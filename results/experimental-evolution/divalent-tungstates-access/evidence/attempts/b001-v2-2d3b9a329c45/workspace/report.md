# Powder XRD phase analysis

The crystalline product is a three-phase mixture of wolframite ZnWO4, monoclinic WO3, and wurtzite ZnO. The GSAS-II weight fractions, normalized over these modeled crystalline phases, are 74.83(13) wt% ZnWO4, 18.42(10) wt% WO3, and 6.75(11) wt% ZnO. The full-grid fit (9.9034–100.0946 degrees 2theta) gives Rwp = 7.977%, Rp = 4.656%, Rexp = 2.264%, and goodness of fit = 3.524.

## Evidence and model selection

The precursor context (WO3 + ZnO heated at 700 degrees C) and the diagnostic peak positions identify wolframite ZnWO4 as the main reaction product. Because the available reference collection contained no exact O-W-Zn entry, its P2/c model was constructed by same-stoichiometry transfer from P2/c CoMoO4 (COD 7205001; Co->Zn and Mo->W), followed by cell, coordinate, common-Uiso, texture, and profile refinement. A two-phase ZnWO4 + WO3 model left the ZnO reflections coherent and had Rwp near 32%; adding wurtzite ZnO in the same multiphase model and accounting for the strong ZnWO4 [100] preferred orientation reduced Rwp to about 9–10% before final refinement. The WO3 and ZnO starting models were COD 2311041 (P21/c) and COD 9004178 (P63mc), respectively.

Residual-directed framework tests were performed against the frozen best three-phase state. ZnWO4 models transferred from FeWO4 (P2/c), CuWO4 (P-1), CdWO4 (I41/a), CaWO4 (C2/c), ZnMoO4 (P-1), and MgMoO4 (C2/m) were each tested as additions from two starting scales. Their stable fitted amounts were 0–0.075 wt%, with at most a 0.0013 percentage-point decrease in Rwp, so no fourth crystalline phase is supported. As replacements for the main phase, the closest alternate P2/c model gave Rwp = 15.73%; the distinct symmetries gave Rwp = 54.07–54.78%. The strongest remaining positive residual at about 41.36 degrees coincides with the modeled ZnWO4 (121) reflection at 41.359 degrees and is therefore an intensity/overlap mismatch rather than an unindexed peak.

The final state was selected by a fresh full-grid GSAS-II replay from the clean accepted checkpoint, not from any failed trial. Refinements used the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, a smooth Chebyshev background, effective zero, phase cells, phase sizes/strain, and March-Dollase texture. Poisson standard deviations (sqrt(counts)) were used because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied.

## Physical checks and uncertainty

The submitted CIFs are ordered, fully occupied structures with the expected formulas and symmetries. Calculated densities are 7.803, 7.257, and 5.632 g/cm3 for ZnWO4, WO3, and ZnO. Minimum interatomic distances are 1.765, 1.733, and 1.979 A, respectively; ZnWO4 has W-O = 1.765–2.114 A and Zn-O = 1.970–2.217 A, while WO3 has W-O = 1.733–2.172 A. No short-contact or occupancy problem was found.

The fractions are reliable as crystalline-phase proportions, but the parenthetical uncertainties are formal least-squares values and are lower bounds. Systematic error from preferred orientation, anisotropic broadening, the residual intensity mismatch, and the assumed counting uncertainties likely makes practical fraction uncertainty of order 1–2 wt% absolute. Amorphous content is not quantified, and trace phases below the diffraction detection limit cannot be excluded. ZnWO4 coordinates are refined and physically plausible; only W positions and a common Uiso were refined for WO3 because free oxygen refinement was unstable, so detailed WO3 bond geometry is less certain than its phase identity and fraction. The elevated goodness of fit likewise indicates remaining profile/systematic error rather than evidence for another resolved phase.
