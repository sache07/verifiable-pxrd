# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases. Fractions are normalized over those phases only.

| Phase | Refined model | Crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| Scheelite CaWO₄ | *I*4₁/*a* | 81.17 wt% (formal 1σ: 0.21 wt%) |
| Tungsten trioxide | *P*2₁/*c* | 13.84 wt% (formal 1σ: 0.06 wt%) |
| Calcite CaCO₃ | *R*−3*c* | 5.00 wt% (formal 1σ: 0.24 wt%) |

## Evidence

The refinement used the full 9.9034–100.0946° 2θ grid and the reported Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. The final atomic-model fit has Rwp = 3.2326%, Rp = 2.4888%, Rexp = 3.1382%, and goodness of fit = 1.0301 for 8,301 observations.

Scheelite CaWO₄ accounts for the dominant families, including calculated reflections at 18.31, 28.44, 33.88, 38.92, 46.90, and 54.16° 2θ. Monoclinic WO₃ accounts for the dense precursor family around 22.77–26.20° and its higher-angle partners. Orthorhombic, hexagonal, and tetragonal WO₃ topology branches fit substantially worse.

The lower-symmetry triclinic *P*−1 WO₃ alternative was carried through the same three-phase scale, cell, calibration, heavy-atom, limited oxygen, and profile stages. Its unrelaxed-atom checkpoint gave Rwp = 3.2514%; bounded heavy-atom relaxation could lower this to 3.2084%, nominally 0.024 percentage point below the submitted model. That gain required the physical parent's α and γ angles to move from 88.81° and 90.93° to 90.02° and 89.97°, making the metric effectively monoclinic, while two W sites reached their coordinate windows. A simultaneous oxygen trial drove 28 parameters to limits, and three one-site oxygen tests gave at most a marginal stable improvement. In contrast, the *P*2₁/*c* cell remains close to its physical parent and is the expected room-temperature WO₃ topology. The *P*2₁/*c* model was therefore retained on physical and parsimony grounds despite the small numerical advantage available to the over-flexible *P*−1 surrogate. Its exported cell is a nonstandard but equivalent monoclinic setting.

Calcite is supported by a coherent set of reflections at 29.09, 35.65, 39.10, 42.85, 47.23, 48.21, and 57.10° 2θ. It is also an exact starting precursor. Adding calcite lowered Rwp from 3.4179% to 3.2326% and its scale remained nonzero after cell, calibration, and profile relaxation. The refined inventory gives Ca:W = 0.971 on a molar basis, close to the laboratory's 1:1 precursor-metal ratio without adding another phase. After the three phases were included, no smoothed positive residual exceeded 2.2 counting-statistical standard deviations and no coherent unassigned reflection family remained.

All exported structures pass a short-contact screen. Representative distances are W–O = 1.791 Å and Ca–O = 2.428–2.451 Å in CaWO₄; W–O = 1.682–2.197 Å in WO₃; and C–O = 1.273 Å and Ca–O = 2.362 Å in calcite.

## Remaining uncertainty

The CaWO₄, WO₃, and calcite compositions and the presence of approximately 5 wt% calcite are reliable, but the exact WO₃ symmetry remains the main structural ambiguity. The quoted covariance ESDs understate model uncertainty. Calcite peak size and microstrain are strongly coupled and poorly determined; physically staged triclinic checkpoints place its fraction between 5.26 and 5.86 wt% rather than the submitted 5.00 wt%. Practical uncertainties of roughly ±1 wt% for CaWO₄ and calcite and about ±0.5 wt% for WO₃ are more defensible than the formal values.

The CaWO₄ oxygen position was refined and remained geometrically physical. The detailed oxygen coordinates in WO₃ and calcite were not independently resolved by this powder pattern: bounded light-atom trials were unstable or gave no supported improvement, so physical reference-parent coordinates were retained while cells and the robust heavy-atom terms were refined. Thus the phase topologies are supported, but subtle WO₃ distortions and the weak-phase light-atom coordinates should not be treated as uniquely determined.

No internal standard or amorphous model was available, so absolute crystalline yield and amorphous content cannot be determined. The reported fractions are crystalline-only. Lattice metrics also carry extra systematic uncertainty from specimen-displacement/angular-calibration covariance.
