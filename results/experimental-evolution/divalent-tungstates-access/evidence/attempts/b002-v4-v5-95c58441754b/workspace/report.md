# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases.

| Phase | Structure | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| ZnWO4 | Wolframite, P 2/c | 82.97 wt% |
| WO3 | Triclinic, P -1 | 11.42 wt% |
| ZnO | Wurtzite, P 63 m c | 5.61 wt% |

The final full-pattern fit has Rp = 3.463%, Rwp = 4.839%, Rexp = 2.646%, and goodness of fit = 1.828. It uses the full 9.903-100.095 degree range and the reported Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. Since point uncertainties were not supplied, count-statistical weights (1/intensity) were used. The formal GSAS-II fraction e.s.d.s are 0.11, 0.05, and 0.10 percentage points for ZnWO4, WO3, and ZnO, respectively.

## Evidence

The dominant reflection family requires a wolframite ZnWO4 model; scheelite and C2/c alternatives miss diagnostic lines. Because the frozen index had no exact Zn-W-O entry, the starting P 2/c topology was transferred from wolframite-type parents (COD 5910277, checked against COD 7205001) and its coordinates were refined with geometry screening. The final octahedra are physical: Zn-O = 2.081-2.114 A and W-O = 1.862-2.141 A. A [111] March-Dollase term (0.7005) is strongly supported for this dominant phase.

Coherent residual families are matched by exact-composition precursor references: triclinic WO3 from COD 2106293 and wurtzite ZnO from COD 9004178. In comparable three-phase checkpoints, triclinic WO3 gave Rwp = 5.38%, versus 5.76% for monoclinic P21/n and 11.89% for orthorhombic Pnma before the final joint replay. Removing ZnO raised Rwp to about 8.27%, so its scale is not a numerical artifact. The final phase inventory contains only the elements in the supplied precursors and implies an overall Zn:W molar ratio of about 1.063:1. The strongest remaining residual maxima coincide with accepted ZnWO4, WO3, or ZnO reflections rather than forming a fourth peak family.

## Remaining uncertainty

The fractions exclude any amorphous material, which cannot be quantified without an internal standard. Their formal e.s.d.s describe counting-statistical precision only; overlap, preferred orientation, profile choice, and displacement/calibration correlations make the total uncertainty larger. Detailed oxygen coordinates in the 11.4 wt% WO3 and 5.6 wt% ZnO phases cannot be determined reliably from this pattern and remain reference-constrained. An unconstrained WO3 coordinate branch gave a slightly lower Rwp but produced an implausible 1.590 A W-O contact, so it was rejected. Thus the phase identities and approximate crystalline fractions are reliable, while fine local distortions in the minor phases are not.
