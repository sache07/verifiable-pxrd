## Evidence and model choice

The dominant reflection family is indexed and fit by wolframite MnWO4 using COD 1520200. The characteristic residual cluster near 23–25° 2θ and its higher-angle partners requires monoclinic WO3; the retained structural reference is the physically valid neutron model COD 2311041. A lower-precision WO3 entry gave an impossible 1.58 Å O–O contact and was discarded even though its peak positions were similar.

After those phases were included, the remaining strong reflections at approximately 20.32, 20.88, 29.14, 35.62, 47.02, and 54.89° form one R-centered trigonal lattice. Indexing gives a=8.905 Å and c=10.474 Å initially, with M20 about 149 and no unmatched members of the selected family. The structural model is analogue-derived from Mn3TeO6 (COD 2211528, R-3) by Te→W substitution, giving charge-balanced Mn3WO6; its refined geometry is physical (W–O 1.84–1.96 Å). Ambient Mn3O4 and Mn2O3 precursor models refined to negligible scale and did not explain this family. Once Mn3WO6 was added, no coherent residual reflection family remained.

Heavy-atom coordinates were refined first, followed by bounded/physical oxygen-coordinate checks. An unconstrained WO3 oxygen refinement produced a 1.65 Å W–O contact and was rolled back to the physical parent coordinates. Phase-common isotropic displacement factors refine to 0.0058, 0.0099, and 0.0111 Å² for MnWO4, Mn3WO6, and WO3, respectively. Texture, anisotropic-strain, surface-roughness, and extra profile branches were not retained because they were null, unstable, or outside the stated laboratory ranges.

## Remaining uncertainty

MnWO4 and WO3 are high-confidence phase assignments. The Mn3WO6 peak lattice and fraction are well supported, but its exact atomic topology is less certain because it is an analogue-derived model rather than an exact-composition reference; in particular, its oxygen coordinates are not determined as reliably as its cell and heavy-atom framework. Formal Rietveld fraction standard uncertainties are much smaller than model uncertainty; practical uncertainty is roughly 1 wt% for MnWO4/WO3 and about 2 wt% for Mn3WO6. The fractions do not quantify amorphous material, and the instrument/size/microstrain partition is correlated, so crystallite-size and strain values should not be treated as independently reliable.
