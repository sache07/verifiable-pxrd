# Powder XRD phase analysis

| Phase | Structural model | Crystalline weight fraction |

The fractions are normalized over the three modeled crystalline phases. The parenthetical uncertainties are one-standard-deviation least-squares estimates in the final scale refinement; they do not include model-selection, preferred-orientation, or microabsorption systematics.

## Evidence and refinement

The specimen context (WO3 + Mn3O4 heated at 800 °C) restricts the ordinary chemistry to the Mn-W-O system. MnWO4 accounts for the characteristic families near 15.3°, 18.3°, 23.5–24.0°, and 29.8–30.2° 2θ. The independent Mn3WO6 family includes the paired lines near 19.7–20.2° and reflections near 28.5°, 35.0°, and 46.4°. Monoclinic WO3 is required by the 22.9–24.2° multiplet and its 33–34° family. Their refined fractions imply a bulk Mn:W molar ratio of 1.027, consistent with the nominal 1:1 precursor ratio. No remaining coherent reflection family supports an additional crystalline phase.

The final atomic Rietveld model used both Cu Kα wavelengths and includes supported preferred-orientation corrections for all three phases, MnWO4 anisotropic microstrain, crystallite-size broadening, background, cells, calibration, and positive tied isotropic displacement parameters. A bounded coordinate polish was accepted only after convergence, stable phase fractions, and geometry checks; the largest atomic displacement from the reference checkpoint was 0.116 Å. Exported structures retain the reference topologies and have no short contacts (minimum Mn-O 2.066 Å and W-O 1.730 Å).

Independent full-grid replay of `final.gpx` gives Rwp = 2.8563% and Rp = 2.1083% over all 8,301 points. The saved GSAS-II refinement interval gives Rwp = 2.8578% and goodness of fit = 2.007.

## Remaining uncertainty

The three phase identities are well supported. Their relative crystalline fractions are also determined, but the formal errors above are smaller than likely systematic errors from overlapping peaks and preferred orientation; those systematic errors cannot be quantified reliably from this scan alone. An amorphous fraction or material not producing coherent Bragg peaks cannot be determined without an internal standard, so the reported values are not absolute whole-specimen fractions. Detailed oxygen coordinates—especially in WO3—are less certain than the phase topology and cell because of peak overlap. No additional residual crystalline structure can be identified reliably, and none is reported.
