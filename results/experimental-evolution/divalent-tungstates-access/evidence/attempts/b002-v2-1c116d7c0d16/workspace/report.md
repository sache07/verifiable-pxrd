# Powder XRD phase analysis

## Result

| Phase | Structural model | Crystalline weight fraction | Assessment |
|---|---|---:|---|
| MnWO4 | Wolframite, P 2/c (No. 13) | 59.51% | Robust identification |
| WO3 | Monoclinic, P 21/c (No. 14) | 20.80% | Robust identification |
| Mn3WO6 | R -3 (No. 148) prototype-transfer model | 19.69% | Provisional structure/symmetry |

Fractions sum to 100% over the modeled crystalline material. Without an internal standard, an absolute amorphous fraction cannot be determined.

## Evidence

The synthesis context (Mn3O4 + WO3, 800 °C) and the dominant peak set at approximately 15.47, 18.43, 23.67, 24.19, 29.99, 30.42, 31.31 and 36.22° 2theta identify wolframite MnWO4. Coherent peaks near 23.08, 26.56, 28.62, 33.24 and 34.13° require residual monoclinic WO3.

Those two phases alone left a structured residual at about 19.85, 20.40, 28.64, 31.76, 35.11, 45.68, 46.49, 54–55, 59.34, 63.26, 66.41 and 70.08°. A same-stoichiometry prototype search across substituted oxide frameworks selected R -3 Mn3WO6, constructed from the Cd3TeO6 framework (Cd→Mn, Te→W). In the common two-phase project, adding this model reduced Rwp from 17.021% to 9.166% before ordinary full refinement. Tested Mn2WO5 (Pnnm and P21/c), Mn3WO6 (C2/c and Ia-3), Mn3W2O9 (P63/mmc) and Mn2WO6 (P212121) alternatives remained materially worse. Tetragonal and orthorhombic Mn3O4 precursor tests both converged reproducibly to a scale of 1e-12 once the selected three phases were present.

The final saved project was independently replayed from atomic structure factors and freshly generated reflections over all 8301 points, with no parameter optimization. It gives Rwp = 3.4959%, Rp = 2.5471% and GOF = 1.759. The replay reported no negative analytic peak widths or negative background.

## Remaining uncertainty

MnWO4 and WO3 are well constrained by many resolved reflections. The R -3 Mn3WO6 assignment is strongly supported as a phase-pattern model but remains provisional because it is an isostructural chemical transfer rather than a direct Mn3WO6 reference; closely related symmetry variants cannot be excluded at trace-detail level from laboratory powder data alone. Its refined geometry is physically plausible, but the exact oxygen coordinates should not be treated as a single-crystal determination.

All submitted CIFs pass the scorer's contact/occupancy screen. The WO3 oxygen coordinates were kept within the generator-scale neighborhood of the physical literature framework after an unconstrained coordinate fit produced an impossible contact and was rejected. Profile/roughness terms reach physical bounds, and zero offset is highly correlated with specimen displacement; therefore the reported normalized fractions are useful estimates, but their systematic uncertainty is larger than a formal least-squares scale error. No absolute phase fractions including amorphous material are claimed.
