# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| CaWO₄, scheelite | I 41/a | a = 5.23643 Å, c = 11.3315 Å | 0.8003 (about 80.0 wt%) |
| WO₃, monoclinic | P 21/c | a = 7.68952 Å, b = 7.55286 Å, c = 10.5120 Å, β = 135.945° | 0.1398 (about 14.0 wt%) |
| CaCO₃, calcite | R -3 c | a = 4.98489 Å, c = 17.0492 Å | 0.0600 (about 6.0 wt%) |

## Evidence

Scheelite CaWO₄ accounts for the dominant peaks, including those near 18.27°, 28.43/28.76°, 33.84°, 46.89°, and 57.65° 2θ. Monoclinic WO₃ is required by the characteristic resolved group at approximately 22.75°, 23.17°, and 23.97° and by its higher-angle families. The selected P 21/c WO₃ reference fitted the common multiphase pattern better than tested triclinic, alternative monoclinic, orthorhombic, and hexagonal WO₃ references.

After fitting CaWO₄ and WO₃, coherent residual peaks remained at approximately 29.07°, 35.65°, 39.17°, 47.25°, and 48.17°. A calcite addition matched this complete set. Its scale converged from substantially different starts and lowered Rwp from about 3.89% to 3.72% before joint profile refinement; the final three-phase refinement reached Rwp = 3.4944% and Rp = 2.6761%. An aragonite CaCO₃ replacement collapsed to zero in the CaWO₄ + WO₃ common state, and adding aragonite to the calcite-containing state gave only 0.036 wt% with scale below one standard uncertainty.

## Refinement and residual tests

The full 9.903–100.095° grid was refined with the reported Cu Kα doublet, a Bragg–Brentano specimen displacement, a smooth 16-term background, and physical phase-specific size/strain terms. No preferred orientation was retained: all tested March–Dollase axes converged close to 1. The final fresh GSAS-II replay used all 8301 points, gave reduced χ² = 1.243 (goodness of fit = 1.115), and raised no negative-background, negative-width, polarization, or displacement-tensor review flags.

In the final common three-phase state, CaO, Ca(OH)₂, and Ca₃WO₆ scales stably collapsed to the nonnegative bound from two scale starts. Ca₄.₂₆W₁₀O₃₀ gave only about 0.11 wt%, lacked a coherent set of unique residual peaks, and was below reliable quantitative significance; it was not retained. Once calcite was included, the remaining structured residuals were derivatives at already modeled CaWO₄/WO₃ peaks rather than an unmatched peak set. Residual-directed prototype transfer was therefore not invoked.

All submitted CIFs pass the broad geometry/occupancy screen with positive isotropic displacement parameters and no impossible short contacts. The atomic shifts were symmetry preserving and limited to the stated reference-mismatch range.

## Remaining uncertainty

The phase identities and the need for all three phases are reliable. The exact minor-phase atomic coordinates are less well constrained than the structure types, especially WO₃ oxygen positions and the calcite oxygen coordinate. Scale factors correlate with isotropic displacement parameters and peak broadening; defensible approximate absolute uncertainties are ±1.5 wt% for CaWO₄ and ±1 wt% for each minor phase. A contribution near the 0.1 wt% level cannot be identified or quantified reliably from this pattern. The reported fractions should therefore be read as approximately 80/14/6 wt%, not as precision beyond that level.
