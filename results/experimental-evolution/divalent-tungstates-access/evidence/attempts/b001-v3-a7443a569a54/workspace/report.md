# Powder X-ray diffraction analysis

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases: wolframite ZnWO4 (P2/c), monoclinic WO3 (P21/c), and wurtzite ZnO (P63mc). Their crystalline-phase-normalized weight fractions are 74.61%, 18.61%, and 6.78%, respectively. These fractions do not quantify amorphous material or any material not represented by the crystalline models.

## Evidence

The dominant wolframite model accounts for the strong families near 15.59, 18.99, 23.89, 24.60, 30.5–31.3, 36.44, and 38.37 degrees 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the separate low-angle family around 23.24–23.73 degrees and further lines near 33.38–35.8 degrees. The remaining wurtzite family, including contributions near 31.8, 47.7, and 56.7 degrees, requires ZnO. Peak overlap is substantial, so identification was based on simultaneous full-pattern indexing and intensity fitting rather than isolated peak matches.

This phase assemblage is also chemically economical for the reported WO3 + ZnO precursors heated at 700 degrees C. The residual WO3:ZnO mass ratio is 2.746, corresponding to a molar ratio of 0.964:1, close to equal unreacted precursor equivalents. After cell, physically stable coordinates, crystallite-size/microstrain, and limited preferred-orientation terms were refined, every coherent reflection family indexed to one of these phases; remaining discrepancies are intensity/profile noise at accepted reflections and do not justify another phase.

The submitted full-grid Rietveld fit has Rwp = 2.822%, Rp = 2.135%, and goodness of fit = 1.246 over 8,301 observations. An independent replay regenerated native GSAS-II atomic structure factors and reflection lists from `final.gpx`, optimized zero parameters, reproduced the phase fractions and calculated profile, and raised no profile-review flags.

## Structural model and uncertainty

The ZnWO4 model uses the wolframite topology transferred from an isostructural divalent-metal tungstate and adapted to Zn; its refined cell and bond distances are physical. For WO3, two P21/c coordinate starts were given comparable staged refinement, and the retained model fit substantially better. Its CIF uses a valid nonstandard monoclinic setting (beta approximately 133.02 degrees). Wurtzite ZnO is a stable weak component.

Phase identities and the three-phase assemblage are reliable. Formal scale-factor errors would understate uncertainty from overlap, texture, and profile-model choice; a pragmatic uncertainty is about 1–2 absolute weight-percentage points, with the 6.78% ZnO fraction the least precise. The WO3 oxygen coordinates could not be refined independently without unstable, nonphysical shifts, so they remain tied to a physical literature coordinate start and should not be interpreted as a precise oxygen-substructure determination. ZnO preferred orientation was likewise not identifiable and was omitted. No quantitative amorphous fraction can be determined from these data.
