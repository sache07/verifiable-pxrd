# Powder X-ray diffraction phase analysis

The specimen was modeled as three crystalline phases. Weight fractions below are normalized over the modeled crystalline material; no amorphous fraction can be obtained from these data without an internal standard.

## Fit and evidence

The final 8301-point fit has Rwp = 14.8141%, Rp = 8.6085%, and χ² = 4.58303×10⁵ using weights of 1/max(observed counts, 1). Pointwise experimental uncertainties were not supplied, so χ² should not be interpreted as a conventional goodness-of-fit statistic. An independent GSAS-II replay regenerated the profile and fractions without changing the project and produced no profile-review flags.

Wolframite MnWO₄ accounts for the dominant reflection family, including the strong features near 15.5°, 18.5°, 23.7–24.2°, and 30–31° 2θ. Monoclinic WO₃ is required by a second broad family around 23–29°, 33–34°, and 41.9°. Several exact-composition WO₃ references were tested; the selected model gave the better replayed fit and retained ordinary oxide geometry, whereas a close competitor contained an implausibly short O–O contact.

After those phases were refined, a coherent broad residual family near 35.1°, 45.7°, 54.4°, and 66.4° indexed to wurtzite-type MnO. It retained a positive scale, refined to a plausible cell, and reduced Rwp from about 16.2% for the two-phase checkpoint to 14.8% in the complete model. Rock-salt MnO, Mn₃O₄, Mn₂O₃, rutile-type MnO₂, reduced tungsten oxides, and candidate ternary Mn–W oxides were also tested. They either refined to negligible abundance, explained only isolated lines, were numerically unstable, or predicted unsupported strong reflections; none justified a fourth phase.

The final model includes an ordinary polynomial background, Cu Kα1/Kα2 radiation, zero/sample-displacement terms, unit cells, bounded size/strain terms, and modest preferred-orientation corrections for WO₃ and hexagonal MnO. Atomic coordinates were audited with the supplied bounded staged polish. Its coordinate branches changed Rwp by only about 0.008 percentage point, so the helper retained the complete physical starting checkpoint. The submitted structures pass the contact/occupancy screen. Shortest refined cation–oxygen distances are 2.081 Å (Mn–O) and 1.773 Å (W–O) in MnWO₄, 1.741 Å (W–O) in WO₃, and 1.945 Å (Mn–O) in hexagonal MnO.

## Remaining uncertainty

The MnWO₄ and WO₃ phase identities are robust. The hexagonal MnO assignment is supported by several reflections, but its peaks are broad and strongly overlapped; its refined coherent-domain size is about 0.053 µm and its scale is correlated with profile and preferred-orientation terms. Its reported 11.29 wt% fraction is therefore semi-quantitative and is not reliably precise to better than several weight percent. Similar profile correlations make all three fractions more uncertain than their displayed digits suggest.

Positive residuals remain at approximately 19.86° and 20.39° 2θ, with smaller intensity mismatches centered on accepted reflections. No physically credible tested structure accounts for the low-angle pair with broad family support and a stable positive scale. Consequently, the structure and weight fraction of any additional crystalline component cannot be determined reliably and no fourth phase is asserted. Fine oxygen coordinates, especially in WO₃, are also less well constrained than the phase topology and unit cell because the pattern is dominated by heavy-atom scattering.
