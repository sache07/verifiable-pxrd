# Powder XRD phase analysis

The crystalline model contains scheelite CaWO4 and residual monoclinic gamma-WO3. The dominant 18.27, 28.43/28.77, 31.21, 33.85, 46.89, 48.85, and 57.65 degree peak sequence indexes to scheelite (I 41/a). The separate 22.77/23.18/23.99 degree triplet and its 26.19, 32.88, 41.51, 49.48, and 55.49 degree companions index to distorted WO3. Every coherent resolved peak family is represented; no independent crystalline CaCO3 or CaO family is evident.

The starting models were COD 7219229 (CaWO4) and COD 2311041 (WO3), followed by full-pattern GSAS-II refinement. Comparable two-phase branches gave Rwp about 4.17% for both monoclinic and triclinic distorted-WO3 descriptions, about 7.11% for orthorhombic WO3, and about 10.84% for the Ca4.26W10O30 bronze intermediate. The minute numerical difference between the monoclinic and triclinic branches is not discriminating, so the ambient-stable monoclinic model was retained. Its final octahedra remain physical (W-O 1.703-2.163 A; minimum O-O 2.511 A); scheelite has W-O 1.795 A and Ca-O 2.422-2.453 A.

The refined crystalline-only fractions are 85.45 wt% CaWO4 and 14.55 wt% WO3. The full 8301-point replay gives Rwp = 3.638%, Rp = 2.766%, Rexp = 3.138%, and GOF = 1.159. The formal scale-covariance uncertainty is only 0.05 wt% absolute, but a more defensible uncertainty is roughly 1-2 wt% because profile, displacement-parameter, and weak oxygen-coordinate correlations dominate the formal counting-statistical error.

The WO3 oxygen coordinates are less secure than the phase identity: unconstrained refinement produced nonphysical short contacts and was rejected, while the submitted coordinates were kept within a geometry-screened neighborhood of the published parent. Monoclinic versus very slightly triclinic WO3 therefore cannot be established uniquely from this broadened laboratory pattern. Fractions are normalized only over modeled crystalline phases; without an internal standard, any amorphous calcium-bearing remainder and the absolute crystalline fraction of the specimen cannot be determined.
