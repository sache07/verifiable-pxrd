# Powder XRD phase analysis

|---|---|---|---:|
| CuWO4 | P-1 | 4.62299, 5.74943, 4.86115; 91.3245, 91.4797, 84.7020 | 65.068 |

| CuO | C2/c | 4.67234, 3.43252, 5.12099; beta 99.5661 | 2.763 |

Fractions are normalized over the four modeled crystalline phases; amorphous material is not quantified. The final full-grid replay gives Rwp = 2.593%, Rp = 1.796%, Rexp = 1.609%, and goodness of fit = 1.611 for 8,301 points.

## Evidence

CuWO4 is the principal phase and accounts for the dense reflection set across the full scan. Monoclinic WO3 is required by its characteristic low-angle triplet near 22.67, 23.07, and 23.89 degrees 2theta. The coherent family near 15.18, 19.80, 32.50, and 33.77 degrees, together with its higher-angle members, indexes to cubic Cu3WO6. The remaining CuO contribution is supported by its strongest C2/c reflections near the 35-38 degree region; its scale remains positive and stable in the four-phase model. All coherent peak families are assigned. The phase inventory gives a bulk Cu/W molar ratio of 1.026, close to the nominal 1:1 precursor ratio without invoking extra phases.

The WO3 model is a standard-cell transformation of physical neutron structure COD 2311041. It fits substantially better than the initially plausible COD 1528915 model, which was rejected because it contains a 1.58 Å O-O contact. The submitted structures pass distance screening: W-O ranges are 1.80-2.16 Å in CuWO4, 1.72-2.17 Å in WO3, and 1.78-2.10 Å in Cu3WO6; Cu-O distances are also chemically reasonable.

## Refinement and uncertainty

Scales, background, cells, specimen displacement, instrumental U/W/X, selected phase broadening, physically bounded March-Dollase terms, heavy-atom coordinates, and phase-wide isotropic displacement terms were staged and rolled back when unphysical. Light-atom trials were retained only for CuWO4; WO3 and Cu3WO6 oxygen refinements did not give a stable improvement over their physical reference coordinates.

Phase identities are robust. The minor CuO fraction is the least reliable because of its weak, overlapping peaks and mild texture sensitivity; its last decimal places should not be interpreted as accuracy. Systematic fraction uncertainty from overlap, texture, and profile choice is larger than counting-statistical errors. Exact oxygen coordinates in WO3, Cu3WO6, and CuO cannot be determined reliably from this X-ray pattern alone. WO3 and Cu3WO6 crystallite sizes reached the profile-resolution upper bound, so their exact sizes are likewise not determined. Remaining residuals are paired line-shape lobes around accepted reflections, especially low-angle Cu3WO6 peaks, rather than an unassigned peak family.
