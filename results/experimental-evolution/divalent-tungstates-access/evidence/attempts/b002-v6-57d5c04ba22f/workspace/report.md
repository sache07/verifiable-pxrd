# Powder XRD phase analysis

The crystalline model is 84.08 wt% wolframite-type ZnWO4 (P 2/c), 11.43 wt% monoclinic WO3 (P 21/c), and 4.49 wt% wurtzite ZnO (P 63mc). Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases; they do not measure amorphous material. The final refinement converged at Rwp = 10.952%, Rp = 7.219%, and goodness of fit = 4.144 (8301 points, 23 refined variables).

ZnWO4 is identified by the complete wolframite reflection sequence, including the low-angle lines near 15.8 and 19.2 degrees and the characteristic split families around 24–31 degrees 2theta. A scheelite model did not reproduce these families. Exact O-W-Zn references were withheld from the frozen index, so the atomic starting model was transferred from the nearest framework neighbour, P 2/c CoMoO4-II (COD 7205001), by Co-to-Zn and Mo-to-W substitution. Its cell and supported coordinates were refined with bounded displacements. The accepted geometry remains ordinary (minimum Zn-O 2.01 A, W-O 1.84 A, and O-O 2.52 A), with no short-contact flags.

The residual families at approximately 23.41, 23.96, 24.65, 28.91, 29.23, 33.60, and 34.49 degrees support room-temperature monoclinic WO3. COD 2311041 was used because it has physical WO6 geometry; a near-equivalent candidate with an implausible 1.57 A O-O contact was rejected despite a similar profile fit. Weaker coherent lines near 32.0, 34.9, 47.9, 56.8, and 69.2 degrees support residual wurtzite ZnO (COD 9004178). After all three phases were included, no coherent residual reflection family remained unindexed.

The intensity model required [111] preferred orientation for ZnWO4 and weaker, stable texture corrections for WO3 [100] and ZnO [001]. Surface roughness and uniaxial-strain alternatives either sought bounds, failed numerically, or worsened when constrained to physical values, and were not retained. The required staged coordinate polish selected a converged physical branch, improving Rwp by 0.027 percentage point while keeping fractions stable.

The phase identities and ZnWO4 topology are reliable. The WO3 and especially ZnO quantities have larger systematic uncertainty than their formal scale-factor errors because they are minor, overlap the dominant phase, and correlate with texture/profile terms. Their specimen-specific atomic-coordinate shifts could not be determined reliably, so published physical coordinates were retained. The ZnWO4 oxygen coordinates are also less certain than its cell/topology because the model is analogue-derived. The elevated goodness of fit partly reflects Poisson weights inferred from counts because pointwise experimental uncertainties were not supplied; remaining large residuals are centered on accepted reflections and are best interpreted as intensity/profile limitations rather than evidence for another crystalline phase.
