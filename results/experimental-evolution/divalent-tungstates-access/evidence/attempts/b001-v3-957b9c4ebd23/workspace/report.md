# Powder XRD phase analysis

The modeled crystalline product contains three phases:

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (Å, °) | Crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| ZnWO₄, wolframite | P 2/c | a = 4.70083, b = 5.72690, c = 4.93838, β = 90.5993° | 0.74859 |
| WO₃, monoclinic | P 2₁/c | a = 7.69199, b = 7.53553, c = 10.5208, β = 136.067° | 0.18318 |
| ZnO, wurtzite | P 6₃mc | a = b = 3.25476, c = 5.21961 | 0.06823 |

The dominant peak family is indexed by wolframite ZnWO₄. The coherent residual family at approximately 23.2–24.5°, 26.7–29.0°, and 33–34° 2θ is indexed by monoclinic WO₃. The remaining family at approximately 31.8°, 34.5°, 36.3°, 47.6°, 56.6°, and 62.8° is wurtzite ZnO. After refinement, no coherent unassigned reflection family remains; residuals are concentrated at reflections of these accepted phases and were treated as structural/profile effects rather than extra phases.

The chemistry independently supports this inventory. On a molar basis, the refined residual WO₃:ZnO ratio is about 0.94:1, and the total Zn:W ratio across all three phases is about 1.02:1. Both are close to the balance expected for an incompletely reacted 1:1 ZnO + WO₃ synthesis.

The final GSAS-II model includes the reported Cu Kα doublet, refined cells and phase scales, one common Uiso per phase, physically screened atomic coordinates, phase-dependent size/strain, preferred orientation for ZnWO₄ and WO₃, specimen displacement and surface roughness, and instrumental profile terms. A fresh native GSAS-II replay regenerated all reflections over the complete 8,301-point grid and gave Rwp = 2.7123% and Rp = 2.0948%. The replay had a positive background and no negative-width or structural-contact flags.

The phase identities are robust. Fractions are normalized only over the modeled crystalline material; an amorphous fraction cannot be obtained without an internal standard. Texture, surface-roughness and background correlations make realistic fraction uncertainties larger than the formal least-squares errors, approximately ±1 weight-percentage point for ZnWO₄ and ±0.5–1 point for each minor phase. The WO₃ framework and symmetry are reliable, but its individual oxygen coordinates are less certain than the W substructure because Cu X-ray scattering is W-dominated.
