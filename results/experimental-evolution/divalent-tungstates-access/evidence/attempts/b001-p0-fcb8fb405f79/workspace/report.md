# Crystalline phase analysis

The raw-count data provide no pointwise uncertainties; the refinement therefore used Poisson counting weights with sigma equal to the square root of intensity.

The fractions are semiquantitative because preferred orientation, surface roughness, and polarization are correlated. Light-atom coordinates in the dominant phase and reference coordinates in the weaker WO3 phase cannot be determined independently from this pattern. No amorphous fraction is included.

The pattern is best described by three crystalline phases: dominant wolframite ZnWO4, monoclinic WO3, and minor wurtzite ZnO. Their refined weight fractions, normalized only over these modeled crystalline phases, are 0.739, 0.193, and 0.068, respectively.

Assignment evidence comes from the complete peak series, not chemistry alone. The dominant phase accounts for the wolframite reflections near 15.6, 19.0, 23.9, 24.6, 30.5-30.8, 36.4, and 38.4 degrees 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for characteristic clusters near 23.2, 26.7, 28.7-29.0, and 33.4-34.3 degrees. Wurtzite ZnO is required by residual peaks near 31.8, 34.4, 36.2, 47.5, 56.6, and 62.8 degrees. These assignments are chemically consistent with the stated ZnO plus WO3 reaction at 700 degrees C.

The refined cells are: ZnWO4 (P 2/c), a = 4.70557, b = 5.73266, c = 4.94308 A, beta = 90.5989 degrees; WO3 (P 21/n), a = 7.30576, b = 7.54317, c = 7.69840 A, beta = 90.9181 degrees; and ZnO (P 63mc), a = 3.25643, c = 5.22802 A. The full-range fit has Rwp = 6.827%, Rp = 4.954%, Rexp = 2.264%, and goodness of fit = 3.015 for 8,301 points.
