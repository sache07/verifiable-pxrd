# Powder XRD phase analysis

The pattern is described by four crystalline phases: scheelite CaWO4, monoclinic WO3, CaO, and calcite CaCO3. The crystalline-normalized result is **65.0 wt% CaWO4, 25.0 wt% WO3, 3.0 wt% CaO, and 7.0 wt% calcite**. The combined GSAS-II model gives Rwp = 4.186% and Rp = 3.130% on all 8301 measured points (weights 1/max(counts,1)).

## Evidence

- CaWO4 accounts for the dominant scheelite reflection set, including the strong lines near 18.7, 28.8, and 31.6 degrees 2theta. Its refined tetragonal cell is a = 5.260756 A, c = 11.343807 A.
- The resolved group near 23.2-24.5 degrees and its higher-angle companions require monoclinic WO3; the COD 2311041 P21/c framework fitted substantially better than alternative WO3 settings. Its refined cell is a = 7.700447, b = 7.554236, c = 10.544737 A, beta = 136.09002 degrees.
- Weak residual peaks near 37.4 and 53.9 degrees are explained by rock-salt CaO. The otherwise missing broad intensity centered near 29.5 degrees, with companion calcite lines, identifies residual CaCO3. Adding both minor phases removes systematic difference peaks.
- The phase amounts satisfy the Ca/W chemical balance expected from CaCO3 + WO3 processing at 650 degrees C. Fractions were obtained from refined GSAS-II HAP scales and unit-cell masses, then normalized over the modeled crystalline phases.

## Uncertainty

The CaWO4 and WO3 identifications are robust. Their abundances are estimated to about +/-1.5 percentage points; the weak CaO and calcite amounts are less precise (roughly +/-0.6 and +/-1.0 points, respectively) because of overlap and line broadening. No amorphous fraction can be measured without an internal standard, so these values are crystalline-normalized only.

The framework, symmetry, and cells are reliable, but the data do not determine every light-atom displacement independently. The CaWO4 oxygen coordinate and the two strongly scattering WO3 W sites were refined within small physical bounds. Unstable WO3 oxygen and minor-phase coordinate shifts were retained close to their reference structures; those fine coordinates should not be interpreted as uniquely determined. Isotropic phase-wide displacement parameters and bounded size/strain/texture terms were used to avoid nonphysical overfitting.
