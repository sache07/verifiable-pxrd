# Powder XRD phase analysis

## Result

The crystalline pattern is explained by two residual precursor phases:

- witherite BaCO3, Pmcn (No. 62): **45.97 wt%**
- monoclinic WO3, P21/n (No. 14): **54.03 wt%**

Fractions are normalized over the modeled crystalline material. They do not quantify amorphous material. A practical uncertainty of about ±1 wt% is more realistic than the much smaller least-squares scale errors because scale, surface roughness, profile shape and light-atom coordinates are correlated.

## Evidence and refinement

Reference structures COD 9010928 (witherite) and COD 1528915 (monoclinic WO3) account for all coherent peaks in a two-phase Rietveld fit. The WO3 low-angle triplet at about 23.42, 23.87 and 24.64° 2theta, the BaCO3-dominant feature at 24.10°, and the phase-specific groups near 33.5–34.7°, 42.1–45.1°, 50.2° and 56.2° are reproduced together. The strongest scheelite BaWO4 line expected near 26.5–26.7° is absent, and adding a tungstate phase was not justified by coherent residual peaks.

The final fit uses the supplied Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, Poisson count weights, a refined polynomial background, phase scales and cells, isotropic size/profile terms, polarization, zero offset and a profiled Bragg-Brentano specimen displacement. Fit statistics are Rwp = 2.1471%, Rp = 1.6604%, Rexp = 1.8383% and GOF = 1.1696 for 8301 points.

## Remaining uncertainty

The phase identities and approximately 46:54 mass ratio are robust; that ratio also agrees with unreacted 1:1-metal BaCO3/WO3 dosing. Atomic shifts were kept close to the published structures because an unrestricted refinement overfit correlated oxygen coordinates. Consequently the structure topology, cells and heavy-atom sites are defensible, but individual oxygen displacements and isotropic displacement parameters cannot be determined reliably from this powder pattern and should not be interpreted as a new high-precision structure determination. The zero offset and specimen displacement are likewise highly correlated. The short O–O contact intrinsic to this historical monoclinic WO3 model remains a crystallographic-model caveat, not evidence for an additional phase.

No unindexed crystalline phase is evident above the residual/noise level; a trace phase with strongly overlapping peaks cannot be excluded reliably.
