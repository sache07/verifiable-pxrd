# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is described by three crystalline Ca–W–O phases. Weight fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| Scheelite CaWO4 | I41/a, COD 7219229 | 69.06(11) wt% |
| Monoclinic WO3 | P21/c, COD 2311041 | 16.90(7) wt% |
| Ordered double-perovskite Ca3WO6 | space-group no. 14; Ca-mapped Sr2CaWO6 parent, COD 2104688 | 14.04(9) wt% |

The parenthetical uncertainties are covariance-derived one-sigma values from the final refinement. The fit has Rwp = 3.3224%, Rp = 2.6401%, GOF = 1.2767, and Rmin = 2.6169% for 8,301 observations.

## Evidence and model selection

Scheelite CaWO4 accounts for the dominant family of sharp reflections. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the coherent residual family around 23.0–24.3 degrees 2theta and its higher-angle partners. Ca3WO6 is required by a separate multi-line family including features near 18.7, 22.0, 30.7, 36.5–37.5, 45.1, 50.7, and 56.9 degrees. Removing CaWO4, WO3, or Ca3WO6 raises Rwp to approximately 43.0%, 12.1%, and 9.0%, respectively.

For WO3, the final fixed-coordinate matched comparison favored COD 2311041 (Rwp 3.3976%, BIC 14823) over the credible triclinic COD 2106293 source (Rwp 3.4274%, BIC 15079) after the triclinic branch was independently restaged to a physical, converged solution.

No exact Ca3WO6 entry was available in the screened source set. Four low-symmetry ordered-double-perovskite parents (Ca2MgWO6, Ca2CoWO6, Ca2NiWO6, and Sr2CaWO6) converged to the same physical framework after equal coordinate treatment. The Sr2CaWO6-derived model was retained because it has the nearest cation chemistry and requires only Sr-to-Ca mapping. Higher-symmetry mapped parents fitted substantially worse. A CaO trial refined to only 0.11 wt% and worsened BIC by 19, so it was excluded.

The final background is positive throughout, all peak-width/domain parameters are physical, and no variables are frozen. Independent low and high nuisance starts converged to the same calculated curve, cells, and fractions. An independent replay of `final.gpx` changed the largest weight fraction by only 8.4e-8 and reproduced Rwp within 0.00002 percentage point.

## Remaining uncertainty

The largest residuals are alternating peak-shape errors at already modeled strong reflections, especially CaWO4 near 18.47 and 28.57 degrees; no coherent unassigned multi-line family remains. Thus an additional crystalline phase cannot be identified reliably. The Ca3WO6 topology and composition are well supported, but its exact local Ca ordering and oxygen coordinates are analogue-derived and are less certain than the phase identity. The quoted fraction uncertainties are formal refinement errors and do not include fixed-coordinate/ADP or source-model systematics. No amorphous fraction can be determined without an internal standard, so the reported fractions must not be interpreted as absolute whole-sample fractions.
