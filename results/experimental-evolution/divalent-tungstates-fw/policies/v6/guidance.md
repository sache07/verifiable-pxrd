# Inventory-first, structure-recovering PXRD refinement

Treat only the public pattern and metadata as evidence. The generator, frozen cases, scorer, and laboratory distributions describe this training system, not the unknown sample. Never use hidden mechanisms, ranges, coordinates, effect axes, or expected values as priors, bounds, targets, or reasons to add terms.

Normalize and physically screen every serious CIF before refinement. A lower Rwp never justifies a review-trigger source when a passing source exists. Build the crystalline inventory from coherent multi-line residual families and chemistry, initially keeping coordinates fixed. For a missing composition, map sensible elements into several plausible frameworks, including ordered multication parents whose distinct sites may collapse onto one product element. Preserve composition- and topology-nearest parents despite poor unadapted sticks, cells, scale, or early Rwp; adapt cells and compare restrained whole-pattern fits. Preserve provenance and refine, rather than impose, fractions. After inventory changes, repeat residual and deletion tests for trace assignments.

Inventory closure precedes expensive source and coordinate comparisons. If coherent unassigned line families remain well above counting noise, return to chemistry-guided phase search. Low-flexibility source comparisons create shortlists, not final decisions. Preserve near-tied same-composition sources for a final matched swap. Carry several credible mapped-framework parents through completed cumulative restarts; bound-hitting, collapsed, or nonconvergent branches are inconclusive and must be restaged from lower flexibility.

Use only enough nuisance flexibility to stabilize inventory and source comparisons: a positive moderate background, scales and cells, alternative angular branches, common peak shape, and simple per-phase broadening. Screen roughness, polarization, asymmetry, and plausible texture, but do not let high background order, directional broadening, or texture substitute for systematic intensity residuals at modeled reflections.

Once inventory closes, perform structure recovery before escalating nuisance complexity. For every phase with systematic modeled-reflection intensity errors or inherited/mismatched coordinates, normalize displacement parameters and test all defensible symmetry-independent coordinate blocks. Refine chemically connected groups—central sites with their ligand framework, or coupled ligand orbits—using soft distance/angle restraints or damped staged release. An isolated-site move can create a transient bad contact because its neighbors are fixed; retry it in the connected block at reduced nuisance flexibility before rejection. Do not stop after the first successful block.

Compare coordinate solutions from the literature parent, alternative credible lineages, and small physically plausible perturbed starts under the same moderate nuisance model. Release blocks cumulatively, re-equilibrating scales, cells, and simple profiles. Accept motion only when multiple starts converge to the same physical structure and curve with a predictive residual improvement. Restage bound or metric failures; reject solutions whose coordinates, fractions, or curve depend on the nuisance start. Give every credible mapped analogue finalist this same treatment before choosing provenance.

After coordinates stabilize, add remaining nuisance categories one at a time and then retest coordinates. Retain high-order background, anisotropic broadening, texture, or correlated angular/profile subspaces only when their residual signature is clear and independent starts reproduce the curve, structure, and fractions without bounds or freezing. Prefer the lowest complexity with equivalent predictive behavior.

Before submission, remove unsupported phases, rerun deletion and physical/project screens, and independently replay `final.gpx`. Its positive phases, CIFs, fractions, and `fit.csv` must agree.
