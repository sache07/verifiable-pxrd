# PXRD phase analysis

The crystalline model contains scheelite CaWO4, monoclinic WO3, and a calcium-rich Ca3WO6 phase. Their GSAS-II quantitative phase fractions, normalized over these modeled crystalline phases, are 78.63, 11.44, and 9.93 wt%, respectively. The full-range final replay converged at Rwp = 3.348%, Rp = 2.638%, Rexp = 2.247%, and GOF = 1.492; its last maximum shift/esd was 0.0054.

CaWO4 is established by its coherent 101, 112/103, 004, 200, 204, 312, and higher-angle families. WO3 is required by the split peaks near 22.94, 23.42, and 24.23 degrees 2theta and corresponding split families near 33--34 and 41--42 degrees. With an otherwise identical nuisance model, the selected neutron-derived P21/c WO3 record gave Rwp = 3.343% before the final range extension, compared with 3.489% for a lower-symmetry triclinic record despite its additional cell freedoms; orthorhombic WO3 was poorer still.

The third phase was initially revealed by one coherent residual family at approximately 18.7, 19.3, 22.0, 30.6, 31.4, 32.1, 36.5, 37.5, 45.0, and 57.0 degrees. No exact Ca3WO6 entry was present, so `learned/stoichiometric_analogues.py` was used. A P21/n Ca3OsO6 parent (COD 1562666) matched all these families and permits the isovalent Os6+ to W6+ substitution. The refined Ca3WO6 cell is a = 5.53267, b = 5.78136, c = 8.01189 A, beta = 90.0688 degrees; its retained prototype geometry is physical (six W--O distances of 1.922--1.939 A). Alternative Ca3UO6 and other stoichiometric parents did not match the same family set.

The retained source models are ordered and fully occupied. The CaWO4 parent is a 298 K scheelite record, the WO3 parent is a neutron-derived monoclinic record, and the Ca3OsO6 prototype was measured at 293 K (although its crystals were grown under pressure). Geometry checks after cell refinement gave tetrahedral W--O = 1.762 A in CaWO4, distorted octahedral W--O = 1.713--2.157 A in WO3, and no implausibly short contacts in any phase.

The final baseline uses the reported Cu K-alpha doublet, a six-coefficient smooth Chebyshev background, one zero correction (-0.20426 degrees), positive common U/W/X terms, and an effective polarization of 0.87164. Supported sample terms were isotropic sizes for CaWO4 (0.0872 micrometres) and Ca3WO6 (0.3289 micrometres), plus a CaWO4 [110] March-Dollase term (1.2666) supported across low-, middle-, and high-angle families. SH/L, extra strain, size-shape mixing, and WO3 texture were pruned after bound, negative, or negligible replay results.

CaO, Ca(OH)2, calcite, a Ca--W bronze, and high-pressure fergusonite were re-challenged under the final common profile. Each refined below about 0.45 wt% with mixed-sign support at its strongest families and only cosmetic Rwp changes; fergusonite also comes from an 11.3 GPa source. They are not included. This leaves an approximate sub-0.5 wt% practical detection uncertainty for such weak phases.

The listed fraction esds are formal GSAS-II values based on Poisson count weights because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied. Preferred orientation, the uncalibrated instrumental profile, and model choice make practical fraction uncertainties larger than those formal esds. In particular, the Ca3WO6 identity and cell are strongly supported, but its atomic coordinates are a chemically justified prototype substitution and were not independently determined from this powder pattern. No amorphous content can be inferred, so all fractions are conditional on the modeled crystalline inventory.
