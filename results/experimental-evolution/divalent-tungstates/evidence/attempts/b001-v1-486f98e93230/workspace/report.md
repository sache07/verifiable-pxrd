# Phase analysis and refinement report

## Result

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases. Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase and structural model | Space group | Weight fraction |
|---|---:|---:|
| CaWO4, scheelite (COD 7219229) | I 41/a | 79.27(9)% |
| WO3, monoclinic (COD 2311041) | P 21/c | 10.75(5)% |
| Ca3WO6, double-perovskite model adapted from Ca3OsO6 (COD 1562666) | P 21/n | 9.99(8)% |

The final full-range fit has Rwp = 4.617%, Rp = 3.419%, and goodness of fit = 2.058 for 8,301 observations and 29 refined variables. The fitted phase assemblage gives a bulk Ca:W molar ratio of 1.010, consistent with the CaO/WO3 precursor chemistry.

## Evidence and candidate selection

Ambient scheelite CaWO4 accounts for the dominant reflection family. Its final cell is a = 5.24232 Å and c = 11.37615 Å. A malformed alternative record with problematic occupancies and a high-pressure CaWO4 polymorph were rejected before refinement.

The residual triplet near 22.95, 23.42, and 24.23 degrees identifies ambient monoclinic WO3. Several WO3 records and polymorphs were compared with the same modest background, scale, zero, cell, and width model. COD 2311041 gave the best physically acceptable model. A statistically tied P 21/c record was rejected because it contained an implausible approximately 1.575 Å O–O contact; tested triclinic and orthorhombic alternatives fit materially worse.

The two-phase model left a coherent family near 18.74, 19.29, 22.02, 30.72–32.16, 37.53, 45.15, and 57.02 degrees. No exact Ca3WO6 entry was available, so a reduced-stoichiometry analogue search was used. The ambient P 21/n Ca3OsO6 parent (COD 1562666) was physically sound and was adapted by the chemically conservative isovalent Os6+ to W6+ substitution while retaining the parent coordinates. Adding it reduced Rwp from 14.89% to 12.15% before phase-specific broadening or texture was introduced, and its scale remained positive after full reconvergence. The final exported structures have full occupancies and reasonable geometry: W–O = 1.762 Å in CaWO4, 1.713–2.157 Å in WO3, and 1.923–1.939 Å in Ca3WO6.

A rocksalt CaO challenge was reconverged both with a refined cell and with its source cell fixed. It returned only about 0.14(6) wt% and improved Rwp by approximately 0.0015 percentage point; moreover, its strongest independent line near 37.1 degrees was overpredicted. CaO was therefore not retained. A trace below roughly 0.2 wt% remains possible but cannot be identified or quantified reliably from this pattern.

## Refinement model and uncertainty

The reported Cu K-alpha wavelengths and K-alpha2/K-alpha1 ratio were fixed. A single constant zero correction (-0.20361 degrees) was selected over specimen displacement because the observed angular offsets were nearly constant. The common analytic width uses only a positive W term; U, V, X, Y, and Z are zero, so the width remains nonnegative over the fitted range. The effective polarization refined within physical bounds to 0.8398. After the inventory stabilized, only supported phase terms were retained: modest CaWO4 [001] March–Dollase texture (r = 0.8936), CaWO4 isotropic size broadening (44.3 nm), and Ca3WO6 isotropic microstrain (4,255 ppm). WO3 phase broadening, anisotropic broadening, extinction, hydrostatic strain, and extra asymmetry were unsupported and fixed off. Atomic coordinates were not refined; phase scales and cells were refined.

The tabulated uncertainties are GSAS-II covariance estimates and are smaller than the likely model/systematic uncertainty because no calibrated instrument profile, pointwise uncertainties, or internal standard was supplied. The presence of the Ca3WO6 peak family is well supported, but its detailed coordinates and even its exact structure are less secure than those of the two exact-composition database phases because the model is prototype-derived. Its fraction is consequently model-dependent. Amorphous content and absolute crystalline yield cannot be determined; all fractions are relative to the modeled crystalline material. No further coherent unmatched reflection family remains, although small profile-shape residuals persist around the strongest peaks.
