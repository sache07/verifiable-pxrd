# Crystalline phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized to these modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (angstrom, degrees) | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| SrWO4, scheelite | I 41/a (No. 88) | a = 5.38332, c = 11.87961 | 57.19 wt.% |
| WO3, monoclinic | P 21/n (No. 14) | a = 7.27616, b = 7.54180, c = 7.68043, beta = 90.8882 | 27.40 wt.% |
| SrCO3, strontianite | P n m a (No. 62) | a = 6.01252, b = 5.08892, c = 8.37974 | 15.41 wt.% |

The full-range Rietveld fit (9.903-100.095 degrees 2-theta) has Rp = 2.989%, Rwp = 3.885%, Rexp = 2.861%, and goodness of fit = 1.360.

## Evidence

The chemistry is consistent with partial reaction of the stated WO3 and SrCO3 precursors at 650 degrees C to form SrWO4. The scheelite assignment jointly accounts for its characteristic measured peaks at approximately 17.456, 27.203, 29.441, 32.636, 44.817, and 47.132 degrees 2-theta. Monoclinic WO3 is required by the 22.519/22.943/23.834-degree triplet and features near 33.603, 40.862, and 41.307 degrees. Orthorhombic SrCO3 accounts for peaks near 24.649, 25.290, 25.975, 36.070, 43.665, and 45.198 degrees. These are measured positions before the refined -0.63693-degree zero correction. No additional phase was supported by a reproducible set of unmatched Bragg peaks.

The Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and intensity ratio were fixed to the metadata. The refinement included a common zero correction, phase scales and cells, background, phase-specific preferred orientation, and WO3 size broadening. The fitted WO3 coherent-domain parameter is about 0.166 micrometres, but it should not be interpreted as a calibrated particle size. Reference atomic coordinates were retained: COD 1528915 for monoclinic WO3, COD 9008198 for SrCO3, and a standard scheelite model for SrWO4. Atomic-coordinate refinement, especially for oxygen, was not supported reliably by this laboratory pattern, so the CIF coordinates are reference-constrained structural models rather than a new structure solution.

## Remaining uncertainty

The formal GSAS-II fraction e.s.d.s are only 0.16-0.18 wt. percentage points, but they exclude model systematics. There is no measured instrument-profile standard, the specimen has measurable preferred orientation, the zero correction is large, and W-rich phases can have appreciable absorption/microabsorption contrast. A realistic interpretation is therefore roughly +/-2-3 absolute wt.% for SrWO4 and WO3 and +/-1-2 wt.% for SrCO3. These fractions do not measure amorphous material because no internal standard was supplied. Trace crystalline phases below the pattern's sensitivity cannot be excluded. The phase identities and unit-cell types are robust; light-atom coordinates, coherent-domain size, and the last few percent of the phase fractions are less certain.
