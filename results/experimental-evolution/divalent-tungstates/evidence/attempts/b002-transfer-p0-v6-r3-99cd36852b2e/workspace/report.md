# Phase analysis and Rietveld refinement

The pattern is modeled as three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases only.

|---|---|---|---:|

| WO3 | Monoclinic gamma-WO3, P2(1)/n (No. 14) | a = 7.30039 Å, b = 7.53954 Å, c = 7.68537 Å, beta = 90.8653° | 34.59 wt% |
| MgO | Periclase, Fm-3m (No. 225) | a = 4.20961 Å | 8.32 wt% |

The covariance-only standard uncertainties are 0.26, 0.19, and 0.35 wt% for MgWO4, WO3, and MgO, respectively. These are smaller than the realistic systematic uncertainty; because no calibrated instrument profile, internal standard, particle-size information, or microabsorption correction was available, an absolute uncertainty of roughly 2–4 wt% per phase is more defensible. Amorphous material, if present, is not measured by these normalized fractions.

## Evidence

MgWO4 accounts for the intense 18.88° peak and the families at approximately 15.56°, 23.82°, 30.41/30.75°, 36.30°, 40.96°, and 54.19° 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 is required by the characteristic 23.13/23.57/24.38° triplet and lines near 26.58°, 28.93°, 33.28–34.16°, 41.87°, 49.95°, and 55.94°. MgO is independently identified by its rocksalt reflections, especially those near 42.93° and 62.26°, with the weaker high-angle series also present. These assignments are chemically consistent with reaction of the stated MgO and WO3 precursors at 700 °C.

Candidate hexagonal, tetragonal, orthorhombic, monoclinic, and triclinic WO3 reference patterns were compared. The monoclinic model reproduces the observed triplet and full high-angle sequence. A low-temperature triclinic trial did not provide robust evidence for the extra distortion: its refined alpha and gamma angles moved markedly toward 90 degrees and its unconstrained profile required a negative high-angle Lorentzian term. The physically constrained, more parsimonious monoclinic model was therefore retained. Residual maxima in the submitted fit coincide with reflections of the three modeled phases rather than forming a separate unexplained peak family.

Starting coordinates were taken from COD 9013902 (MgWO4), COD 2311041 (WO3, transformed to the conventional near-orthogonal P2(1)/n setting), and COD 9000499 (MgO). Unit cells, phase scales, background, zero offset, and profile terms were refined. A single mild March–Dollase correction for MgWO4 (100), ratio 0.878, was needed for the pressed reflection specimen. Atomic coordinates were kept at the reference values: unconstrained coordinate trials were correlated with preferred orientation and changed weakly constrained oxygen positions implausibly, so coordinate refinement cannot be claimed reliably from this overlapping laboratory pattern. The zero-valued WO3 displacement parameters in its CIF are reference placeholders, not measured thermal parameters.

## Fit and limitations

The refinement used the complete 9.9034–100.0946° range, the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, and Poisson approximations sigma = sqrt(counts) because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied. Final statistics are Rp = 4.65%, Rwp = 7.28%, Rexp = 1.53%, and goodness of fit = 4.78 for 8,301 observations and 27 refined parameters. The elevated goodness of fit reflects systematic profile/intensity errors relative to ideal counting statistics; it is why covariance-only fraction errors should not be treated as total uncertainty.

The three phase identities are reliable. The WO3 monoclinic assignment is favored, though a very small unresolved triclinic distortion is harder to exclude than the phase chemistry itself. The normalized crystalline fractions are useful semiquantitative estimates, but absolute phase amounts and amorphous content cannot be determined reliably from this dataset alone. A minor additional phase below roughly 1–2 wt%, especially if its peaks overlap these phases, cannot be excluded.
