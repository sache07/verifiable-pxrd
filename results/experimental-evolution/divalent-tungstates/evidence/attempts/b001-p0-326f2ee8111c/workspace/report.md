# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is reliably described by one crystalline phase: scheelite calcium tungstate, CaWO4, in space group I 41/a (No. 88). This is chemically consistent with the CaO + WO3 precursor pair heated at 900 °C. The diagnostic reflections at approximately 18.61, 28.70, 29.03, 31.42, 34.16, 39.17, and 47.08° 2theta, as well as the higher-angle reflection set, are all indexed by this structure. The neighboring peaks at 28.70 and 29.03° are the scheelite (112) and (103) reflections, not separate phases.

The final full-range GSAS-II Rietveld fit gives Rwp = 2.802%, Rp = 2.133%, Rexp = 2.281%, GOF = 1.228, and RF = 2.193%. The refined tetragonal cell is a = 5.24907(4) Å and c = 11.38642(7) Å. The modeled crystalline-phase fraction is therefore CaWO4 = 1.0000 (100.00 wt% after normalization over modeled crystalline phases).

CaO, monoclinic/triclinic WO3, and Ca3WO6 were tested as possible secondary phases. Ca3WO6 refined to zero. A CaO trial gave only 0.167(81) wt% and negligible fit improvement. Different WO3 models gave roughly 0.10–0.19 wt%, but the value depended on the chosen polymorph and their strongest diagnostic peaks were not coherently present in the residual. These sub-0.2 wt% terms are below reliable phase-identification and quantification limits here, so they are not included in the submitted phase model. A trace of residual WO3 at about this level cannot be excluded.

No internal standard was supplied, so 100 wt% is a normalization among modeled crystalline phases and does not establish absolute crystallinity or exclude amorphous material. The instrument profile was not calibrated; effective polarization, Gaussian profile terms, and specimen displacement were therefore fitted empirically. Atomic displacement parameters were fixed to the starting reference values because they correlate strongly with the unknown effective polarization. The oxygen coordinates and cell are conditional on that model; the phase identity and one-phase bulk composition are much more secure than fine structural or trace-level conclusions.
