# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

Three crystalline phases are required by the pattern:

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| WO₃ | triclinic P-1, pseudo-monoclinic metric | 59.70 wt% |
| Cu₃WO₆ | cubic Pa-3 | 33.40 wt% |
| CuO | monoclinic C2/c | 6.91 wt% |

The fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases. The final fit over 10–100° 2θ has Rp = 3.413%, Rwp = 4.480%, Rexp = 2.410%, and GOF = 1.862. The refinement converged without an SVD singularity (final maximum shift/s.u. 0.38).

## Evidence and model

WO₃ accounts for the characteristic low-angle triplet at approximately 23.21, 23.65, and 24.46° and its higher-angle series. The unusually strong first member required a [001] March–Dollase preferred-orientation correction (refined ratio 0.7179). Matched refinements favored the P-1 WO₃ model (Rwp 4.48%) over monoclinic P2₁/n (5.57%) and orthorhombic Pnma (17.49%). Its refined cell is a = 7.29458, b = 7.53773, c = 7.68261 Å, β = 90.9195°. The weakly determined α and γ deviations were constrained to 90° for stability.

Cu₃WO₆ is identified by its distinct Pa-3 series at about 15.76, 20.37, 33.08, 34.31, 36.81, 58.19, 63.88, 70.80, and 87.65°; its refined cubic cell is a = 9.77715 Å. CuO is supported by the residual series near 35.7, 38.9, 49.0, 53.6, 66.8, and 89.7° after fitting the two tungsten-containing phases. A simulated CuWO₄ reference pattern was rejected because its expected strong cluster around 29.5–32° is absent at the required relative intensity.

Reference atomic coordinates and occupancies were retained; lattice constants, phase scales, zero offset, common line profile, background, and WO₃ texture were refined. Count-based Poisson weights were used because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied.

## Remaining uncertainty

The WO₃ phase is secure, but its P-1 versus very closely related monoclinic description is a subtle distinction; the data favor the lower-symmetry reflection/intensity pattern but do not reliably resolve tiny α/γ departures from 90°. The CuO cell and fraction are less precise because it is minor and its strongest peaks overlap other phases. Covariance-only fraction uncertainties are about 0.1 wt% and are unrealistically small; comparison of reasonable profile/polymorph models suggests approximately ±2 wt% absolute systematic uncertainty. No amorphous content can be quantified without an internal standard, so the reported fractions apply only to modeled crystalline material.
