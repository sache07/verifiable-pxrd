# Phase analysis and refinement

The pattern is modeled by three crystalline phases: **73.05 wt% wolframite ZnWO4**, **18.27 wt% monoclinic WO3**, and **8.69 wt% wurtzite ZnO**. Fractions are normalized over these crystalline phases.

The assignments are independently supported by coherent peak families. ZnWO4 accounts for 18.9°, 30.4/30.7°, 38.3°, 51.7°, and the 64–65° group; WO3 gives the diagnostic 23.17/23.62/24.40° triplet together with 26.6°, 28.6/29.0°, and 33–34° families; ZnO is identified by 31.79°, 34.46°, 47.57°, 56.60°, 62.87°, and 69.09°. All material positive residual maxima coincide with modeled reflections, so no fourth crystalline phase is supported.

Full-occupancy, ordered literature structures were used and their cells refined. The models are COD 1520641 (ZnWO4, P 2/c), COD 2311041 (neutron WO3, P 21/c), and COD 9004178 (ZnO, P 63mc). Their final geometries remain physical: Zn–O 2.03–2.23 Å and W–O 1.80–2.14 Å in ZnWO4, W–O 1.72–2.16 Å in WO3, and Zn–O 1.98–1.99 Å in ZnO. A triclinic WO3 record was re-refined under the same final profile and fit worse (Rwp 3.125% versus 3.094%); ambient monoclinic WO3 is therefore retained. An older monoclinic WO3 record was rejected because its geometry contained a 1.57 Å O–O contact and it fit worse.

The final baseline uses a smooth six-term Chebyshev background, one zero correction, fixed Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and 0.5 ratio, and a nonnegative common W-only Gaussian width (U, V, X, and Y neutral). Effective polarization and SH/L asymmetry were supported by coherent angle-dependent and repeated line-shape residuals. Isotropic size broadening was stable for all phases; complementary isotropic strain was retained only for WO3. Negative strain/U trials, unsupported X, bound or negligible profile-mixing terms, and preferred-orientation trials along symmetry-distinct axes were pruned. The final full-range replay converged with Rwp = 3.094%, Rp = 2.442%, Rexp = 2.765%, and GOF = 1.121.

The formal one-sigma fraction uncertainties are 0.16, 0.10, and 0.14 wt%-point for ZnWO4, WO3, and ZnO, respectively. A more realistic model-dependent uncertainty is about ±1 wt%-point for ZnWO4 and WO3 and ±0.5 wt%-point for ZnO because of peak overlap and microstructure/profile choices. Atomic coordinates and occupancies were not independently refined, so subtle defects cannot be determined reliably. An amorphous fraction or absolute bulk phase fraction also cannot be determined reliably without an internal standard.
