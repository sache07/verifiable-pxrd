## Evidence and model selection

BaWO₄ is established by its dominant scheelite sequence, including 26.685° (112), 32.040° (020), 43.190° (024), 48.980° (116), and 53.682° (312/132). Monoclinic WO₃ is required by the resolved 23.400/23.857/24.664° and 33.539/34.455° multiplets. Ba₃W₂O₉ contributes coherent partly unique families at 26.258°, 30.811°, 37.653°, 40.788°, 51.842°, 63.073/63.831°, and 88.592°. Witherite BaCO₃ is supported by the 24.167/24.548° pair and matching higher-angle families at 34.340/34.843°, 42.270°, 45.128°, and 47.020°. Adding BaCO₃ gave a strongly positive, replay-stable scale; remaining positive residual maxima coincide with reflections of these four phases rather than a coherent fifth phase.

The exact BaWO₄ database record screened initially contains half-occupied split oxygen alternatives and implausible short contacts. An ordered scheelite EuWO₄ parent (COD 1561850) was therefore found by the requested stoichiometric-analogue search and used with the isovalent Eu²⁺→Ba²⁺ substitution; its refined BaWO₄ geometry has W–O = 1.860 Å and Ba–O = 2.72 Å. For WO₃, the physically poor 1960 record was replaced by the 1966 neutron model (COD 2311041), which both improved the fit and gives complete WO₆ octahedra with W–O = 1.71–2.16 Å. Ba₃W₂O₉ uses COD 1533878. BaCO₃ uses COD 1011149 transformed into the GSAS-II-compatible *Pmcn* setting; this regular model was preferred to an alternate record with an anomalous 1.41 Å C–O contact.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The replay-stable baseline uses the specified Cu Kα₁/Kα₂ doublet, a smooth six-term Chebyshev background, one zero correction (+0.27985°), nonnegative U/W/X profile terms, fixed polarization 0.7, and one low-angle roughness term. WO₃ needs complementary isotropic size (0.310(54) µm) and strain (1332(286) µstrain); Ba₃W₂O₉ and BaCO₃ need isotropic strains of 1513(72) and 6803(450) µstrain. Size terms for the other phases collapsed. SH/L, a second roughness term, free polarization, anisotropic broadening, and tested March–Dollase axes were rejected because they were correlated, unsupported, or improved only isolated peak clusters. All accepted terms survived save/reopen full-range replay.

Atomic coordinates and displacement parameters were fixed to the screened literature/prototype models; only cells, scales, and profile terms were refined. Thus the phase identities and cells are supported, but the atomic structures were not independently solved from this laboratory pattern. WO₃ size and strain are correlated (0.87), and its nonstandard-cell metric parameters are also correlated (0.88), so their individual formal errors should be treated cautiously. Fractions may also carry unmodeled microabsorption, specimen, and structural-model systematics; the formal errors are lower bounds. No external standard was supplied, so amorphous content cannot be quantified and the reported fractions must not be interpreted as whole-sample absolute fractions.
