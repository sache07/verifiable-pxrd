# Phase analysis and refinement report

## Result

The modeled crystalline material is single-phase scheelite CaWO4 (space group I 41/a, No. 88). Its weight fraction is 1.0000 after normalization over the modeled crystalline phases. This normalization does not measure amorphous content or prove absolute chemical purity.

The full 9.9034–100.0946° 2theta range was refined with the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. Final statistics are Rwp = 2.3133%, Rp = 1.7755%, Rexp = 2.2809%, goodness of fit = 1.0142, and Bragg RF = 2.1281%.

## Evidence and structural model

All resolved families are indexed by scheelite, including 18.61° (101), 28.70° (112), 29.03° (103), 31.42° (004), 34.16° (200), 47.08° (204), and the higher-angle families through 100°. In particular, the 29.03° feature is the scheelite 103 reflection rather than evidence for another phase. A room-temperature CaWO4 record (COD 7219229) supplied the starting topology; the refined model has a = 5.248685(33) Å, c = 11.385591(62) Å, and V = 313.6582(45) Å3. The refined oxygen site is (0.1541(4), 0.0106(3), 0.20843(16)), with Uiso = 0.0141(8) Å2. It gives four W–O distances of 1.771 Å and eight Ca–O distances of 2.464–2.474 Å, which are physically reasonable for scheelite CaWO4.

The six-coefficient Chebyshev background remains smooth and positive. One zero correction, -0.00259(11)°, was used. The fitted effective polarization, 0.6915(20), is physically consistent with a Cu laboratory source and removed the coherent low-to-high-angle intensity trend. The nonnegative common profile has U = 38.70(54), W = 33.723(169), and X = 0.983(31); representative calculated FWHM values increase smoothly from 0.144° at 18.6° to 0.165° at 57.8° and 0.209° at 90.5°. Low-, middle-, and high-angle residual means are all small relative to counting noise.

Unsupported nuisance terms were pruned. Refined SH/L was strongly correlated with zero and did not improve the fit reliably. Surface roughness became singular/unphysical. Preferred-orientation trials along [001], [100], [110], [101], and [112] lacked support across multiple isolated families. Isotropic crystallite size is exactly degenerate with instrument X for this single-phase, uncalibrated pattern; strain became negative/insignificant when X was present. Consequently, size, strain, roughness, texture, and extra asymmetry are neutral in the submitted project.

## Alternate-phase screen and uncertainty

Unreacted CaO, several ambient monoclinic/triclinic WO3 records, Ca3WO6, and high-pressure fergusonite CaWO4 were re-challenged under the same final profile. Ca3WO6 and fergusonite scales were below 0.1 standard deviation, and the strong unique Ca3WO6 and WO3 low-angle families were absent. Valid WO3 alternatives were below 1.9 standard deviations and at most about 0.06 wt%; one malformed WO3 database record was rejected as a record, while the physical alternate records supplied the phase-level test.

CaO is the only marginal watch-list candidate: its scale corresponds to approximately 0.16(7) wt% (2.4 standard deviations), but its strongest unique peaks add only 9–22 counts against about 40-count point noise, its higher-angle lines are not coherent, and its inclusion changes Rwp by less than 0.001 percentage point. It is therefore not modeled as a reliably determined phase. A rough 95% upper bound is about 0.3 wt% CaO under this model.

Because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied, Poisson counting weights were inferred from the original counts. Formal standard uncertainties therefore omit possible instrumental and model systematics. The reported 100% CaWO4 fraction is conditional on the retained crystalline inventory; trace phases below the stated evidence level and amorphous material cannot be quantified from this pattern alone.
