## Evidence and structural models

The dominant peak families index coherently as P2/c wolframite. No exact MnWO₄ record was present in the supplied index, so the required stoichiometric-analogue search was used. Ordered MgWO₄ (COD 9013902) was substituted Mg→Mn because Mg²⁺ and Mn²⁺ occupy the same octahedral wolframite site; ordered ZnWO₄ and FeWO₄ parents and C2/m, scheelite, and triclinic alternatives were challenged under the same nuisance model. The Mg-derived model gave the best physical fit (the Zn-derived model was close), while the non-wolframite alternatives were poor or collapsed to zero scale. The staged coordinate test subsequently moved Mn and W by only 0.044 and 0.023 Å.

Residual families required WO₃. Ambient monoclinic P2₁/c WO₃ (COD 2311041) was retained over a triclinic record: the latter lowered Rwp by only 0.045 percentage point before coordinate completion while adding cell freedom. Both W sites passed the staged coordinate test with moves of 0.058 and 0.077 Å. Remaining distinct/partly overlapped lines, including the isolated hausmannite 103 family near 31.76°, support I4₁/amd Mn₃O₄ (COD 9001963); an alternate hausmannite record was nearly equivalent, whereas Pbcm Mn₃O₄ collapsed to zero scale.

The final heavy-site geometry is physical: minimum interatomic distances are 1.786 Å (MnWO₄), 1.706 Å (WO₃), and 1.931 Å (Mn₃O₄), with no GSAS-II short-contact warnings. The fresh reopen/replay retained all accepted coordinate gains and had no frozen variables.

## Fit and profile adequacy

All 8,301 observations from 9.90343322–100.09455322° 2θ were replayed without rounding or resampling. The model uses the reported Cu Kα₁/Kα₂ wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, a smooth six-coefficient Chebyshev background, one specimen-displacement correction (0.8009 mm), nonnegative W/X profile terms (11.2839/7.5239), and replay-stable Suortti roughness (0.2694/0.6552). Size, strain, texture, transparency, zero shift, and extra asymmetry terms were not retained. Representative least-overlapped MnWO₄ families span low, middle, and high angle with smoothly increasing widths and no monotonic position error: (1 0 0) at 18.02°: FWHM 0.128°, peak-max Δ +0.033°; (3 1 -1) at 62.38°: FWHM 0.137°, peak-max Δ +0.011°; (4 1 -1) at 84.00°: FWHM 0.147°, peak-max Δ -0.022°. No phase-specific width term was supported.

The exact final profile gives Rwp = 8.3816%, Rp = 5.6453%, Rexp = 2.4855%, and goodness of fit = 3.372 for 32 refined variables. Because the input supplied no pointwise uncertainties, Poisson counting uncertainties were used; the goodness of fit is therefore not an independently calibrated absolute error test.

## Remaining uncertainty

The fractions are reliable as a three-phase crystalline normalization, but no internal standard was supplied, so amorphous content cannot be measured. The near-tied wolframite parent records and monoclinic/triclinic WO₃ descriptions are the main structural systematics; they are larger than the tabulated covariance-only fraction errors.

Mn₂O₃ remains a watch-list possibility, not a reported phase. In the final-profile re-challenge, cubic bixbyite refined to 1.43 wt% and improved Rwp by only 0.023 percentage point: its strongest residual match was coherent, but the expected line near 37.95° worsened. An orthorhombic record reached 2.10 wt% only by distorting its nearly cubic reference cell from about 9.41 Å to 9.28/9.27/9.62 Å. Thus neither its structure nor fraction can be determined reliably from this pattern. Final-profile MnO₂ and Mn₃WO₆ trials reached only 1.20 and 0.62 wt%, with Rwp gains of 0.014 and 0.008 percentage point, respectively; neither showed enough independent evidence to report.
