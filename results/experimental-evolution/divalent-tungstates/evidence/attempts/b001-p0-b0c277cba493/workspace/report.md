# Crystalline phase analysis

The pattern is best described by three crystalline phases: wolframite ZnWO4, monoclinic WO3, and wurtzite ZnO. The refined fractions below are normalized only over these modeled crystalline phases.

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction | Formal s.u. |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| ZnWO4 | P 2/c (No. 13) | 69.10 wt% | 0.18 wt% |
| WO3 | P 21/n (No. 14) | 20.11 wt% | 0.11 wt% |
| ZnO | P 63mc (No. 186) | 10.79 wt% | 0.17 wt% |

The GSAS-II Rietveld fit over 9.903–100.095 degrees 2theta gives Rp = 2.796%, Rwp = 3.747%, Rexp = 2.765%, and goodness of fit = 1.358. Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and the reported 0.5 intensity ratio were fixed from the metadata. Scale factors, a smooth background, effective profile terms, sample displacement, and lattice parameters were refined. The final refinement is converged without an SVD singularity.

## Evidence

ZnWO4 is established by its characteristic 15.53 and 18.92 degree peaks, the resolved 30.45/30.69 degree pair, and reflections near 38.30, 51.70, and 68.17 degrees. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the diagnostic 23.17/23.62/24.40 degree triplet and peaks near 33.30 and 34.19 degrees. Wurtzite ZnO is required by peaks at 31.79, 34.46, 47.57, 56.60, and 62.87 degrees; its strongest 36.30 degree reflection overlaps ZnWO4. These assignments are also chemically consistent with incomplete reaction of the ZnO and WO3 precursors at 600 degrees C.

Starting coordinates came from COD entries 1520641 (ZnWO4), 1528915 (WO3), and 9004178 (ZnO). The refined cells are approximately 4.70151 x 5.72544 x 4.94134 A, beta 90.6158 degrees for ZnWO4; 7.30352 x 7.54150 x 7.69005 A, beta 90.9815 degrees for WO3; and a = 3.25087 A, c = 5.20320 A for ZnO. Atomic coordinates were retained from the reference structures because this multiphase laboratory pattern does not determine them independently with useful reliability.

## Uncertainty and limits

The tabulated s.u. values are covariance-only. Changing the smooth-background order from 10 to 20 and using alternative physically acceptable profile descriptions changed individual fractions by less than about 0.4 wt%; a practical uncertainty of roughly +/-0.3 wt% is therefore more realistic than the formal values alone. Small systematic intensity residuals remain in the overlapping WO3/ZnWO4 region around 23–24.5 degrees, potentially from phase-specific broadening or mild preferred orientation.

The weak ZnO cell terms oscillated when refined simultaneously with all global parameters, so its cell was optimized separately and then held fixed for the final converged scale refinement. The ZnO phase identity and normalized fraction are supported by multiple distinct peaks, but its lattice precision should not be inferred from the final CIF digits. No external standard was supplied, so amorphous content and the absolute crystalline fraction cannot be determined; the reported fractions must not be interpreted as whole-sample fractions if amorphous material is present.
