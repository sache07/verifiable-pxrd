# Crystalline phase analysis

## Result

Three crystalline phases are required. Fractions are normalized only over these modeled crystalline phases.

|---|---|---|---:|
| ZnWO4, wolframite type | P 2/c | a = 4.70248(21), b = 5.72682(21), c = 4.94263(20), β = 90.6106(20) | 69.10(21)% |
| monoclinic WO3 | P 21/n | a = 7.30566(49), b = 7.54326(46), c = 7.69226(53), β = 90.971(9) | 19.83(13)% |

The joint GSAS-II Rietveld fit gives Rp = 3.31%, Rwp = 4.40%, Rexp = 2.76%, and goodness of fit = 1.59 for 8,301 points and 28 refined parameters. The final cycle converged with maximum shift/esd = 0.68.

## Evidence and model

The dominant ZnWO4 assignment accounts for the characteristic peaks at approximately 18.92°, 24.4°, 30.45/30.69°, 38.30°, and 53.58° 2θ. Monoclinic WO3 is independently identified by its 23.17/23.62/24.40° triplet and peaks near 33.30° and 34.19°. Residual ZnO is required by its wurtzite lines at 31.79°, 34.46°, 36.30°, 47.57°, 56.60°, and 62.87°. The remaining residual does not contain a coherent set of peaks supporting a fourth crystalline phase.

Starting structures were COD 1520641 (ZnWO4), 1528915 (monoclinic WO3), and 9004178 (ZnO). The Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio from the metadata were fixed. Because pointwise uncertainties were absent, counting-statistical weights of 1/I were used. The refinement included phase scales and cells, zero offset, a constrained nonnegative CW peak-width model, and a 12-term Chebyshev background. A weak WO3 (010) March–Dollase correction refined to 0.945(4). For ZnWO4, the W and Zn y coordinates were refined before the final pass; oxygen coordinates were retained from the reference structure. Coordinates for WO3 and ZnO were also fixed to their references because the overlap and their lower abundance do not support reliable coordinate refinement.

The parenthesized errors are covariance-derived formal standard uncertainties and do not include model systematics. A more realistic fraction uncertainty is about 1–2 absolute wt% because no calibrated instrumental profile or internal standard was supplied and the strongest peaks overlap. Amorphous content, if present, cannot be quantified, so these are not absolute whole-sample fractions. The phase identities and approximate crystalline fractions are reliable; detailed oxygen positions and minor-phase atomic coordinates are not independently determined by this pattern.
