# PXRD phase analysis

The crystalline sample is modeled as **75.22 wt% wolframite-type MnWO4**, **19.55 wt% monoclinic WO3**, and **5.23 wt% tetragonal Mn3O4**. Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (A, degrees) | Crystalline wt% |
|---|---|---|---:|
| MnWO4 | P 2/c | a=4.79937, b=5.70697, c=4.97639, beta=88.8737 | 75.22 |
| WO3 | P 21/n | a=7.30022, b=7.55871, c=7.68580, beta=90.9542 | 19.55 |
| Mn3O4 | I 41/amd | a=5.74620, c=9.50792 | 5.23 |

## Evidence

MnWO4 accounts for the dominant lines at about 15.1, 18.1, 29.58/30.03, 35.7-35.9, 51.25, and 53.0-53.2 degrees 2theta and for the dense high-angle pattern. Monoclinic WO3 is required by its characteristic three-line cluster at about 22.70, 23.09, and 23.94 degrees, the 33-34 degree group, and further lines near 41.5 and 49.5 degrees. Adding WO3 reduced Rwp from about 13.7% to about 11.0% in the controlled model comparison.

Weak features and shoulders near 17.6, 31.8, 35.8, 59.5, 64.5, and 80.5 degrees support residual hausmannite Mn3O4. Including it reduced Rwp from 10.65% to 10.55%. Its contribution is heavily overlapped, so this assignment and especially its fraction are less secure than those of MnWO4 and WO3. The residual WO3:Mn3O4 amount is also chemically consistent with incompletely reacted WO3/Mn3O4 precursor material. Mn3WO6 was tested and rejected because its predicted strong reflections near 20.0, 20.5, 28.8, and 35.3 degrees are not observed in the required proportions.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The final GSAS-II fit used the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and fixed 0.5 intensity ratio, a Bragg-Brentano specimen-displacement correction, a Chebyshev background, a positive peak-width model, mild MnWO4 [001] preferred orientation, and phase-specific broadening for WO3. The fit statistics are Rwp=10.55%, Rp=7.15%, Rexp=2.49%, and goodness-of-fit=4.24 over 8301 points.

Formal one-sigma fraction uncertainties are 0.43, 0.26, and 0.42 percentage points for MnWO4, WO3, and Mn3O4, respectively, but these understate systematic uncertainty. No pointwise uncertainties or calibrated instrument profile were supplied; Poisson counting weights were therefore used. The high goodness-of-fit and correlated residuals indicate profile/model error, and practical fraction uncertainty is closer to a few wt% for the major phases and roughly 1-2 wt% for Mn3O4. An amorphous fraction cannot be determined from this refinement.

Only the Mn and W positional parameters in MnWO4 were allowed to adjust. Oxygen positions in MnWO4 and all internal coordinates in the weaker WO3 and Mn3O4 phases were retained from reference structures because this laboratory X-ray pattern does not determine them reliably. Unit-cell values are also correlated with the sizable specimen-displacement correction, so their quoted least-squares errors are not full accuracy estimates.
