# Powder diffraction phase analysis

The replay-stable crystalline model contains four phases. Fractions are normalized only over the modeled crystalline phases:

| Phase model | Weight fraction |
|---|---:|
| SrWO4 (scheelite) | 63.37% (formal s.u. 0.19%) |
| WO3 (orthorhombic) | 5.73% (formal s.u. 0.25%) |
| SrCO3 (orthorhombic strontianite) | 16.00% (formal s.u. 0.15%) |
| WO3 (monoclinic) | 14.90% (formal s.u. 0.23%) |

The full-range final replay gives Rwp = 3.475%, Rp = 2.703% and goodness of fit = 1.217 over 8301 points. The model uses the measured Cu Kalpha doublet, six smooth Chebyshev background coefficients, one specimen-shift correction, nonnegative common W/X profile terms, and a fixed roughness correction established by the focused angular-trend comparison. Unsupported size, extra strain, asymmetry and polarization terms were pruned or fixed at their physical neutral values.

## Evidence for the inventory

The dominant pattern is scheelite-type SrWO4. Since the supplied structure index contained no exact SrWO4 record, an isovalent SrMoO4 scheelite parent (COD 2300460) was converted to SrWO4 and health-screened; only its cell and whole-pattern nuisance terms were refined. A lower-symmetry fergusonite analogue improved the neutral fit slightly but lost the matched compact texture/profile comparison, so it was rejected. Structurally distinct WO3 records were compared; the orthorhombic COD 2107312 record was the strongest neutral competitor. All four submitted final CIFs pass the required geometry, occupancy and contact health screen.

Residual families required both an orthorhombic and a monoclinic WO3 model. The monoclinic model carries independent lines near 22.51 and 41.33 degrees 2theta, while the orthorhombic model carries independent lines near 47.48 and 54.07 degrees. From the same mature checkpoint, deleting orthorhombic WO3 raised Rwp from 4.049% to 4.766%, and deleting monoclinic WO3 raised it to 5.104%; both retained scales were positive and replay-stable. SrCO3 is also independently supported by families near 24.62/25.26, 35.65/36.03 and 43.61 degrees, consistent with unreacted precursor.

## Remaining uncertainty

No pointwise standard uncertainties were supplied, so the listed fraction s.u. values are conditional GSAS-II least-squares values and are smaller than realistic uncertainty. Preferred orientation, the empirical roughness correction, severe peak overlap and partitioning between the two related WO3 polymorphs make the individual WO3 fractions model-dependent; their combined abundance is more reliable than either individual value. A conservative interpretation is that the minor orthorhombic WO3 fraction is only semiquantitative and both WO3 fractions may shift by several weight-percent under alternative texture/profile assumptions. The scheelite topology is well supported, but its detailed atomic coordinates were inherited from the isovalent analogue and cannot be determined reliably from this powder pattern alone. No amorphous fraction or external-standard absolute crystallinity was determined.
