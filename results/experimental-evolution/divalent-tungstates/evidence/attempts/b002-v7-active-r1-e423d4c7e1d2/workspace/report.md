# Quantitative phase analysis

## Result

The crystalline sample is modeled as monoclinic MgWO4, monoclinic gamma-WO3, and cubic MgO. Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases; an amorphous fraction cannot be measured without an internal standard.

The WO3 CIF uses the valid nonstandard monoclinic setting of its source record. Atomic coordinates, occupancies, and displacement parameters were held to the source structures; the cells and powder-profile quantities were refined. Consequently, the data establish the phase structures and lattice metrics but do not independently determine subtle atomic displacements, oxygen defects, or cation disorder.

The full-range fit (9.9034-100.0946 degrees 2theta, 8,301 observations) has Rwp = 2.3286%, Rp = 1.7767%, Rexp = 1.5271%, and goodness-of-fit = 1.5276 for 29 refined parameters.

## Phase evidence and inventory challenge

The chemical and thermal context is a 700 degrees C reaction of MgO and WO3 measured in reflection geometry with Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 radiation. MgWO4 is identified by its 18.88 degrees peak and the 30.41/30.75 degrees pair. The 23.13/23.57/24.38 degrees family is characteristic of monoclinic gamma-WO3, while MgO is independently supported by the 42.92 and 62.30 degrees peaks. Removing MgO from the accepted model worsened Rwp from 2.329% to 2.985%.

The WO3 assignment was physically challenged with exact orthorhombic, tetragonal, triclinic, and alternate monoclinic records under the same restrained nuisance model. With MgWO4 texture and isotropic WO3 broadening applied consistently, COD 2311041 gave Rwp = 2.486%, versus 2.631% for the best triclinic candidate and 3.675% for the alternate monoclinic candidate; orthorhombic and tetragonal candidates were substantially poorer. Directional WO3 broadening then improved the accepted model further. Thus the record comparison favors gamma-WO3, although the powder data do not exclude low-level defect variants of that structure.

All retained phases are charge neutral as Mg2+WO4, W6+O3, and Mg2+O. Per gram of modeled crystalline material, Mg = 0.0037615 mol and W = 0.0036595 mol, giving Mg/W = 1.028. The residual MgO/WO3 molar ratio is 1.058. This is consistent with incomplete reaction of an approximately equimolar precursor mixture and leaves no material element-balance deficit. The residual profile was re-screened for weak phases after every accepted phase/profile change; no repeatable unmatched peak family justified another tungstate or a non-1:1 product.

## Profile model and measured-peak audit

The final model uses a smooth 10-term Chebyshev background, phase scales and cells, a single zero correction (-0.01905 degrees), and a common nonnegative W/X instrumental width baseline. U refined to its zero bound and was pruned; V, Y, and Z are zero. The Cu doublet ratio and polarization were fixed to the measurement model, and SH/L was not released because repeated unexplained asymmetry was absent. Background orders 6 and 8 were worse; order 12 produced only a negligible gain. Refined polarization and roughness did not produce a supported improvement.

MgWO4 required a [010] March-Dollase term (1.2994), as expected for a textured specimen in reflection geometry. WO3 required [110] uniaxial domain-size broadening (two GSAS-II size components of about 0.144 and 0.084 micrometres) plus isotropic microstrain (about 3,797 ppm). Isotropic size and strain were tested one phase at a time from the same saved baseline. MgWO4 and MgO broadening terms were unsupported or nonphysical; for WO3, size outperformed strain alone, and the complementary strain term was replay-stable. Distinct MgWO4 texture axes were challenged, with [010] clearly preferred; texture was not used to absorb WO3 line-width mismatch.

The strong low- and middle-angle features are reproduced well. The weak high-angle MgWO4 line, the high-angle WO3 family, and overlapped weak MgO lines retain position/width discrepancies. Together with goodness-of-fit above the counting expectation, these are evidence of residual profile/systematic error rather than a coherent omitted phase.

## Reliability and deliverables

The parenthetical fraction uncertainties above are covariance estimates only. Preferred orientation, possible microabsorption, source-record choice, peak overlap, and residual high-angle shape error mean that the true fraction uncertainty is larger, plausibly a few weight-percent absolute. MgO is securely detected but has the least reliable fraction because it is weak and substantially overlapped. No amorphous content can be quantified, and the reported fractions must not be interpreted as whole-sample fractions if amorphous material is present.

The accepted project was saved, reopened, and replayed across the complete measured range before export. Unsupported or bound terms were pruned and no frozen refinement variables remain. `final.gpx`, the three submitted CIFs, `fit.csv`, and `result.json` all derive from that reopened project; the CIF paths exist, fractions sum to 1.000000, and the profile and fit statistics agree with the project.
