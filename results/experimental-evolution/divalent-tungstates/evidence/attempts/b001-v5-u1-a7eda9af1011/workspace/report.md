# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is modeled by three crystalline phases. Fractions are normalized to these phases; no amorphous fraction can be measured without an internal standard.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell (Å, °) | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| SrWO4, scheelite | I41/a (No. 88) | a = 5.393157, c = 11.900592 | 61.19 ± 0.18 wt% |
| WO3, monoclinic | P21/c (No. 14, nonconventional source setting) | a = 7.69175, b = 7.55426, c = 10.51406, β = 136.11208 | 23.25 ± 0.13 wt% |
| SrCO3, strontianite | Pnma (No. 62) | a = 6.02570, b = 5.09740, c = 8.39233 | 15.57 ± 0.19 wt% |

Final full-range statistics are Rwp = 4.7598%, Rp = 3.2972%, Rexp = 2.8612% and goodness-of-fit = 1.666 for 8,301 points.

## Evidence and model selection

The SrWO4 assignment accounts for resolved low-, middle- and high-angle scheelite families. Because the supplied index has no exact SrWO4 entry, COD 2300460 (room-temperature, fully occupied SrMoO4 in I41/a) was selected from stoichiometric analogues and Mo was replaced by W. CaWO4 required an additional A-site substitution, while the available BaWO4 record has split half-occupied oxygen sites. The final substituted model has no short contacts (W–O = 1.727 Å; Sr–O = 2.606–2.610 Å).

Monoclinic WO3 is required by the diagnostic 22.52/22.94/23.82° triplet. The fully occupied neutron model COD 2311041 outperformed the alternative conventional monoclinic record and tetragonal polymorphs under the same nuisance model. Orthorhombic room-temperature SrCO3 (COD 9008198) explains the 24.62–25.30° carbonate group; a high-temperature disordered R-3m record fit worse. A watched Sr3W2O9 candidate refined to the nonnegative scale floor, and its unique low-angle families are absent.

The phase fractions are chemically coherent with partial reaction of the reported precursors: per 100 g of modeled crystal content, residual WO3 and SrCO3 are 0.1003 and 0.1054 mol, respectively. Their near 1:1 ratio is expected for SrCO3 + WO3 → SrWO4 + CO2 and corresponds to about 64% conversion on this crystalline-only basis.

The Cu Kα1/Kα2 doublet was modeled explicitly. A specimen-displacement-only branch was retained over zero-only; the refined 1052 µm value should be treated as an effective angular correction because no independent calibration was supplied. The common nonnegative profile converged from two interior starts; U returned to zero, while W, X and SH/L were retained. Only WO3 isotropic strain survived phase-by-phase size/strain replay. An eight-term smooth background was retained; extra background terms gave negligible gain. Polarization survived three starts, whereas roughness was unstable, bound-hitting and highly correlated. March–Dollase axes [110] for SrWO4, [101] for WO3 and [001] for SrCO3 were supported by multiple isolated families and conditional axis swaps. The final project was saved, reopened and replayed over the complete measured range with no frozen parameters.

## Remaining uncertainty

The final audit found no frozen/bound variables or sub-1.1 Å contacts, and all exported CIFs round-trip to the project cells and atom counts. The largest off-diagonal correlation is +0.887 between 1::A0 and 1::A4 (coupled WO3 reciprocal-cell terms); the SrWO4 scale–polarization correlation is -0.770.

The tabulated uncertainties are formal covariance values only. Pointwise uncertainties were absent, so 1/I Poisson-surrogate weights were used. Preferred orientation, the large uncalibrated angular correction, fixed source coordinates/occupancies and the SrMoO4→SrWO4 prototype substitution create larger model-systematic uncertainty than the formal sub-0.2 wt% values; the fractions are best regarded as robust to a few absolute wt%, not to their last reported decimal. Atomic coordinates were not independently refined from this multiphase laboratory pattern. A very weak phase with fully overlapped peaks cannot be excluded or quantified reliably.
