# PXRD phase analysis

The crystalline sample is modeled as **NiWO4 + WO3 + NiO**. The refined crystalline-phase fractions are 60.32 wt% NiWO4, 29.80 wt% WO3, and 9.88 wt% NiO, normalized to the three modeled phases.

| Phase | Structure | Refined cell | Crystalline wt% |
|---|---|---|---:|
| NiWO4 | Wolframite type, P 2/c (No. 13) | a = 4.59940, b = 5.66253, c = 4.91129 Å; beta = 90.5014° | 60.32 |
| WO3 | Monoclinic, P 21/c (No. 14; valid nonstandard cell setting) | a = 7.68207, b = 7.54437, c = 10.50787 Å; beta = 136.0238° | 29.80 |
| NiO | Rocksalt type, F m -3 m (No. 225) | a = 4.16488 Å | 9.88 |

## Evidence

The precursor context and 700 °C treatment make NiWO4 the expected reaction product. Its assignment is supported by the lines at approximately 15.62, 19.27, 23.96, 24.91, 30.85/31.04, and 36.58° 2theta. The sharp WO3 triplet at 23.14, 23.56, and 24.38°, together with lines near 26.58, 28.62/28.95, and 33.28–34.16°, requires residual monoclinic WO3. The sequence at about 37.34, 43.42, 63.07, 75.55, 79.68, and 95.4° is characteristic of rocksalt NiO and requires a third phase. No additional repeatable unindexed peak family remains in the difference profile.

The final joint Rietveld refinement gives Rwp = 2.128%, Rp = 1.712%, and goodness of fit = 1.474 over 10–100° 2theta. The Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio were fixed to the metadata values. Because pointwise uncertainties were absent, counting-statistical uncertainties of sqrt(I) were used. Phase scales, cells, zero offset, an empirical common peak profile, all independent NiWO4 coordinates, and the W coordinates in WO3 were refined. The WO3 oxygen coordinates were retained from the neutron structure model; isotropic displacement parameters were fixed because unconstrained refinement drove them to nonphysical values.

## Uncertainty and scope

The phase identities are reliable. Formal least-squares fraction uncertainties are only about 0.03–0.04 percentage points, but they do not include model systematics. Trials with simpler physically plausible profile and coordinate models changed the fractions by roughly one percentage point, which is a more realistic precision estimate. The absence of a calibrated instrumental profile, an internal standard, particle-size information, and a defensible microabsorption correction limits absolute quantitative accuracy, particularly between the W-rich phases and NiO.

These fractions are normalized only over modeled crystalline material. Any amorphous content cannot be determined from this measurement, and trace crystalline phases below roughly the percent level cannot be excluded. Detailed oxygen positions and displacement parameters should not be interpreted as independently determined by this mixed-phase laboratory pattern.
