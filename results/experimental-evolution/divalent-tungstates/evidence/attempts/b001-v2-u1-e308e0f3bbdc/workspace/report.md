# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

| Phase | Space group | Crystalline weight fraction |
|---|---:|---:|
| MnWO4, wolframite | P2/c | 71.92 wt% (formal e.s.d. 0.65 wt%) |
| WO3, monoclinic | P21/c | 18.28 wt% (formal e.s.d. 0.23 wt%) |
| Mn3O4, hausmannite | I41/amd | 9.80 wt% (formal e.s.d. 0.75 wt%) |

The fractions sum to 100% over the modeled crystalline phases only. The final full-range, save/reopen GSAS-II replay gave Rwp = 9.055%, Rp = 6.020%, Rexp = 2.490%, and goodness of fit = 3.642 (8,301 points and 27 refined variables).

## Evidence and model choice

The dominant wolframite assignment is fixed by the 18.06, 23.29/23.80, 29.58/30.03, 30.91, 37.05, and 51.25 degree reflection families. No exact MnWO4 entry was present in the supplied index, so cross-chemistry stoichiometric analogues were screened. The final model preserves the ordered, fully occupied 293 K ZnWO4 structure (COD 1520641) with the sole chemical substitution Zn -> Mn. Under the same nuisance model, the fixed-coordinate ZnWO4 parent gave Rwp 9.54%, compared with 9.60% for MgWO4, 11.16% for MnReO4, and 12.12% for the physically usable FeWO4 record. Two other FeWO4 records were rejected for impossible W-O distances. Only the Mn and W special-position coordinates were refined; releasing all oxygen coordinates improved the numerical fit but gave an implausible W bond-valence sum near 4.54 and was rejected. The submitted model has full occupancies, Mn-O = 2.064-2.220 Å, W-O = 1.797-2.161 Å, and bond-valence sums near Mn 1.82 and W 5.95.

Residual peaks at 22.70, 26.16, 32.83, 33.73, and 41.47 degrees identify unreacted monoclinic WO3. The selected ordered neutron structure is COD 2311041. A triclinic record gave a marginally lower initial residual only by collapsing its three angles toward a pseudo-monoclinic metric; orthorhombic, tetragonal, and hexagonal alternatives were worse under the same modest profile model. Hausmannite Mn3O4 is supported by its 17.51, 31.79, 43.98, and 58.08 degree families and by the precursor inventory; COD 9001963 is ordered and fully occupied. Trial Mn2O3, MnO, and Mn3WO6 phases did not retain chemically plausible cells/scales or diagnostic unique peaks. The final residual contains profile-shaped errors at strong modeled reflections but no coherent unmatched peak family requiring a fourth crystalline phase.

## Refinement and uncertainty

Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and the reported 0.5 intensity ratio were used. Angular dependence favored specimen displacement over zero shift (inventory-stage Rwp 14.0645% versus 14.0987%); zero was fixed and the final displacement is 0.806 mm. The common width baseline was taken from sharp MnWO4 peaks: W was fixed at 25 and only a positive common X term was retained. U-containing branches became negative, W+X was strongly correlated, and an SH/L branch was 96% correlated with displacement. Size-only branches ran to bounds and were rejected. Reproducible broader profiles supported isotropic microstrain for WO3 (0.764%) and Mn3O4 (0.993%); no texture or anisotropic broadening was used. The nonstandard WO3 cell was refined to stability and then held in the final replay to remove a 0.93 reciprocal-cell correlation. The final project has no frozen variables or correlations above 0.8.

Pointwise uncertainties and a calibrated instrument profile were not supplied, so counting-statistics weights were assumed. Consequently, the formal e.s.d.s above understate model uncertainty. In particular, scale and broadening are coupled for the two minor phases: defensible profile branches span about 72-80 wt% MnWO4, 15-19 wt% WO3, and 5-10 wt% Mn3O4. Thus the phase identities are reliable, but the minor-phase fractions cannot be claimed more precisely than those approximate ranges. Amorphous material, if present, is not quantified.
