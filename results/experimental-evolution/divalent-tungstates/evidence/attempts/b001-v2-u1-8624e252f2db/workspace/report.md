# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is modeled as three crystalline phases. Fractions are normalized only over those modeled crystalline phases.

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction | Refined cell |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| ZnWO4 (wolframite) | P 2/c | 73.86(19)% | a = 4.70145, b = 5.72567, c = 4.94133 Å, beta = 90.6108° |
| ZnO (wurtzite) | P 63 m c | 8.74(18)% | a = 3.25109, c = 5.20342 Å |
| WO3 (room-temperature monoclinic) | P 21/n | 17.40(12)% | a = 7.30408, b = 7.54188, c = 7.69059 Å, beta = 90.9754° |

Final full-range fit: Rwp = 3.840%, Rp = 2.989%, Rexp = 2.765%, and GOF = 1.391 (8301 observations, 27 refined variables). Because point uncertainties were not supplied, counting-statistical weights of 1/intensity were used.

## Evidence and model selection

The phase assignment follows diagnostic reflection families, not only global residual reduction. ZnWO4 accounts for 15.45 and 18.90°, the resolved 30.44/30.68° pair, and many higher-angle lines. Wurtzite ZnO accounts for 31.80, 34.47, 36.29, 47.58, 56.60, 62.90, and 68.16°. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the characteristic 23.15/23.62/24.42° triplet and the 26.63/28.64/28.97° family. No coherent unmatched peak family remains. The fractions also give a chemically plausible ZnO + WO3 to ZnWO4 precursor/product balance.

Candidate records were screened for occupied composition, symmetry, source conditions, occupancies, and geometry. The selected ordered records were COD 1520641 for ZnWO4 (minimum distance 1.797 Å), COD 9004178 for 293 K wurtzite ZnO (1.975 Å), and COD 2311041 for monoclinic WO3 (1.715 Å). Cu-substituted triclinic records indexed as ZnWO4 and cubic ZnO polymorphs were rejected as chemically or diagnostically incompatible. Under the same provisional nuisance/profile model, the selected WO3 record gave Rwp = 4.917%, versus 4.972% for COD 1528915 and 6.666% for triclinic COD 2106293. COD 2311041 was represented by an exactly equivalent unimodular short-cell P 21/n setting to avoid the highly correlated long-cell setting.

The angular correction was modeled as a Bragg-Brentano specimen shift, refined to -46.81(1.34) micrometres; zero was fixed. Zero and shift trials were nearly tied, but the shift branch followed the observed angular dependence slightly better. The common lower-envelope profile retained only W = 6.603; U, V, X, Y, and Z were zero and fixed. Phase-by-phase challenges favored size-only over strain-only broadening, giving approximate coherent-domain sizes of 62.2, 231, and 113 nm for ZnWO4, ZnO, and WO3. A refined SH/L term was rejected because its small improvement was 96.7% correlated with specimen shift. No strain, anisotropy, texture, or atomic-coordinate terms were added.

The final project was saved, reopened, and refined twice over the complete measured range before export. The phase fractions changed by less than 7e-7 between replay checks, and the final project has no frozen variables or correlations above 0.8.

## Remaining uncertainty

The parent structures and phase identities are reliable, but this multiphase laboratory pattern does not support an independent determination of atomic coordinates; coordinates and displacement parameters therefore remain those of the vetted parent records while unit cells were refined. The parenthesized fraction uncertainties are covariance-based statistical values conditional on this model. Unknown instrumental calibration, fixed polarization/profile assumptions, background choice, and possible W-rich/ZnO microabsorption can produce larger systematic fraction errors. Amorphous material and phases below the diffraction detection limit are not quantified, so the reported fractions must not be interpreted as whole-sample mass fractions if such material is present. Some crowded WO3/ZnWO4 intensity mismatch remains, especially near 23–24° and 50–65°, but it does not define a distinct missing-phase reflection family.
