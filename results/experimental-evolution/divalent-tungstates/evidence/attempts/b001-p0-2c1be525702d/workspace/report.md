# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The pattern is modeled as two crystalline phases, normalized over the modeled crystalline material:

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| CaWO4, scheelite | tetragonal *I* 41/*a* | 87.49 wt% |
| WO3, monoclinic | *P* 21/*c* | 12.51 wt% |

The formal covariance-derived standard uncertainty is 0.14 weight-percentage point for each fraction. This is substantially smaller than the likely systematic uncertainty; a more realistic interpretation is about +/-1--2 weight-percentage points because no calibrated instrument profile or measured pointwise uncertainties were supplied, and weak non-modelled features remain.

## Evidence and refinement

Scheelite CaWO4 accounts for the dominant reflections, including those at approximately 18.43, 28.54, 31.27, 34.01, 47.0, 54.1 and 57.7 degrees 2theta. Its refined cell is *a* = 5.24297 A and *c* = 11.37452 A. The 22.95/23.42/24.23-degree triplet and additional lines near 26.43, 33.10/33.42 and 41.29/41.76 degrees require monoclinic WO3. The selected *P* 21/*c* reference setting refines to *a* = 7.68313 A, *b* = 7.53246 A, *c* = 10.50433 A and beta = 136.0615 degrees.

The Rietveld model used the reported Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, Poisson weights (1/counts), a 12-term Chebyshev background, refined phase scales and cells, zero, Gaussian profile terms, effective polarization, CaWO4 crystallite size and [001] March-Dollase preferred orientation. Literature atomic coordinates were retained because releasing the CaWO4 oxygen coordinates produced implausible bond geometry. The two W atoms in WO3 shared one refined isotropic displacement parameter; the weak oxygen contribution did not support an independent value.

The final fit gives Rwp = 8.625%, Rp = 5.341%, Rexp = 2.247% and goodness of fit = 3.845 over 10.001--100.008 degrees 2theta. The elevated goodness of fit reflects systematic line-shape/intensity residuals in addition to counting noise.

## Remaining uncertainty

Monoclinic and pseudotriclinic WO3 descriptions fit almost identically; the data establish WO3 but do not reliably resolve that subtle symmetry choice. The monoclinic model was retained as the higher-symmetry, ambient-condition description consistent with the precursor chemistry.

CaO, Ca(OH)2, fergusonite CaWO4, mixed Ca-W oxides and W suboxides were tested as possible additional phases. Their scales refined to zero or to unstable broad components, so no third phase can be quantified reliably. Several weak residual peaks occur near expected Cu K-beta ghost positions, while a few low-angle residuals are not fully explained. A trace crystalline impurity below the practical detection level, amorphous material, and unreacted material hidden by overlap therefore cannot be excluded; the reported fractions do not include amorphous content.
