# Phase analysis and refinement report

The pattern is modeled as three crystalline phases, normalized over the modeled crystalline material:

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction |
|---|---:|---:|
| Wolframite MnWO4 | P2/c | 74.95 ± 0.10 wt% |
| Triclinic WO3 | P-1 | 18.94 ± 0.05 wt% |
| Hausmannite Mn3O4 | I41/amd | 6.11 ± 0.11 wt% |

The uncertainties in the table are formal GSAS-II covariance estimates from the final scale/background replay. They do not include phase-model, preferred-orientation, absorption, or microstructure uncertainty.

## Evidence and model selection

The precursor context (WO3 + Mn3O4, heated at 700 °C) and the dominant reflections at approximately 15.11, 18.07, 23.3, 29.6, and 30.0° support wolframite MnWO4 as the principal reaction product. No exact MnWO4 entry was present in the supplied index, so cross-chemistry analogues were searched. An ordered P2/c ZnWO4 parent (COD 1520641) was used with the isovalent Zn2+→Mn2+ substitution; its cell, atomic coordinates, and MnWO4 displacement factors were then refined. An FeWO4-derived alternate was substantially inferior. The final Mn–O distances are 2.11–2.22 Å and W–O distances are 1.77–2.14 Å.

Residual WO3 is required. The selected P-1 record (COD 2106293) resolves repeated split families near 32.8–34.0 and 55.3–55.7°, with supporting gains at middle and high angle. Monoclinic P21/c records COD 2311041 and 1528915 were challenged under the same nuisance model and fit worse. WO3 required both isotropic size and strain broadening (about 0.088 µm and 4175 microstrain). W positions were refined; oxygen positions were retained from the source record.

Minor Mn3O4 is supported by relatively isolated families near 31.8 and 59.4° and additional intensity near 50.1 and 58.2°. COD 9001963 was retained over the source alternate COD 1011262. Its broad peaks require an isotropic strain term (about 8586 microstrain). Neutralizing that term worsened Rwp from 2.615% to 2.668% and changed its fitted fraction from about 6.1 to 4.2 wt%; consequently, the Mn3O4 amount is model-sensitive and a practical uncertainty of roughly ±1–2 wt% is more realistic than the formal ±0.11 wt% value.

Mn2O3 produced a provisional sub-percent scale (about 0.6 wt%) and improved one diagnostic family, but other strong, relatively isolated Mn2O3 families were absent or worsened. It therefore cannot be identified or quantified reliably and was omitted. MnO, Mn3WO6, and a C2/m MnWO4 analogue refined to zero or statistically insignificant scales.

## Refinement and limitations

The full 9.903–100.095° range was fitted in Bragg–Brentano reflection geometry using the supplied Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. A specimen-displacement-only position correction was retained; a zero-only branch was slightly worse, and zero plus displacement was not used. Common U/W/X and SH/L profile terms converged from multiple interior starts. An eight-term smooth Chebyshev background was retained (six terms fit worse), polarization refined to 0.7873, and roughness was rejected because it became almost perfectly correlated with phase scales. Tested texture axes either returned to unity or improved only one family, so no preferred orientation was retained.

The final save/reopen replay used all 8,301 observations and converged with Rwp = 2.6128%, Rp = 2.0393%, and GOF = 1.0499, with no frozen or bounded variables. Refined physical/profile/structural values were fixed for this stable final replay; background and phase scales supplied the final covariance matrix.

The triclinic WO3 assignment is supported by peak splitting, but its exact low-symmetry oxygen framework is not independently determined by these X-ray powder data; the source temperature is also unspecified. WO3 oxygen coordinates and displacement parameters should therefore be treated cautiously. The reported fractions are crystalline-phase normalized only: without an internal standard, amorphous content and absolute crystalline yield cannot be determined.
