# PXRD phase analysis

The pattern is described by three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Space group | Refined cell | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| CaWO4, scheelite | I 41/a (No. 88) | a = 5.24220 A, c = 11.37595 A | 78.29(6)% |
| gamma-WO3, monoclinic | P 21/c (No. 14) | a = 7.68255 A, b = 7.53159 A, c = 10.50426 A, beta = 136.0695 deg | 11.564(33)% |
| Ca3WO6, double-perovskite prototype | P 21/n (No. 14) | a = 5.53277 A, b = 5.78135 A, c = 8.01247 A, beta = 90.0727 deg | 10.146(52)% |

## Evidence and model

Scheelite CaWO4 accounts for the dominant families near 18.43, 28.54, 31.27, 34.01, 46.93, 54.11, and 57.70 degrees 2theta. Monoclinic gamma-WO3 is identified especially by the 22.94/23.42/24.23 degree triplet and supporting families near 26.44, 33.1-33.4, and 41-42 degrees. After those phases were fitted, coherent residual families near 19.3, 22.0, 30.7, 31-32, 36-38, and 45 degrees required a calcium-rich phase.

No exact Ca3WO6 entry was available. Several stoichiometric parents were screened; the P21/n Ca3OsO6 record (COD 1562666) uniquely matched the residual family and permits the chemically conservative, isovalent Os6+ -> W6+ substitution. Its scale was refined positive with the parent cell fixed before releasing the cell. The final substituted model has six-coordinate W with W-O distances of 1.923-1.939 A. This supports the phase assignment, but it is a prototype model rather than an independent atomic-structure solution; its atomic coordinates were therefore held fixed.

The ambient scheelite model came from COD 7219229. Alternate WO3 records were compared under the same nuisance model; the room-temperature monoclinic neutron model COD 2311041 gave the best fit. Atomic coordinates for all phases were retained from their source/prototype models; unit cells, phase scales, and supported profile terms were refined.

The final full-range refinement used both reported Cu K-alpha wavelengths with the K-alpha2/K-alpha1 ratio fixed at 0.5, one zero correction (-0.20429 degrees), a smooth eight-coefficient Chebyshev background, and a nonnegative common U/W/X profile. The refined effective polarization is 0.873. Unsupported V/Y/Z, roughness, strain, and extra asymmetry terms were neutralized. CaWO4 required [110] March-Dollase texture (1.268) and a weakly anisotropic size model (91.8 nm equatorial, 85.1 nm axial); Ca3WO6 used an isotropic size of about 315 nm. Texture trials for WO3 and Ca3WO6 returned to random values.

The final statistics are Rwp = 2.848%, Rp = 2.217%, Rexp = 2.247%, and goodness of fit = 1.270 over 8301 points. The project replayed after save/reopen with no frozen or bound parameters and a maximum shift/e.s.d. of 0.019.

## Remaining uncertainty

Fixed-cell watch-list trials gave only trace scales with negligible fit improvement: CaO about 0.12 wt%, alternate WO3 polymorphs at or below 0.06 wt%, a Ca-W oxide bronze near 0.05 wt%, and high-pressure CaWO4 effectively zero. Their complete unique reflection families were not present, so these trace fractions cannot be determined reliably and they are excluded. A practical CaO upper bound is roughly 0.3 wt% for this model.

Parenthetical uncertainties in the table are formal covariance estimates and do not include source-model, preferred-orientation, or profile-systematic uncertainty. Practical absolute uncertainty is closer to about 1 wt% for CaWO4 and WO3 and 1-2 wt% for prototype-based Ca3WO6. Any amorphous content is outside the normalized crystalline fractions and cannot be quantified from this refinement without an internal standard.
