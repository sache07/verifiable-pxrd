# PXRD phase analysis

## Result

The crystalline pattern is modeled by wolframite MnWO4 with residual monoclinic WO3 and hausmannite Mn3O4. Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases; no amorphous fraction or absolute crystalline content can be obtained without an internal standard.

| Phase | Space group | Crystalline weight fraction | Structural source |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| MnWO4 (wolframite) | P 2/c (No. 13) | 73.46(36) wt% | FeWO4 COD 9008124 prototype, Fe replaced by Mn |
| WO3 (monoclinic) | P 21/c (No. 14) | 21.50(24) wt% | COD 2311041 |
| Mn3O4 (hausmannite) | I 41/amd (No. 141) | 5.04(34) wt% | COD 9001963 |

The final 9.9034–100.0946 degree 2theta fit has Rwp = 10.0815%, Rp = 6.3570%, goodness of fit = 4.0553, and 29 varied parameters for 8301 observations. Parentheses above are one-standard-uncertainty values from the GSAS-II covariance matrix, not total model uncertainty.

## Phase evidence and candidate tests

The precursor context (WO3 + Mn3O4 heated at 700 degrees C) supports formation of MnWO4 while allowing unreacted precursors. The refined crystalline inventory has Mn/W = 0.921 on a molar basis, consistent with MnWO4 plus modest W-rich residual material, although the unspecified starting amounts make this only a consistency check.

No exact MnWO4 record was present in the supplied candidate index. Cross-chemistry screening of ABX4 analogues identified fully occupied wolframite FeWO4 (COD 9008124); the isovalent Fe2+ to Mn2+ site substitution was made while retaining the parent coordinates, and an observed-pattern indexed trial cell was then refined. Its P 2/c reflection families at about 15.1, 18.0, 23.3–23.8, and 29.6–30.0 degrees account for the dominant pattern. A chemically plausible C 2/m MnMoO4-derived MnWO4 challenge was run with the same common profile and nuisance schedule; its scale collapsed to the lower limit at the source cell and it did not account for the diagnostic wolframite peaks.

For residual WO3, the selected P 21/c record gave Rwp = 13.78% at the common source-cell scale/background stage, compared with 15.27% for triclinic COD 2106293 and 15.56% for orthorhombic COD 2107312. The triclinic model reached a marginally lower residual only after its angles moved strongly toward a pseudo-monoclinic metric, so that compensation was not treated as a polymorph identification. The P 21/c model was retained as the parsimonious average structure. High-pressure WO3 and records with poor geometry or incompatible source conditions were rejected.

The modern, fully occupied 295 K hausmannite record COD 9001963 was preferred to the older COD 1011262 model after source-condition and geometry screening. On addition at its source cell, the common-profile fit improved from Rwp about 11.57% to 11.49%, with 4.59(42) wt% Mn3O4 before releasing its cell. Releasing the cell improved multiple Mn3O4 families, including 211, 103, 224, and 321, and gave approximately 5–5.5 wt%. A Mn3WO6 trial refined to zero scale and was rejected. After every inventory change, background, all scales, the common angular correction, and cells were reconverged before comparison.

All selected records are fully occupied and free of modeled disorder. Final interatomic distances remain chemically credible: MnWO4 has Mn–O = 2.080–2.177 A and W–O = 1.758–2.195 A; WO3 has W–O = 1.717–2.162 A; and Mn3O4 has four-coordinate Mn–O = 2.045 A and six-coordinate Mn–O = 1.925 (four) and 2.299 A (two).

## Refinement model and validation

The common profile uses an eight-term Chebyshev background and nonnegative W/X width terms; U, V, and Y are zero. A specimen-displacement correction was retained instead of zero shift (common-schedule Rwp 16.477% versus 16.504%), and the two were not co-refined. A bounded SH/L test was unstable on replay and was pruned. Polarization refinement sought its physical bound, so polarization was fixed at 0.7. A one-parameter Suortti roughness term was stable; the correlated two-parameter form was rejected.

Phase broadening was challenged from neutral values. Only isotropic WO3 size broadening was supported, refining to 0.0716(29) micrometres; the comparable size-only branch fit better than strain-only (Rwp 11.569% versus 11.682% at that stage). MnWO4 and Mn3O4 sizes were left large and all phase strains were set to zero. No reproducible anisotropic broadening was found. Weak MnWO4 [010] March-Dollase texture, 1.0396(37), was retained after [100], [010], [001], and no-texture comparisons across multiple reflection families.

Cells and phase scales were refined. For dominant MnWO4, only the symmetry-allowed Mn and W y coordinates were refined; occupancies, displacement parameters, and oxygen coordinates were held to the screened parent model. Unrestrained oxygen refinement lowered Rwp but produced an implausibly short W–O contact and was rejected, including after soft-restraint challenges. WO3 and Mn3O4 coordinates were held to their literature models. Consequently, the CIFs are refined average structural models rather than independent full structure determinations from this pattern.

The submitted project was saved, reopened, and replayed with native structure factors over the full range. It converged with maximum shift/standard uncertainty = 0.203, no frozen variables, no absolute parameter correlations at or above 0.90, and no active parameter at a bound. The exported CIF cells and profile agree with that reopened project. The largest positive residual maxima coincide with modeled MnWO4 or WO3 reflections; displaced companions around peaks at about 18, 23.8, and 29.6–30.0 degrees are profile/asymmetry lobes rather than a coherent independent peak family.

## Remaining uncertainty

Pointwise uncertainties were not supplied, and the goodness of fit is well above one relative to the counting-statistical residual floor (Rwp,min = 2.490%). The formal fraction uncertainties therefore understate profile, texture, source-model, and absorption/roughness systematics. The Mn3O4 fraction is clearly nonzero, but its cell/profile overlap changes it by roughly one weight percent across physically valid staged models; it should be quoted as about 5 wt%, not as a sub-percent-precision assay. The dominant MnWO4 and WO3 fractions are more stable, but remain crystalline-normalized estimates.

The average wolframite and hausmannite assignments are well supported. The data do not reliably determine oxygen coordinates, occupancies, nonstoichiometry, or subtle symmetry lowering of the average WO3 model; weak triclinic distortion and additional phases below the practical detection limit cannot be excluded. No coherent residual family justifies another crystalline phase in the submitted model.
