Fractions are normalized over the three modeled crystalline phases. The final full-range GSAS-II replay gave Rwp = 6.732%, Rp = 4.579%, Rexp = 2.485%, and goodness of fit = 2.714; its last maximum shift/esd was 0.004.

## Evidence and model selection

The 500 °C WO3 + Pb3O4 context supports PbWO4 formation with residual precursors. Broad scheelite reflections occur at about 27.80, 30.02, 33.15, 45.11, 51.57, and 55.61° 2theta. The sharp 23.53/24.02/24.84° triplet and its higher-angle families identify WO3, while the 14.66, 26.81, 31.19, 32.55, and related reflections require Pb3O4. After all three phases were reconverged, the main residual extrema were at modeled reflection families rather than forming a coherent fourth-phase pattern. A PbO trial refined to effectively zero scale.

Candidates were screened before fitting for composition, occupancy/disorder, source conditions, symmetry, and geometry. The submitted models are ordered synthetic or neutron structures and remain geometrically sound after cell refinement: shortest W-O distances are 1.777 Å in PbWO4 and 1.716 Å in WO3; shortest Pb-O in Pb3O4 is 2.131 Å. A nominally good monoclinic WO3 record (COD 1528915) was rejected for a 1.58 Å O-O contact. Under a common refinement schedule, the physical monoclinic model outperformed physical triclinic and orthorhombic alternatives (trial Rwp about 6.74%, 7.75%, and 8.34%, respectively). Scheelite also outperformed defect-scheelite, an older coarse scheelite record, and raspite; the defect model's diagnostic superstructure reflections were not supported.

## Refinement and uncertainty

The raw counts were weighted with sigma = sqrt(counts). The Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths were fixed at 1.540598/1.544426 Å with the reported 0.5 ratio. A single zero correction (+0.45345°) was used because the offset was angle-independent; specimen displacement was fixed at zero. The accepted common profile has nonnegative W and X terms plus axial asymmetry. Isotropic size broadening was retained only for broad PbWO4 and Pb3O4 peaks; strain, preferred orientation, transparency, and roughness were pruned. Polarization and roughness trials improved Rwp only by driving parameters to bounds or near-perfect correlation, and a common refined ADP likewise sought an unphysical lower bound. Atomic coordinates were therefore held to the screened source models and all candidates used the same fixed Uiso = 0.01 Å2 convention.

The parenthesized fraction errors are counting-statistical GSAS-II esds, not total uncertainties. Fixed-ADP and rejected intensity-nuisance sensitivity tests indicate roughly 1 wt% absolute model uncertainty, larger than those formal esds; coherent angle-dependent intensity residuals remain, especially for strong PbWO4 peaks. Phase presence and prototype/space-group assignments are reliable, but independent atomic coordinates and ADPs cannot be determined reliably from this powder pattern. Absolute phase fractions including amorphous material also cannot be determined without an internal standard, so only normalized crystalline fractions are reported.
