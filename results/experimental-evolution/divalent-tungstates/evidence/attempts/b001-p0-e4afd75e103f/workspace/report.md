# Phase analysis and quantitative refinement

The pattern is modeled by three crystalline phases: wolframite ZnWO4, monoclinic gamma-WO3, and wurtzite ZnO. This is chemically consistent with partial reaction of the ZnO and WO3 precursors at 600 °C. No additional coherent set of unassigned reflections remains at a level that supports a fourth crystalline phase.

| ZnO (wurtzite) | P63mc | a = 3.25100, c = 5.20385 | 9.80 wt% |

The fractions sum to 100% over the modeled crystalline phases. Their covariance-derived standard uncertainties are 0.18, 0.11, and 0.15 wt%, respectively, but these are counting-statistical/model-internal values and understate the total uncertainty.

## Evidence

ZnWO4 accounts for the characteristic reflections near 18.92, 23.80, 30.45/30.69, 36.71, 38.30, and 53.58/54.13 degrees 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the diagnostic low-angle triplet at 23.17, 23.62, and 24.40 degrees and lines near 26.61, 28.63, 33.30, 34.19, and 55.99 degrees. Residual wurtzite ZnO is independently identified by its 31.79, 34.46, 36.30, 47.57, 56.60, 62.87, and 67.96 degree reflections. These peak groups and the refined cells distinguish the selected polymorphs from the alternative WO3 and ZnO structures in the local database.

The final GSAS-II Rietveld fit used the supplied Cu K-alpha1/K-alpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, Poisson count weights, a smooth 18-term Chebyshev background, refined phase scales and cells, a common empirical U-W-X profile, and one isotropic size-broadening term for ZnWO4 (about 0.113 micrometre). The final statistics over 9.9034-100.0946 degrees are Rwp = 3.537%, Rp = 2.715%, Rexp = 2.765%, and goodness of fit = 1.282.

## Remaining uncertainty

The phase assignments are reliable, but the fractions have profile-model systematic uncertainty because no calibrated instrumental profile or internal standard was supplied. Omitting the clearly supported ZnWO4-specific broadening worsens Rwp from about 3.54% to 3.88% and shifts the fractions by approximately 3.4, 2.5, and 1.0 absolute percentage points for ZnWO4, WO3, and ZnO. Those shifts are a more realistic indication of fraction sensitivity than the formal standard uncertainties alone.

Atomic coordinates, occupancies, and displacement parameters were retained from the reference structures (COD 1520641, 1528915, and 9004178); this multiphase laboratory pattern does not determine independent atomic-coordinate refinements reliably. The CIFs therefore contain refined cells with reference atomic models. No particle-size-dependent microabsorption correction was possible; the large Cu K-alpha absorption contrast between W-bearing phases and ZnO is an additional possible fraction bias. An amorphous fraction, if present, cannot be quantified without an internal standard, so the reported fractions must not be interpreted as absolute whole-sample fractions.
