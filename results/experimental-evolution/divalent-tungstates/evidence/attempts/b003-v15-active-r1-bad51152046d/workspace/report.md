# Crystalline phase analysis

The pattern is modeled as three crystalline phases. Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Refined cell | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| Cu3WO6, cubic Pa-3 | a = 9.77828 Å | 34.47 wt% |
| WO3, monoclinic P21/n | a = 7.29453, b = 7.53784, c = 7.68265 Å; beta = 90.9142° | 56.11 wt% |
| CuO (tenorite), C2/c | a = 4.66442, b = 3.42719, c = 5.12977 Å; beta = 99.4772° | 9.42 wt% |

The phase assignments are supported by coherent peak families rather than isolated matches. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the diagnostic 23.215/23.671/24.464° triplet and its higher-angle split and 00l families. Cubic Cu3WO6 accounts for independent peaks near 15.760, 20.368, 33.081, 34.309, 36.809, 58.194, 63.877, and 70.799°. Weaker CuO families are present near 35.6, 38.8–39.2, 48.9, and 53.5° and remain positive under source/profile replays. This inventory is chemically consistent with Cu3WO6 formation from CuO and WO3 at 550 °C plus residual precursors.

The starting structures were COD 1527404 (Cu3WO6), COD 1528915 (room-temperature monoclinic WO3), and COD 1011194 (tenorite). The room-temperature WO3 model was preferred over neutron-monoclinic, triclinic, orthorhombic, and tetragonal alternatives under the same baseline. A strong, multi-family WO3 [001] March-Dollase texture term was required (final ratio 0.719); axial and diagonal alternatives were substantially worse. A weaker CuO [101] texture term was retained only after it improved several independent CuO families. Both tested CuWO4 records were rejected: the best trace trial gave only 0.18(8) wt%, a 0.0015 percentage-point Rwp gain, and no coherent improvement of its strongest families.

The final baseline uses a smooth six-term background, one zero correction (+0.05679°), and a nonnegative common U/W/X profile. Unused size, strain, roughness, asymmetry, and polarization terms were neutralized. Supported complementary terms were retained for Cu3WO6 size (0.237 µm) and CuO isotropic microstrain (5720 microstrain). Representative observed/calculated FWHM values are 0.195/0.189° at 15.76°, 0.283/0.282° at 43.45°, and 0.550/0.570° at 70.80° for Cu3WO6; and 0.178/0.175°, 0.247/0.257°, and 0.925/0.928° for WO3 low-, mid-, and high-angle families. Representative peak positions agree within about one 0.01087° observation step, and the K-alpha doublet asymmetry is reproduced without refining SH/L.

The mandated staged heavy-atom challenge accepted both WO3 W sites and the Cu3WO6 Cu and W sites. All accepted moves were at most 0.056 Å, survived reopen/replay, introduced no frozen variables, and passed contact checks. CuO Cu refinement was rejected as unsupported. Oxygen coordinates remain fixed at their literature values, so the CIFs are conservative Rietveld models rather than complete independent structure solutions.

Final statistics from `final.gpx` on all 8301 unrounded observations are Rwp = 4.4708%, Rp = 3.0781%, Rexp = 2.4095%, and goodness-of-fit = 1.8594; maximum shift/esd is 0.0217 and no variables are frozen.

The covariance-only fraction uncertainties are 0.12, 0.13, and 0.17 wt% for Cu3WO6, WO3, and CuO, respectively, but they are optimistic because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied and texture/source/profile choices are systematic. Model replays indicate changes of order 1 wt% are more realistic, with the CuO fraction least robust. Absolute phase fractions including amorphous material cannot be determined without an internal standard, and a trace CuWO4 contribution below reliable quantification cannot be excluded.
