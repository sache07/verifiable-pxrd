# Quantitative phase analysis

## Result

Four crystalline phases are required by the pattern. Weight fractions below are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases; they do not measure any amorphous content.

| Phase | Space group | Weight fraction (wt%) | Formal esd (wt%) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BaWO4 | I 41/a | 62.04 | 0.26 |
| Ba3W2O9 | R -3 c | 17.86 | 0.20 |
| WO3 | P 21/c | 12.54 | 0.23 |
| BaCO3 | P m c n | 7.57 | 0.23 |

The final fit gives Rp = 3.784%, Rwp = 5.450%, Rexp = 3.017%, and goodness of fit = 1.810.

## Evidence

Scheelite BaWO4 accounts for the dominant reflections, including those near 26.69, 32.04, 43.19, 48.98, 53.68, 69.16, and 82.62 degrees 2theta. Ba3W2O9 is required for peaks near 26.28, 30.82, 37.66, 40.80, and 51.84 degrees. The characteristic split WO3 groups at approximately 23.40/23.88/24.65 and 33.56/33.83/34.45 degrees identify distorted tungsten trioxide. Residual peaks near 24.17, 34.84, 42.26, and 45.18 degrees require witherite BaCO3. This assemblage is chemically consistent with partial reaction of the stated WO3 and BaCO3 precursors at 750 degrees C, leaving both precursors alongside Ba-W-O products.

The four reference structures were refined together in GSAS-II using the supplied Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and intensity ratio. Starting models were COD 1527204 (BaWO4), 1533878 (Ba3W2O9), 2311041 (WO3), and 9010928 (BaCO3); the reported Pmcn carbonate setting is equivalent to standard Pnma (No. 62). Because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied, Poisson counting weights (1/count) were used. Phase scales and unit cells, a 12-term Chebyshev background, the pattern zero offset, selected crystallite-size terms, and a [112] March-Dollase correction for the dominant BaWO4 phase were refined. Atomic coordinates were retained from the reference structures because this powder pattern does not support reliable coordinate refinement for four overlapping phases. The profile started from a fixed, physically plausible instrumental-width floor because no instrumental standard or resolution function was supplied.

## Uncertainty and limitations

The tabulated esds are least-squares values only. Profile-width and texture sensitivity tests indicate a more realistic systematic uncertainty of roughly 1–2 wt% absolute for each phase, with the carbonate fraction especially correlated with peak width. The phase fractions are therefore best reported as about 62.0% BaWO4, 17.9% Ba3W2O9, 12.5% WO3, and 7.6% BaCO3.

The exact low-symmetry description of WO3 is not uniquely determined. A triclinic literature model gives an essentially indistinguishable fit (Rwp lower by only about 0.005 percentage point). The submitted monoclinic gamma-WO3 model is preferred because it is the expected ambient polymorph and avoids unsupported symmetry lowering, but its symmetry should be regarded as provisional. No reliable amorphous fraction can be obtained without an internal standard.
