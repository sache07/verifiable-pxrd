# Powder diffraction phase analysis

## Result

The modeled crystalline material contains **Cu3WO6 (Pa-3)**, **WO3 (P2_1/c)**, and **CuO (C2/c)**. Weight fractions normalized over those crystalline phases are:

| Phase | Weight fraction | Formal 1-sigma | Refined cell |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Cu3WO6 | 36.234% | 0.074% | a = 9.77736 A |
| WO3 | 55.382% | 0.082% | a = 7.68294, b = 7.53767, c = 10.50948 A; beta = 136.0515 deg |
| CuO | 8.384% | 0.099% | a = 4.66690, b = 3.42905, c = 5.11898 A; beta = 99.5021 deg |

The final full-range fit has Rwp = 2.9595%, Rp = 2.3297%, Rexp = 2.4095%, and goodness of fit = 1.2301 (8301 points, 25 variables). The last maximum shift/e.s.d. was 0.002737.

## Evidence and model selection

Cu3WO6 is supported by coherent Pa-3 families at approximately 15.76, 20.37, 32.94/33.08, 34.31, 36.81, 50.44, 58.19, and 63.88 degrees 2theta. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the diagnostic 23.21/23.65/24.46 degree triplet and repeated families near 26.66, 28.71/29.02, 33.34/33.65, 47.36, 53.59, 62.37, and 76.35 degrees. CuO is required by its 35.68 and 38.81 degree pair plus corroborating higher-angle reflections near 48.99, 53.59, 58.19, and 61.98 degrees. Adding CuO lowered Rwp from 5.17% to 3.83% before phase-width refinement.

This assemblage is chemically consistent with reaction of the reported CuO and WO3 precursors at 550 degrees C to form Cu3WO6 while leaving residual precursor oxides.

Exact-composition records were screened for occupancy, symmetry, source conditions, and geometry. The selected structures are COD 1527404 (Cu3WO6), room-temperature neutron-derived COD 2311041 (WO3), and COD 1011194 (tenorite CuO), all fully occupied. Refined-cell geometry remains plausible: W-O distances are 1.79-2.08 A in Cu3WO6 and 1.71-2.16 A in WO3; Cu3WO6 has Cu-O distances of 1.92-2.24 A, while CuO retains four short Cu-O bonds near 1.95 A and two longer contacts near 2.79 A. The room-temperature monoclinic and low-temperature triclinic WO3 records fit very similarly; the room-temperature monoclinic record was selected on source conditions and marginal fit advantage. The high-pressure WO3 polymorph was strongly rejected. C2/c CuO was slightly better than the Cc alternate and is the standard tenorite symmetry.

After the three-phase common profile was rebuilt, CuWO4 (P-1 and P2/c), Cu2WO4, and Cu2O challenges had scales below 1.5 sigma and changed Rwp by less than 0.0005 percentage point; they were not retained. No coherent unmodeled peak family remains.

## Refinement choices and uncertainty

The baseline uses the supplied Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and fixed 0.5 ratio, a six-coefficient smooth Chebyshev background, one zero correction (0.05738 degrees), and a nonnegative common U/X profile (U = 48.462, X = 13.054; V, W, and Y neutral). Roughness, transparency, extinction, and unused texture/size/strain terms are neutral. Common SH/L refinement collapsed to its lower bound and was pruned. WO3 requires March-Dollase orientation along (1, 0, -1), with parameter 0.71804(37). Replay-stable phase broadening consists of Cu3WO6 coherent size 0.1700(25) micrometre and CuO strain 5833(283) microstrain. WO3 width trials and the complementary Cu3WO6 strain trial returned to neutral values and were removed.

The tabulated fraction e.s.d.s are covariance-only and do not include structural-model, preferred-orientation, absorption, or sampling systematics; a practical uncertainty of roughly 1-3 wt% is more realistic. Fractions exclude any amorphous material. Phase identities and cells are reliable, but this laboratory pattern cannot uniquely resolve the subtle monoclinic-versus-triclinic WO3 distortion. Atomic coordinates and displacement parameters were retained from the source structures because independent refinements produced bound or poorly determined values; the exported CIFs should therefore be interpreted as phase-identification/QPA models rather than new high-precision structure determinations.
