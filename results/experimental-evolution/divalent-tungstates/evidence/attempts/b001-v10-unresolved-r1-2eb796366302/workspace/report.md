# PXRD phase analysis

The submitted model contains three crystalline phases. Weight fractions are normalized over those modeled crystalline phases; no amorphous fraction or absolute crystallinity was determined.

| Phase | Structural model | Weight fraction |
|---|---|---:|
| CaWO4 | scheelite, I 41/a | 0.797660 (formal e.s.d. 0.000579) |
| WO3 | monoclinic, P 21/c | 0.110594 (formal e.s.d. 0.000360) |
| Ca3WO6 | ordered monoclinic analogue, P 21/c | 0.091746 (formal e.s.d. 0.000485) |

The full 9.9034–100.0946 degree 2theta range was replayed after reopening `final.gpx`. The final statistics are Rwp = 3.7282%, Rp = 2.9193%, Rexp = 2.2469%, and goodness of fit = 1.6619 for 8301 points and 26 refined parameters. Because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied, counting-statistical weights based on sqrt(counts) were used.

## Evidence and model selection

The dominant line positions and systematic absences identify scheelite CaWO4. COD 7219229 passed the occupancy/contact audit and was retained; a health-passing monoclinic CaWO4 record failed as a replacement (Rwp about 43% in the matched neutral-profile test) and refined to only about 0.11 wt% with a strongly displaced cell when added alongside scheelite. It therefore did not meet the same-composition coexistence gate.

The residual groups near 16.3–16.7, 20.2–20.4, 22.94/23.40/24.21 degrees and many higher-angle families identify WO3. Health-passing monoclinic COD 2311041 and a triclinic record were compared with the same nuisance model; their Rwp values were 8.4552% and 8.4581%, respectively, before adding Ca3WO6. The higher-symmetry P 21/c model was retained because it was marginally better and the extra triclinic splitting had no independent profile support. This is a genuine but small WO3 polymorph ambiguity.

After fitting CaWO4 and WO3, coherent unmatched families remained around 18.71–19.30, 21.96–22.02, 30.71–32.13, 36.59–37.55, 45.01–45.14, 50.5–50.9, 55.2, and 57.05 degrees. No precursor amounts were reported, so a fixed material-balance constraint could not be applied. Charge-balanced non-1:1 Ca–W–O formulas were searched. An ordered Ca3WO6 model was made by the isovalent Os6+ to W6+ substitution in health-passing Ca3OsO6 (COD 1562666), then health-screened before fitting. Its fixed-cell scale was positive (about 7.8 wt%), cell release was stable, and it reduced Rwp from 8.455% to 3.728% while accounting for several independent, non-coincident families. A Ca3UO6-derived competitor collapsed to zero scale. The submitted Ca3WO6 topology is therefore well supported by the diffraction pattern, but its atomic coordinates remain an analogue model rather than an independently solved structure.

Profile decisions were based on isolated low-, middle-, and high-angle peak positions and half-widths. The retained terms are one sample shift, a nonnegative common W contribution, CaWO4 [001] uniaxial size broadening, and a single low-order CaWO4 [110] March-Dollase term; U, V, X, Y, Z, strain, and broadening for the minor phases were restored to neutral values. Polarization and surface-roughness removal each caused a replay-stable loss of fit, so both were retained despite their scale correlation. Ca3WO6 size and strain trials improved the global residual but overbroadened isolated Ca3WO6 lines (especially near 36.61 degrees), so they were rejected as intensity/overlap surrogates.

Trace CaO and an isovalently constructed Ca2WO5 analogue each refined to a small positive value (about 0.32 and 0.24 wt%, respectively), but their apparent gains came from overlapping peaks and neither supplied two independent non-coincident residual families. They remain watch-list possibilities and are not included. All three exported CIFs pass the final occupancy, disorder, and short-contact health audit.

## Remaining uncertainty

The covariance e.s.d.s in the table describe only the selected model and are smaller than the realistic uncertainty. The Ca3WO6 broadening trials moved its fraction from about 9.2 to 10.1 wt%, and polarization/roughness are correlated with scale; approximately 1 absolute wt% is a more realistic model-dependent uncertainty for Ca3WO6, with compensating changes mainly in CaWO4. The chosen WO3 symmetry and the detailed Ca3WO6 coordinates are less certain than phase presence. Structured residuals remain around several intense/overlapped CaWO4 and high-angle families, but the rejected candidates did not meet the required independent-family and replay-stability tests, so no additional crystalline phase is claimed.
