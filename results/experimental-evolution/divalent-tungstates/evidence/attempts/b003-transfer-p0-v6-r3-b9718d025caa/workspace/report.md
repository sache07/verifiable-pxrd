# Phase analysis and refinement

The pattern is explained by three crystalline phases: wolframite-type NiWO4, monoclinic WO3, and rocksalt NiO. This is chemically consistent with reaction of the reported WO3 and NiO precursors at 700 °C, with both precursors remaining in the measured product.

|---|---|---|---:|
| NiWO4 | P 2/c | a = 4.59929, b = 5.66260, c = 4.91116, β = 90.5021 | 60.059 ± 0.031% |

## Evidence

NiWO4 accounts for the characteristic lines near 19.28°, the split group at 30.85–31.04°, and lines near 36.58° and 52.34° 2θ. Monoclinic WO3 gives the diagnostic 23.14/23.56/24.37° triplet and the 33.28/34.16° pair. NiO is required by its complete rocksalt sequence near 37.34°, 43.42°, 63.08°, 75.54°, and 79.68°. After joint refinement, no coherent unassigned peak sequence remains.

The full 9.903–100.095° range was refined with the reported Cu Kα1/Kα2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio. Lattice parameters, phase scales, NiWO4 and WO3 coordinates, background, zero offset, effective polarization, and a common peak profile were refined. The final statistics are Rp = 1.350%, Rwp = 1.788%, Rexp = 1.448%, and goodness of fit = 1.240.

## Remaining uncertainty

The tabulated fraction uncertainties are formal GSAS-II standard uncertainties using σ(I) = √I because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied. Systematic uncertainty from the uncalibrated instrumental profile, background, absorption, and possible preferred orientation is larger than those formal values. Fractions are normalized only over the modeled crystalline phases: amorphous content cannot be measured without an internal standard, and a trace phase hidden under stronger peaks cannot be excluded.

Phase identities, space groups, cells, and the three-phase fractions are well determined by the pattern. The low-symmetry oxygen coordinates, especially in WO3, are less secure than the cells because they are refined from an overlapping multiphase powder pattern; displacement parameters could not be determined reliably and were held fixed in the CIFs.
