# Phase analysis and refinement report

## Result

The crystalline pattern is explained by three phases. Fractions are normalized over these modeled crystalline phases only.

| Phase | Structure / space group | Weight fraction | Formal covariance esd |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| ZnWO4 | wolframite, P 2/c (No. 13) | 72.662 wt% | 0.129 wt% |
| ZnO | wurtzite, P 63 m c (No. 186) | 8.584 wt% | 0.109 wt% |
| WO3 | monoclinic, P 21/c (No. 14) | 18.754 wt% | 0.101 wt% |

The final full-range Rietveld fit has Rwp = 3.0419%, Rp = 2.3930%, Rexp = 2.7646%, and GOF = 1.1020 for 8301 points and 25 refined variables.

## Evidence and structural inventory

The result is chemically consistent with the reported 600 °C reaction of ZnO and WO3: ZnWO4 is the principal product and both precursors remain. ZnWO4 is identified by the 18.9°, 24.4°, split 30.4/30.7°, 31.2°, 38.3°, 48.7°, and related families. Wurtzite ZnO contributes its characteristic 31.8°, 34.5°, 36.3°, 47.6°, 56.6°, and 62.9° families. Monoclinic WO3 accounts for the 23.2/23.6/24.4° group, 26.6°, 28.6/29.0°, and additional 33–34° and 41.9° intensity. These assignments are supported by coherent families across the full angular range, rather than single overlapping peaks.

Starting structures were full-occupancy COD records: 1520641 for ambient-temperature single-crystal ZnWO4, 9004178 for 293 K zincite, and 2311041 for neutron-derived monoclinic WO3. Atomic coordinates, occupancies, and displacement parameters were kept at the source values; phase scales and cells were refined. The exported structures retain the expected stoichiometries and coordination: sixfold Zn–O (2.026–2.225 Å) and W–O (1.797–2.140 Å) in ZnWO4, tetrahedral Zn–O (1.975–1.988 Å) in ZnO, and sixfold W–O (1.715–2.160 Å) in WO3. No partial occupancy or chemically implausible contact was introduced.

The WO3 choice was challenged under a common nuisance model. The selected monoclinic record gave Rwp = 3.302% at that stage; another monoclinic record gave 3.539%, triclinic WO3 gave 3.310% with two extra cell degrees of freedom, orthorhombic WO3 gave 5.172%, and tetragonal WO3 gave 6.525%. Thus the powder data favor the selected monoclinic model, although they do not resolve every subtle monoclinic-versus-triclinic distortion independently.

Additional-phase challenges did not justify a fourth phase. Forced orthorhombic WO3 refined to 0.21 wt% with only a 0.001 percentage-point Rwp change and no coherent unique family; zincblende ZnO refined to 0.046 wt% with a negligible change. Tetragonal WO3 and rocksalt ZnO collapsed to zero scale, and a high-pressure WO3 structure was chemically/source-condition inappropriate. Orthorhombic WO3 at roughly the 0.2 wt% level therefore remains a watch-list possibility, not a reliably determined phase.

## Refinement model and adequacy

The fit used the complete 9.903–100.095° range, the reported Cu Kalpha1/Kalpha2 wavelengths and 0.5 intensity ratio, a smooth six-term Chebyshev-1 background, all phase scales, cells, and one angular correction (zero = 0.02980°). With no calibrated instrumental profile supplied, isolated low-, middle-, and high-angle ZnO families were used to establish a nonnegative common profile: U = V = Y = 0, W = 7.811, X = 3.8, and fixed SH/L = 0.002. Free U attempted to become negative and was rejected; free asymmetry was strongly correlated with zero and was pruned. No systematic position or same-phase width trend remained that justified more common-profile terms.

ZnWO4 required isotropic coherent-domain broadening (0.0873 micrometre). ZnO size and strain trials returned to neutral values. WO3 was better represented by uniaxial microstrain along [0 1 1] than by isotropic size or strain; the residual reduction occurs in several angular regions and was replay-stable. Symmetry-distinct March-Dollase trials produced near-unity coefficients and small, non-unique improvements, so no texture term was retained. Unused size/strain, extinction, polarization refinement, and other nuisance terms are neutral or fixed in the final project.

Point weights are Poisson estimates (1/count) because pointwise uncertainties were not supplied. The final replay started from an already stationary solution: GSAS-II rejected numerically uphill trial steps, while the maximum shift/esd was 0.000 and the saved/reopened profile, fractions, and statistics agreed exactly.

## Remaining uncertainty

The tabulated esds are formal covariance values and are smaller than realistic model uncertainty. Alternate structure/profile trials indicate a practical uncertainty of approximately 0.5–1 wt% per phase. Fractions do not include amorphous material or absolute crystalline content, neither of which can be determined from this dataset without an internal standard. Atomic positions were not refined from this powder pattern, and the exact low-symmetry distortion of WO3 remains the main structural ambiguity.
